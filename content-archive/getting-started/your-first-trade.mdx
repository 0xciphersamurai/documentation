# Your First Trade

This is it. The moment where you stop reading and start participating. Where theory becomes practice. Where you put your insights on the line.

**Let's make your first trade count.**

## Finding Your Edge

Before clicking buttons, understand something crucial: consensus markets reward a different kind of intelligence than traditional trading.

You're not predicting future events. You're evaluating where *collective agreement* will form. That requires understanding people, communities, social dynamics.

**The best traders here might not come from Wall Street.** They might come from community management, social media, cultural trend-spotting. They understand humans, not just charts.

Your edge comes from three sources:

**Domain Knowledge** - You understand a specific community deeply. You know what they value, what they debate, where opinion leaders sit. This knowledge tells you which perspectives will gain traction.

**Pattern Recognition** - You've watched debates unfold before. You recognize the patterns of consensus formation - how early extremism gives way to nuanced middle ground, how fringe ideas sometimes capture mainstream attention.

**Information Asymmetry** - You have access to signals others don't. You're in the Discord. You read the forums. You know what's coming before prices reflect it.

## Choosing Your First Market

Don't overthink this. Pick something you genuinely care about and understand.

**Good first markets:**

Markets on topics where you have real knowledge. Technical debates in your field. Governance questions for communities you participate in. Cultural questions you've thought deeply about.

**Avoid for now:**

Markets on topics you don't understand. Markets moving purely on hype. Markets with very low liquidity. Markets you're entering just because prices are moving.

The goal isn't to make maximum money on trade one. It's to learn the mechanics while having genuine insight into what you're trading.

## Reading the Room

You've chosen a market. Now analyze it like you're analyzing a conversation at a party. Who's saying what? What's gaining momentum? What's losing steam?

**Look at current prices.** They represent where consensus sits *right now*. High prices mean broad current support. Low prices mean the community is skeptical - or hasn't discovered it yet.

**Check price trends.** Is an opinion gaining or losing support over time? Steady climbs suggest genuine consensus formation. Volatile swings suggest uncertainty, opportunity, risk.

**Read the opinions carefully.** Not just what they say, but how they say it. Strong, specific arguments? Vague generalities? Does the framing resonate with the target community?

**Consider the market's age.** Brand new markets are wide open - consensus hasn't formed. Older markets nearing settlement have less room to move but more clarity.

## The Four Trade Types

You have four ways to take a position. Each expresses a different conviction.

| Trade Type | What It Means | When To Use |
|------------|---------------|-------------|
| **Buy Low** | This opinion is undervalued and will gain consensus | You believe community will shift toward this view |
| **Buy High** | The consensus leader will dominate even more | You think the winner is obvious and will settle higher still |
| **Sell High** | This opinion is overvalued and will lose support | You see hype that will fade, consensus shifting away |
| **Sell Low** | Even this low price is too high for this opinion | You believe this perspective will be thoroughly rejected |

For your first trade, buying is simpler than selling. Pick an opinion you believe will gain support.

## Making the Trade

The interface shows you an opinion and its current price. You're about to take a position.

**Enter your amount.** Start modest - maybe 1-5% of your trading capital. This is trade one. You're learning. Losses are tuition.

**Watch the price impact.** Your trade will move the price. Small trades in liquid markets barely register. Larger trades or thin markets show significant impact. This is normal - you're providing information to the market.

**Review the fees.** There's a small fee for trading - it funds market creators and opinion proposers. Factor this into your profit calculation.

**Confirm and execute.** Click through. Sign the transaction. Wait for confirmation.

**You're in.** You now have skin in the game. Your position represents your conviction about where consensus will form.

## What Happens Next?

The market continues trading. Other participants see your trade's price impact. They react. Maybe they agree and buy more, pushing prices higher. Maybe they disagree and sell, pushing prices down.

This continuous exchange of information through prices is price discovery. It's how markets aggregate distributed knowledge into a single signal.

You're now part of that process.

**Your position will fluctuate.** Prices move as new information arrives and traders update their beliefs. Small movements are normal. Watch them, but don't panic.

**Consensus forms gradually.** This isn't day trading. Most positions take days or weeks to play out. Patience is often rewarded. Panic is often punished.

**You can exit anytime.** If you change your mind, new information arrives, or you hit your profit target - trade out. You're never locked in.

## The Psychology Game

Here's what will happen: moments after you trade, you'll second-guess yourself.

Prices will move against you. You'll wonder if you made a mistake. Or prices will move with you, and you'll wonder if you should take profits or hold for more.

**Welcome to trading.** This psychological roller coaster is normal. The successful traders manage it. Here's how:

**Have a plan before trading.** Know your entry point, your profit target, your loss limit. Decide these with a clear head before you have money on the line.

**Size positions appropriately.** If you're anxious about a position, it's too big. You should be able to lose your first trade's capital without life disruption.

**Give positions time to work.** Consensus doesn't form in hours. If your thesis is sound, small contrary movements don't invalidate it.

**Don't revenge trade.** Lost money on trade one? Don't immediately double down trying to win it back. That's emotion, not strategy.

**Learn from every trade.** Whether you win or lose, what did the market teach you? What patterns did you see? This knowledge is more valuable than any single trade's profit.

## Three Common Scenarios

**Scenario 1: Immediate Profit**

You bought at 30 cents. Within hours, price is at 38 cents. You're up 27%.

The temptation: take profits NOW.

The question: was your thesis just about getting to 38 cents? Or do you believe consensus is forming higher still?

If you hit your target - take profits. If the move validates your thesis but the journey isn't over - hold. Have a plan.

**Scenario 2: Slow Bleed**

You bought at 30 cents. Days pass. Price slowly declines to 25 cents. You're down 17%.

The temptation: panic sell.

The question: has your thesis changed? Did new information invalidate your view? Or is this just market noise?

If your analysis was wrong - admit it and exit. If nothing fundamental changed - hold or even buy more. Distinguish between being wrong and being early.

**Scenario 3: Dead Money**

You bought at 30 cents. Weeks pass. Price is still at 30 cents. Nothing happens.

The temptation: boredom. Exit because nothing's moving.

The reality: consensus takes time. Price stability might even be *good* - it's part of how markets eventually settle. Patience is a position.

## Reading Your First Results

Let's say your first trade settles. The market reached consensus. How did you do?

**If you won:** Great! But understand *why*. Was your analysis correct? Did you spot something others missed? Or did you get lucky?

Winners who don't understand why they won don't stay winners. Extract the lesson. What pattern can you apply to future trades?

**If you lost:** Even better for learning. Where did your analysis fail? What did you miss? What signals did other traders see that you didn't?

The best traders lose plenty of trades. They just make sure they learn from each one. Losses that teach are investments in future edge.

**If you broke even:** You learned the mechanics without tuition. Now you know how the platform works, how prices move, what trading feels like.

Next trade, you'll have experience others don't. That's worth something.

## Building Your Strategy

Don't just trade randomly. Start developing an approach.

**Some traders focus on early markets.** Get in when consensus is wide open, opinions are cheap, maximum upside available. High risk, high reward.

**Some traders wait for signals.** Let markets develop, watch for clear consensus formation, buy the obvious winner late but safely. Lower risk, lower reward.

**Some traders arbitrage sentiment.** They read community discussions and trade before that sentiment hits prices. Fast, information-dependent, high edge when it works.

**Some traders hold long-term.** Buy their conviction, hold through volatility, collect payouts at settlement. Patient, conviction-driven, less stressful.

Try different approaches. See what matches your skills, knowledge, and temperament. Your strategy should leverage your specific advantages.

## The Compounding Effect

Here's what most people miss about markets: the knowledge compounds.

Trade one teaches you platform mechanics. Trade two teaches you how prices respond to information. Trade ten teaches you patterns of consensus formation. Trade fifty gives you intuition others don't have.

**The traders winning six months from now are the ones learning today.**

They're building pattern recognition that can't be rushed. They're making mistakes while stakes are low and markets are thin. They're developing edge while edge is available.

You just made trade one. You're now ahead of everyone who hasn't started yet.
