# Quick Start

You're minutes away from joining something entirely new. Not predicting events. Not betting on outcomes. Participating in debates where your voice has weight. Where consensus forms through passion and conviction.

**Let's get you started.**

## What You Need

The beauty of Trigger Market is its simplicity. You don't need special credentials, complex setups, or institutional access.

All you need:

**A Web3 Wallet** - MetaMask, Coinbase Wallet, Rainbow, or any wallet that supports Base. If you're already in crypto, you're ready. If not, installing MetaMask takes two minutes.

**Some USDC on Base** - Start small. You can back opinions, create debates, and participate meaningfully with modest amounts. Many early users began with just $50-$200 to learn how it works. Bridge to Base if needed - it's cheap and fast.

**Passion** - Opinions you care about. Debates that matter to you. This isn't just another platform. It's where beliefs meet consequences.

## Your First 5 Minutes

**1. Connect Your Wallet**

Navigate to the Trigger Market platform. Click "Connect Wallet" in the top right. Make sure you're on **Base network**. Sign the connection request. That's it - you're in.

No registration forms. No KYC. No waiting. Just connect and you're ready to trade. Gas costs pennies on Base.

**2. Explore Active Debates**

You'll see a list of live debates. Each one is a question, a conversation, consensus forming through prices.

Browse them. Notice the variety:
- Technical debates about frameworks and protocols
- Governance questions about strategic direction
- Cultural discussions about values and priorities
- Community decisions about resource allocation

Find one that interests you. Something you have an opinion about. Something where you think you understand the community better than others do.

**3. Read the Opinions**

Click into a debate. You'll see multiple opinions - different perspectives on the question.

Each opinion has a price. That price represents how much the community currently backs that perspective.

Read through them. See which ones resonate. Notice what's getting support and what's not. Think about where you stand.

**4. Make Your Voice Heard**

Ready? You have three options:

**Back an opinion** - Support one you believe in. Your stake moves its price up.

**Propose a new opinion** - Don't see yours? Add it. Explain why it matters.

**Create your own debate** - See a question that deserves a market? Launch it.

Pick what feels right. Start small. Learn as you go.

**Congratulations - you're now part of building consensus.**

## What Just Happened?

You didn't place a bet. You joined a conversation with real stakes.

**If you backed an opinion:** Your support moved its price. Others will see that. They'll react. This back-and-forth is how consensus forms.

**If you proposed an opinion:** You added a new perspective to the debate. Others can stake on it. If it gains enough support, it enters the main debate.

**If you created a debate:** You launched a venue for your community to express beliefs and form consensus. You own that space.

You're now part of something bigger. When debates settle, your participation matters.

## The First Week

You've made your first move. Now what?

**Watch the Debates**

Check back regularly. See how opinions gain or lose support. Notice what causes changes - new arguments, new participants, shifts in sentiment.

You're building intuition. Learning how consensus forms. This knowledge compounds.

**Start Small, Learn Fast**

Don't stake everything on day one. Start with amounts you're comfortable with as you learn.

The goal right now isn't maximum returns. It's understanding how debates evolve. How communities make decisions. How consensus emerges.

**Explore Different Debates**

Don't just join one debate. Explore variety:
- Topics you know deeply vs topics where you're learning
- Technical questions vs governance decisions
- Established debates vs brand new ones

Each type teaches different lessons. Understanding the full landscape helps you participate better.

## What Makes This Different?

If you've used other platforms - social media, forums, DAOs - this will feel familiar but more powerful.

**Your voice has weight.** Backing an opinion literally moves its price. Your conviction is visible. You're not just commenting - you're shaping outcomes.

**It's collaborative content creation.** Debates exist because users create them. Opinions exist because users propose them. You're building the platform by participating.

**Timeframes are natural.** Debates run as long as they need to. Consensus forms at its own pace. No artificial deadlines. No forced conclusions.

**Passion matters.** The people who win here aren't necessarily finance bros. They're community members, domain experts, people who care deeply. Different skills matter here.

## Common Early Questions

**"Do I need to understand finance?"**

Not really. You need to understand people, communities, and arguments. If you know a topic deeply and can read where discussions are headed, that's the edge.

**"What if my opinion loses?"**

You lose what you staked. That's the trade-off for having your voice amplified. But it's limited to what you put in.

**"When do debates end?"**

When natural consensus forms and prices stabilize. Could be weeks, could be months. The debate itself determines when consensus has been reached.

**"Can I change my mind?"**

Anytime. Exit your position, switch to a different opinion, propose a new one, or create your own debate. You're never locked in.
