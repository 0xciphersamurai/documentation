# Opinion Dynamics

Opinions aren't static. They compete, evolve, enter, exit, and sometimes return. Understanding this flow helps you see debates as living ecosystems, not frozen snapshots.

**This is natural selection for ideas.**

## The Competition Model

Think of debates like ecosystems where opinions compete for limited space.

**The constraint:** Only 10 opinions can be in the big league (active debate) at once.

**Why 10?** Human cognition. More than 10 competing options becomes cluttered and confusing. The debate stays focused on real contenders.

**What this creates:** Competition. Opinions must prove they deserve a spot. Weak ones get replaced. Strong ones defend their position through community support.

## Baby League → Big League

### **Starting in Baby League**

Every new opinion (except the initial ones) starts as a candidate:

**What happens:**
- Someone proposes it with a written argument
- They stake some initial support ($10+ typically)
- Others can add their stakes
- Total stake accumulates

**The hurdle:**
To enter big league, the candidate needs roughly 5% more stake than the weakest big league opinion's value.

**Why 5%?** Prevents endless flip-flopping. You need to prove meaningfully more support, not just barely edge past.

### **The Graduation**

When a candidate hits the threshold:

**Anyone can trigger it** - Costs a bit of gas, but enables the transition

**What happens:**
1. Weakest big league opinion automatically gets replaced
2. That opinion exits to baby league with its value preserved
3. Your candidate enters big league with its accumulated stake
4. All stakes convert to tradeable shares
5. Trading begins immediately at a real market price

**Your position:**
- If you staked early on the candidate, you now own shares
- Your stake converted at the ground-floor entry price
- You were early, so your effective price is better than later entrants

## Big League → Baby League

### **Losing Your Spot**

**When it happens:**
A candidate accumulates enough stake to challenge you. Someone triggers the replacement. You're out.

**What happens to your opinion:**
- Gets liquidated at current market value
- That value becomes its new stake in baby league
- Drops back to candidate status
- Can gather more support and return

**What happens to your shares:**
- Automatically represent proportional claim on the new baby league stake
- Your ownership percentage stays the same
- Can unstake anytime or hold for potential comeback

**Example:**
You held 100 of 1000 total shares (10%) in an opinion that got replaced.
- Opinion liquidates for $500
- Becomes candidate with $500 stake
- Your 100 shares now represent 10% of $500 = $50 claim
- You can unstake that $50 or hold and support the comeback

### **Why This Doesn't Feel Bad**

**Your value is preserved** - You don't lose money from the replacement itself. You lose money only if the opinion's price fell before replacement (market decided it was weak).

**Comebacks happen** - Replaced opinions often return. If you believe in it, support the comeback. If not, unstake and exit.

**It's fair competition** - Your opinion was replaced because another one had more support. That's the market working. Weak gets replaced by strong.

## The Cycle

**Here's the beautiful part:** Opinions can cycle through multiple times.

```
Candidate ($200)
  → Gets to $210 (beats $200 threshold)
  → Enters Big League
  → Trades, gains support
  → Price/value reaches $450
  → Newer candidate at $475 challenges
  → Gets replaced
  → Drops to Candidate with $450 stake
  → Believers add more stakes
  → Reaches $525 (beats new threshold)
  → Returns to Big League
  → Cycle continues
```

**Real example from early markets:**
"Approach X" cycled 3 times (Big → Baby → Big → Baby → Big) before finally settling in the top 3. Early believers who held through the cycles made 8x because they backed it every time it was in baby league.

## What Drives the Cycle?

**Opinions enter when:**
- New perspective gains traction
- Community shifts interest to new angle
- External events boost an argument's case
- Strong believers accumulate stakes

**Opinions exit when:**
- Support wanes (people sell)
- Better alternatives emerge
- Arguments prove weak under scrutiny
- Community loses interest

**Opinions return when:**
- Believers didn't give up, kept staking
- New information strengthens their case
- Community revisits after trying alternatives
- Passionate minority keeps pushing

## Strategy Implications

### **If You're Backing Big League Opinions**

**Watch for threats:**
- Check baby league candidates regularly
- See which ones are close to threshold
- Evaluate if your opinion is the weakest (vulnerable)

**Defend or exit:**
- If you believe your opinion will survive challenges, hold
- If you see a stronger challenger coming, consider exiting before replacement
- Or buy more to strengthen your opinion's position

### **If You're Staking Baby League**

**Bet on graduation:**
- You're betting the opinion will enter big league
- If it does, your stake converts to shares at entry price
- Earlier you stake, better your effective price

**Watch the threshold:**
- Track how much more stake is needed
- Promote to get others to stake
- Time your stakes strategically

**Evaluate competition:**
- Which big league opinion is weakest?
- Is your candidate strong enough to replace it?
- Be realistic about chances

### **If Your Opinion Gets Replaced**

**Three choices:**

**1. Hold for comeback**
- Keep your stake in baby league version
- Add more if you still believe
- Support it returning to big league

**2. Unstake and exit**
- Take your proportional share of current stake
- Accept the loss (if any)
- Move on to other debates

**3. Switch allegiance**
- Unstake from your old opinion
- Back the one that replaced it (if you think it's better)
- Or back a different candidate

## The Ecology Metaphor

Think of debates as ecosystems:

**Big league = 10 available niches**
- Top predators
- Most visible
- Most competition
- Most rewards

**Baby league = Unlimited challengers**
- Waiting for their chance
- Growing strength
- Some will make it, most won't
- But all have a shot

**The cycle = Natural selection**
- Fit opinions survive
- Weak opinions get replaced
- But replaced doesn't mean dead - they can adapt and return
- Over time, the strongest opinions dominate

## What Makes Opinions Strong?

**Strong arguments:**
- Clear, specific case
- Evidence and reasoning
- Addresses counterarguments
- Resonates with community values

**Strong backing:**
- Passionate believers
- Network effects (backers promote it)
- Key community figures supporting
- Sustained stake accumulation

**Strong timing:**
- Catches momentum of external events
- Addresses current community concerns
- Proposes solutions to active problems
- Fills obvious gaps in debate

**Strong persistence:**
- Believers don't give up after setbacks
- Comeback attempts after being replaced
- Continuous refinement of arguments
- Long-term commitment

## Reading the Dynamics

**Healthy debate signs:**
- Regular opinion rotation (market self-curating)
- Mix of established and new perspectives
- Active baby league with multiple candidates
- Clear arguments for each position

**Stagnant debate signs:**
- No opinion changes in weeks
- No new candidates emerging
- Same opinions dominating without challenge
- Discussion died down

**Chaotic debate signs:**
- Too many replacements too fast
- No opinion establishes itself
- Baby league candidates constantly changing
- No consensus forming

## The Takeaway

**Opinions aren't permanent fixtures** - They're dynamic participants in an ongoing conversation.

**Competition is the point** - It ensures quality. Weak arguments don't persist. Strong ones prove themselves.

**You shape the dynamics** - Your backing, your proposals, your arguments - all influence which opinions rise and fall.

**The math handles fairness** - Replacements happen based on stake levels. Your value is always preserved proportionally. No one can game it.

<Callout title="Darwin Meets Debates" type="info">
Traditional debates: Everyone talks, nothing changes, arguments persist forever regardless of merit.

Consensus markets: Weak arguments get replaced. Strong ones defend their position. The ecosystem evolves toward the best perspectives.

**This is how debates should work.**
</Callout>
