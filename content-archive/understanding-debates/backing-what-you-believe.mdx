# Backing What You Believe

You have conviction. You believe in a perspective. You think the community will come to agree with you.

**Now make that belief count.**

Backing opinions isn't about trading strategies or profit optimization. It's about putting weight behind what you think is right - and potentially profiting when consensus aligns with your views.

## Start With Conviction

**The foundational question:** Do you actually believe this?

Not "is this likely to pump?" or "can I flip this for quick profit?" But: **Do you think this opinion deserves more support than it currently has?**

**Good reasons to back an opinion:**
- You've thought deeply about the topic and genuinely believe this perspective is correct
- You're part of the community and see sentiment shifting this direction
- New information strengthens this case and others haven't realized yet
- The argument is sound but underappreciated

**Bad reasons to back an opinion:**
- Price is moving and you're chasing momentum
- Someone told you to (without understanding why)
- It's the cheapest one (maybe it's cheap for good reason)
- You want to gamble

**Why conviction matters:**
You'll hold through volatility only if you actually believe. Without conviction, you'll exit at the first price drop. With conviction, you can weather short-term moves and wait for consensus to form.

## When To Back

### **Early Phase Backing**

**The situation:** Debate just launched or opinion just proposed. Chaos. Uncertainty. Low prices.

**Why back now:**
- Cheapest entry point
- Highest potential upside if you're right
- You help establish early market structure

**The catch:**
- Highest uncertainty
- Prices will swing wildly
- Many won't make it
- Requires real conviction

**Your edge:**
Deep domain knowledge. You understand the topic better than the market reflects. Early prices are noise - you see signal.

### **Middle Phase Backing**

**The situation:** Debate active for weeks. Patterns emerging. Some opinions ahead, some behind.

**Why back now:**
- More information available
- Clearer sense of community sentiment
- Still meaningful upside available
- Lower volatility than early phase

**The catch:**
- Less upside than early entry
- Prices partially reflect information already
- You're not as early

**Your edge:**
Reading social dynamics. You see where consensus is forming before it's fully priced in. Not first, but still early enough.

### **Late Phase Backing**

**The situation:** Debate maturing. Clear leaders. Low volatility. Approaching settlement.

**Why back now:**
- High certainty about outcomes
- Small remaining gains, but reliable
- Low risk if you're reading correctly

**The catch:**
- Minimal upside
- Prices mostly reflect consensus already
- Essentially waiting for settlement confirmation

**Your edge:**
Certainty over returns. You're buying confirmed consensus at fair prices, accepting small gains for high confidence.

## How Much To Back

**Not a portfolio allocation strategy - a conviction expression strategy.**

### **Small Stakes ($10-50)**

**When:** Testing the waters, learning, or low conviction

**Means:** "I think this is interesting but not sure yet"

**Good for:**
- First-time participants
- Opinions you're uncertain about
- Learning the mechanics

### **Medium Stakes ($50-500)**

**When:** Moderate conviction, want meaningful exposure

**Means:** "I believe in this and want skin in the game"

**Good for:**
- Topics you know well
- Opinions you've researched
- Balance of risk and reward

### **Large Stakes ($500+)**

**When:** High conviction, deep knowledge, strong belief

**Means:** "I'm confident this is correct and underpriced"

**Good for:**
- Your area of expertise
- When you have information edge
- Opinions you've been following closely

**The principle:** Stake according to conviction, not according to "portfolio optimization." You're expressing belief, not building a diversified fund.

## Big League vs Baby League Backing

### **Backing Big League (Active Opinions)**

**What you get:**
- Immediate price exposure
- Can sell anytime
- Clear market price
- Part of main conversation

**Why do it:**
- Straightforward: you think price is too low
- Liquid: can exit whenever
- Visible: everyone sees and trades these

### **Staking Baby League (Candidates)**

**What you get:**
- Stake in candidate pool
- If it graduates → converts to shares
- If it doesn't → you can unstake

**Why do it:**
- Earlier entry → better effective price if it makes it
- Support perspectives you think deserve to be in main debate
- Help good opinions reach big league

**The decision:**
- Back big league if you want clarity and liquidity
- Stake baby league if you want upside and believe in graduation

## When To Add More

**Your opinion is gaining traction:**
- Arguments winning in comments
- Price rising steadily
- New backers arriving
- Momentum building

**Decision:** Add more if you still have conviction and think there's room to run

**Your opinion is under threat:**
- Baby league candidate challenging it
- Support seems to be waning
- Price declining

**Decision:** Add more to defend it (if you still believe) or prepare to exit

## When To Exit

**Not about "taking profits" - about conviction changes.**

### **Exit Because You Changed Your Mind**

**Good reasons:**
- New information contradicted your thesis
- Better arguments emerged for other opinions
- You learned something that changed your view
- Community is headed different direction than you thought

**This is honorable** - Changing your mind based on new information is rational. Exit and back what you now believe.

### **Exit Because Consensus Formed Elsewhere**

**The situation:**
- Clear that a different opinion will win
- Your opinion losing support
- Settlement approaching with you on wrong side

**This is pragmatic** - Accepting you misread consensus and minimizing loss makes sense.

### **Exit Because You Need The Money**

**The situation:**
- External reasons unrelated to debate
- Need liquidity for something else
- Debate taking longer than expected

**This is practical** - Life happens. Exit and move on.

### **Don't Exit Because:**

**Short-term price drops** - If conviction remains, volatility is noise

**Others are exiting** - Maybe they're wrong and you're right

**Impatience** - Consensus takes time. Don't exit just because it's slow.

## The Psychological Game

### **You Will Second-Guess Yourself**

**After you back:** Prices might drop immediately. You'll wonder if you made a mistake.

**Reality:** Short-term price movement is noise. If you have conviction, hold.

### **You Will Want To Check Constantly**

**Temptation:** Check prices every hour. Watch every small move.

**Better approach:** Check daily or every few days. Consensus forms slowly. Constant checking adds stress without value.

### **You Will See Other Opinions Doing Better**

**Feeling:** "I should have backed that one instead"

**Reality:** Hindsight is easy. You made the best decision with information available. Can't predict everything.

### **You Will Want To Switch**

**Temptation:** Exit your opinion and jump to the winning one.

**Consider:** Why didn't you back it initially? Has new information emerged? Or are you just chasing prices?

**If new info changed your view:** Switch is rational.
**If just chasing:** Probably don't.

## Backing As Participation

**Remember:** You're not just betting. You're participating in forming consensus.

**Your backing:**
- Signals to others: "This perspective has support"
- Moves the price: "This opinion is gaining weight"
- Shapes the conversation: "This view matters"

**You're part of the process** - Not observing from outside, but inside the consensus formation.

When you back an opinion strongly, you're saying: "I think the community should pay more attention to this." If you're right, you profit. But more importantly, you helped consensus form around a better answer.

<Callout title="Conviction + Action = Voice" type="info">
Traditional debates: Strong opinions weakly held. No consequences.

Consensus markets: Strong opinions backed with stake. Real consequences.

**This is how beliefs become visible, measured, and ultimately resolved.**
</Callout>

## Practical Scenarios

**Scenario 1: You're a developer, technical debate on frameworks**

You know deeply which one is better. Others don't. Back it heavily early. Hold through noise. Consensus will form your direction because you're right.

**Scenario 2: You're in a DAO, governance debate**

You're active in discussions, see sentiment shifting. Back the direction you see forming. You're reading social dynamics, not technical merit.

**Scenario 3: You're passionate about a cause, values debate**

You believe deeply in a perspective. Back it because you want it amplified. Whether you profit or not, you made your voice count.

**Scenario 4: You're uncertain but curious**

Small stake on multiple opinions. Learn by participating. Build intuition before committing heavily.
