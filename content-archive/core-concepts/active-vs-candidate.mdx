# Active vs Candidate Opinions

Opinions in Trigger Market exist in different states. Understanding the distinction between active and candidate opinions is crucial for both traders and market creators.

## Opinion States

Every opinion is in one of three states:

### Active Opinions

**Definition**: Opinions currently included in the market's AMM and available for trading.

**Characteristics**:
- ✅ Tradeable right now
- ✅ Has a price (0-1.0 / 0-100%)
- ✅ Satisfies L² norm constraint √(Σp_i²) = 1
- ✅ Earns fees from trades
- ✅ Can settle with final payout

**Typical count**: 3-7 active opinions per market

**Example**:
Market: "Best state management for React?"

**Active opinions**:
- Redux (price: 0.60 / 60%) ← Tradeable
- Zustand (price: 0.55 / 55%) ← Tradeable
- Jotai (price: 0.45 / 45%) ← Tradeable
- Context API (price: 0.40 / 40%) ← Tradeable

### Candidate Opinions

**Definition**: Newly proposed opinions that are not yet tradeable but gathering support.

**Characteristics**:
- ❌ Not tradeable yet
- ❌ No price in the AMM
- ✅ Can gather support via staking
- ✅ Can become active if supported
- ⏳ Waiting to challenge active opinions

**Example**:
**Candidate opinions**:
- Recoil (gathering support)
- MobX (recently proposed)
- Valtio (early stage)

### Inactive Opinions

**Definition**: Opinions that were previously active but have been replaced.

**Characteristics**:
- ❌ No longer tradeable
- ❌ No current price
- ✅ Historical record preserved
- ✅ Former holders compensated
- 📜 Part of market history

**Example**:
**Inactive opinions**:
- React Context (replaced by Context API)
- Easy Peasy (replaced by simpler alternative)

## Active Opinion Details

### What Makes an Opinion Active?

Opinions become active through:

**1. Market Creation**
- Creator selects 3-5 initial opinions
- All start as active
- Market launches with these

**2. Successful Replacement**
- Candidate gathers enough support
- Meets replacement criteria
- Activates and replaces weakest active opinion

### Active Opinion Properties

**Price Discovery**:
Each active opinion has a price determined by the AMM based on:
- Share quantities in the pool
- Overall market liquidity
- Trading activity

**Trading**:
Users can:
- Buy shares (long the opinion)
- Sell shares (short the opinion)
- Hold positions
- Exit positions

**Fee Earnings**:
Opinion creator earns:
- 0.25% of all trades involving this opinion
- Perpetual revenue stream
- Grows with opinion popularity

**Settlement Payout**:
At settlement:
- Final price locks
- Shares pay out that price
- Example: 500 shares at 0.68 (68%) = $340 payout

### Managing Active Opinions

**For traders**:
- Focus analysis on active opinions
- Only active opinions can be traded
- Track price movements
- Consider replacement risk

**For creators**:
- Select strong initial active opinions
- Monitor which are thriving
- Watch for replacement threats
- Earn fees from active opinion trades

## Candidate Opinion Details

### Why Candidates Exist

**Problem**: Markets need to evolve as perspectives change

**Solution**: Candidate system allows:
- New opinions to be proposed
- Community vetting before activation
- Gradual market evolution
- Avoiding stagnation

### Proposing a Candidate

Anyone can propose a new opinion:

<Steps>

### Submit Proposal

Navigate to the market and click "Propose Opinion"

**Required information**:
- Opinion title (short, clear)
- Description (detailed explanation)
- Rationale (why this opinion matters)

### Provide Collateral

Submit collateral (typically 0.01 - 0.1 ETH):
- Shows commitment
- Deters spam
- Returned if activated or can be reclaimed

### Become Candidate

Opinion enters candidate state:
- Visible to all users
- Can gather support
- Not yet tradeable

</Steps>

### The Support Phase

Candidates need support to activate:

**How support works**:
- Users "stake" to support a candidate
- Staking shows belief in the opinion's value
- No trading yet - pure signaling
- Stakes can be withdrawn

**Support threshold**:
- Must reach minimum support level
- Typically expressed in ETH staked
- Higher threshold = higher quality bar

**Why support matters**:
- Filters out weak proposals
- Ensures community buy-in
- Creates accountability
- Reduces spam

**Example**:
- Candidate: "Recoil" for React state management
- Threshold: 0.5 ETH total support
- Current support: 0.32 ETH (64%)
- Supporters: 12 users
- Status: Still gathering support

### Candidate Evaluation

Before supporting a candidate, consider:

**Is it distinct?**
- Meaningfully different from active opinions?
- Not redundant or overlapping?
- Unique perspective?

**Is it relevant?**
- Addresses the market question?
- Timely and applicable?
- Would people trade it?

**Is it defensible?**
- Has rational basis?
- Can be argued for?
- Supported by evidence?

**Is it better?**
- Stronger than weakest active opinion?
- Would improve the market?
- Worth the replacement?

<Callout title="Support Strategically" type="info">
Supporting candidates is like voting for market evolution. Support opinions that:
- Add value to the debate
- Would attract trading volume
- Are better than current weakest active opinion
</Callout>

## The Replacement Mechanism

### When Replacement Occurs

Replacement happens when:

1. **Candidate reaches threshold**: Enough support gathered
2. **Replacement criteria met**: Market conditions allow it
3. **Weakest active identified**: Lowest-priced active opinion targeted
4. **Execution**: Candidate activates, weakest deactivates

### Replacement Process

<Steps>

### Candidate Qualifies

Candidate opinion meets all requirements:
- Sufficient support staked
- Collateral provided
- Waiting period elapsed
- Market not settling soon

### Trigger Replacement

Anyone can trigger the replacement transaction:
- Calls the replacement function
- Contract verifies criteria
- Execution begins

### State Transition

**Outgoing opinion** (weakest active):
- Status: Active → Inactive
- Price: Locked at final price
- Trading: Stops immediately
- Shares: Converted to equivalent value in other opinions

**Incoming opinion** (candidate):
- Status: Candidate → Active
- Price: Initialized (typically low, like replaced opinion)
- Trading: Becomes available
- Shares: Can now be traded

### Market Rebalance

AMM adjusts to new opinion set:
- Liquidity redistributes
- Prices rebalance
- New equilibrium established
- Trading resumes normally

### Compensation

Holders of replaced opinion:
- Don't lose value
- Shares converted to basket of remaining opinions
- Proportional to final prices
- Can trade new positions immediately

</Steps>

### Replacement Example

**Before replacement**:

**Active opinions**:
- PostgreSQL: 0.70 (70%)
- MySQL: 0.55 (55%)
- MongoDB: 0.45 (45%)
- Redis: 0.30 (30%) ← Weakest, will be replaced

**Candidate**:
- SQLite: Has gathered 0.6 ETH support (threshold met)

**Replacement executes**:

**After replacement**:

**Active opinions**:
- PostgreSQL: 0.68 (68%)
- MySQL: 0.53 (53%)
- MongoDB: 0.44 (44%)
- SQLite: 0.35 (35%) ← New active opinion
- Verification: √(0.68² + 0.53² + 0.44² + 0.35²) ≈ 1 ✓

**Inactive**:
- Redis: Removed, holders compensated

**Result**: Market now has SQLite instead of Redis

## Strategic Implications

### For Traders

**Active opinion positions**:
- **Safest**: High-priced active opinions (0.65+ / 65%+)
  - Less replacement risk
  - Established consensus
  - Lower volatility

- **Moderate risk**: Mid-priced (0.40-0.65 / 40-65%)
  - Some replacement risk
  - Active competition
  - Good risk/reward

- **Highest risk**: Low-priced (under 0.40 / under 40%)
  - High replacement risk
  - Could be eliminated
  - Contrarian opportunity

**Candidate speculation**:
- Support candidates you believe will succeed
- Early supporter advantages possible
- Can trade once activated
- Risk: May never activate

### For Opinion Creators

**Creating valuable opinions**:
- Propose as market creator (instant active)
- OR propose as candidate (needs support)
- Earn 0.25% fees if active
- Perpetual revenue stream

**Strategy**:
- Propose distinct, valuable perspectives
- Time proposals well
- Build community support
- Target weak active opinions

### For Market Creators

**Initial opinion selection**:
- Choose strong, distinct opinions
- Cover key perspectives
- Leave room for evolution
- Don't over-populate

**Managing evolution**:
- Monitor candidate proposals
- Community-driven improvement
- Market stays relevant
- Natural selection of ideas

## Advanced Scenarios

### Multiple Candidates

Multiple candidates can compete:

**Scenario**:
- 3 active opinions + 5 candidates
- All 5 candidates gathering support
- Only 1 can replace at a time

**Competition**:
- First to meet threshold replaces
- OR highest support replaces
- Market chooses best new opinion
- Natural selection

### Preventing Replacement

If you hold a low-priced active opinion:

**Defense strategies**:
1. **Buy to increase price**: Move it above replacement range
2. **Exit position**: Sell before replacement
3. **Accept risk**: Hold and see what happens
4. **Lobby against candidates**: Argue they're not better

**Reality**: Replacement may be inevitable if opinion is weak

### Replacement Timing

**Good time to replace**:
- Market still active (not near settlement)
- Weakest opinion clearly inferior
- Strong candidate available
- Community consensus for change

**Bad time to replace**:
- Market approaching settlement
- All active opinions strong
- Weak candidate
- Would disrupt price stability

## Candidate Opinion Opportunities

### Early Support Advantage

Supporting successful candidates early:

**Benefits**:
- Influence market evolution
- Potential fee sharing (if implemented)
- Reputation as market curator
- First mover when activated

**Risks**:
- Candidate may never activate
- Collateral locked up
- Opportunity cost

### New Opinion Trading

When candidate activates:

**Opportunities**:
- Often underpriced initially
- Less known = mispricing possible
- Can buy cheap early
- Profit if consensus builds

**Example**:
- SQLite activates at 0.35 (35%, low)
- You believe it's undervalued
- Buy at 0.38
- Consensus builds → 0.62 (62%)
- **Profit**: 63%

## Key Differences Summary

| Aspect | Active | Candidate | Inactive |
|--------|--------|-----------|----------|
| **Tradeable** | ✅ Yes | ❌ No | ❌ No |
| **Has Price** | ✅ Yes | ❌ No | 📜 Historical |
| **Earns Fees** | ✅ Yes | ❌ No | ❌ No |
| **Can Support** | ❌ No | ✅ Yes | ❌ No |
| **Settlement Payout** | ✅ Yes | ❌ No | ❌ No |
| **Count per Market** | 3-7 | 0-10+ | 0-100+ |

## Key Takeaways

✅ **Active opinions** = currently tradeable, have prices, part of AMM

✅ **Candidate opinions** = proposed but not yet active, gathering support

✅ **Inactive opinions** = previously active, now replaced, historical record

✅ **Replacement mechanism** = candidates can replace weakest active opinions

✅ **Support system** = community vetting before activation

✅ **Traders watch** = replacement risk on low-priced active opinions

✅ **Market evolution** = candidate system keeps markets relevant over time
