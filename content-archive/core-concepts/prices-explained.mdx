# Prices Explained

Prices in Trigger Market are both familiar and unique. They work like prediction market prices (representing consensus probability), but with novel dynamics. This guide explains everything about pricing.

## Price Fundamentals

### What Prices Represent

Opinion prices represent **current collective consensus** about that perspective's validity or quality.

**Interpretation**:
- **0.70 (70%)** = Strong consensus supporting this opinion
- **0.50 (50%)** = Moderate support, competitive with others
- **0.30 (30%)** = Weaker consensus, but still in consideration
- **0.10 (10%)** = Minimal support, losing relevance

<Callout title="Not Probabilities" type="info">
While prices look like probabilities (0-100¢), they don't represent probability of being "correct" (there's no objective answer). Instead, they reflect **strength of consensus** among market participants.
</Callout>

### The L² Norm Constraint

**Most important rule**: All active opinion prices are constrained to lie on a **unit sphere** in price space, satisfying: **√(p₁² + p₂² + ... + pₙ²) = 1**

**Example market** (4 equal opinions):
- Each price: 0.5 (50%)
- Verification: √(0.5² + 0.5² + 0.5² + 0.5²) = √(4 × 0.25) = √1 = 1 ✓

**Example market** (unequal distribution):
- React: 0.60 (60%)
- Vue: 0.55 (55%)
- Svelte: 0.45 (45%)
- Angular: 0.40 (40%)
- Verification: √(0.60² + 0.55² + 0.45² + 0.40²) = √(0.36 + 0.3025 + 0.2025 + 0.16) = √1.025 ≈ 1 ✓

**Why this matters**:
- Buying one opinion implicitly bets against all others
- Prices follow a spherical constraint, not a linear sum
- Creates natural price competition through geometry
- Ensures prices remain bounded without artificial constraints

**Mathematical guarantee**:
The AMM enforces this through the L² norm formula: p_i = q_i / ||q||₂ where ||q||₂ = √(q₁² + q₂² + ... + qₙ²). It's mathematically impossible for prices to leave the unit sphere.

### Price Bounds

Prices are constrained by the L² norm:
- **Minimum**: 0 (complete rejection)
- **Maximum**: 1.0 (100%, total consensus)
- **Typical range**: 0.15 - 0.70 (15% - 70%)

**Edge cases**:
- **0.95+**: Near-unanimous consensus, little debate
- **0.05-**: Near-total rejection, losing relevance
- **0.707 each (2 opinions)**: Perfect split (√0.5 ≈ 0.707)
- **0.5 each (4 opinions)**: Equal consensus (√0.25 = 0.5)

## How Prices Form

### Automated Market Maker (AMM)

Prices are set by an automated market maker, not an order book.

**Key difference from exchanges**:
- **Exchanges**: Buyers and sellers match orders, price = last trade
- **AMM**: Algorithm quotes prices based on pool state

**Advantages**:
- Always liquid (can always trade)
- Deterministic pricing
- No bid-ask spread manipulation
- Self-arbitraging

### The L² Norm Formula

Trigger Market uses an L² norm AMM that constrains prices to a unit sphere:

**Constant maintained**:
```
C² = q₁² + q₂² + ... + qₙ²
```

Where:
- `C` = liquidity constant = ||q||₂ (the L² norm of quantities)
- `qᵢ` = share quantity for opinion i
- `n` = number of active opinions

**Price derivation**:
Each opinion's price is its normalized quantity:

```
Price(i) = qᵢ / C = qᵢ / √(q₁² + q₂² + ... + qₙ²)
```

This ensures: √(p₁² + p₂² + ... + pₙ²) = 1 (unit sphere constraint)

**Properties**:
- Smooth price curves
- Convex trading costs
- Larger trades → larger price impact
- Prices satisfy L² norm constraint √(Σp_i²) = 1 automatically

### Example: Price Calculation

**Market setup**:
- Initial liquidity: 1 ETH = 10,000¢
- C = √10,000 = 100
- 4 equal opinions initially

**Initial state**:
- Each qᵢ = 50 (since 50² × 4 = 10,000)
- Each price = 50/100 = 0.5 (50%)
- Verification: √(0.5² + 0.5² + 0.5² + 0.5²) = √1 = 1 ✓

**After buying React** (example):
- React q: 50 → 65
- Other opinions q adjust to maintain C² = 10,000
- New React price: 65/100 = 0.65 (65%)
- Other prices decrease (geometrically, not linearly)
- Still satisfies √(Σp_i²) = 1

## Price Dynamics

### What Moves Prices?

Prices change due to:

**1. Trading Activity**

**Buying**:
- Removes shares from pool
- Increases that opinion's q
- Raises its price
- Others' prices fall to maintain L² norm constraint

**Selling**:
- Adds shares to pool
- Decreases that opinion's q
- Lowers its price
- Others' prices rise to maintain L² norm constraint

**2. Information Events**

External information affects trading:
- Technical announcements
- Community sentiment
- Expert endorsements
- Real-world developments

These don't directly move prices - traders interpreting information do.

**3. Market Dynamics**

- **Momentum**: Prices trending attract more traders
- **Mean reversion**: Extreme prices sometimes correct
- **Consensus formation**: Prices converge as debate matures
- **Settlement approach**: Volatility decreases near settlement

**4. Opinion Replacement**

When an opinion is replaced:
- Price space redistributes
- New equilibrium established
- Can create trading opportunities

### Price Impact

**Trade size affects price impact**:

**Small trades**:
- Minimal price movement
- Nearly constant price
- Low slippage

**Medium trades**:
- Noticeable price impact
- Some slippage
- Affects entry price

**Large trades**:
- Significant price movement
- High slippage
- May require splitting

**Formula** (approximate):
```
Price Impact ∝ Trade Size / Total Liquidity
```

**Example**:
- Market liquidity: 1 ETH
- Buy 0.01 ETH → ~1% price impact
- Buy 0.1 ETH → ~10% price impact
- Buy 0.5 ETH → ~40%+ price impact

<Callout title="Managing Price Impact" type="warn">
Large traders should:
- Check price impact before confirming
- Consider splitting large orders
- Trade over time, not all at once
- Choose high-liquidity markets
</Callout>

### Slippage

**Slippage** = difference between expected and executed price

**Example**:
- Current price: 40¢
- You buy large amount
- Execution price: 43.2¢ (average)
- **Slippage**: 3.2¢ (8%)

**Why slippage occurs**:
- AMM moves price as you buy
- Larger trades → more movement
- Your own trade impacts your price

**Minimizing slippage**:
- Trade smaller amounts
- Use high-liquidity markets
- Accept higher price impact for urgency
- Split orders across time

## Price Interpretation

### Reading the Market

**High-priced opinion (0.60+, 60%+)**:
- Strong consensus leader
- Less upside potential
- Lower risk
- May be approaching settlement consensus

**Medium-priced opinion (0.30-0.60, 30-60%)**:
- Competitive positioning
- Room for movement either direction
- Active debate ongoing
- Good risk/reward balance

**Low-priced opinion (0.10-0.30, 10-30%)**:
- Weak consensus currently
- High upside if consensus shifts
- Higher risk
- Contrarian opportunity

**Very low price (under 0.10, under 10%)**:
- Near rejection by market
- Extreme upside if reversal
- Very high risk
- Often replacement candidates

### Price Spreads

The distribution of prices reveals market structure:

**Tight spread** (e.g., 0.52, 0.51, 0.49, 0.48):
- No clear leader
- Active debate
- High volatility potential
- Good trading opportunities

**Wide spread** (e.g., 0.85, 0.35, 0.25, 0.15):
- Clear consensus leader
- Hierarchy established
- Lower volatility
- Approaching settlement

**Two-way split** (e.g., 0.71, 0.71):
- Two main contenders (at √0.5 ≈ 0.707 each)
- Others eliminated
- Binary-like structure
- Final showdown

### Historical Price Analysis

Price history reveals:

**Uptrend**:
- Growing consensus
- Momentum building
- Consider if sustainable

**Downtrend**:
- Losing support
- Negative momentum
- Assess if reversal possible

**Volatility**:
- Active price discovery
- New information processing
- Higher risk, higher opportunity

**Stability**:
- Consensus forming
- Less new information
- Approaching settlement

**Reversal**:
- Consensus shifted
- Major information event
- Trend change

## Advanced Price Concepts

### Price Sensitivity

How responsive prices are to trades:

**High liquidity market**:
- Less sensitive
- Large trades needed to move price
- Stable pricing
- Lower impact per trade

**Low liquidity market**:
- More sensitive
- Small trades move price significantly
- Volatile pricing
- Higher impact per trade

**Relationship**:
```
Price Sensitivity ∝ 1 / (Total Liquidity)³
```

Price sensitivity increases dramatically as liquidity decreases.

### Cross-Opinion Effects

Buying one opinion affects all others:

**Example market** (4 opinions):
- React: 0.55 (55%)
- Vue: 0.50 (50%)
- Svelte: 0.45 (45%)
- Angular: 0.40 (40%)
- Verification: √(0.55² + 0.50² + 0.45² + 0.40²) ≈ 1

**Buy React**:
- React: 0.55 → 0.65 (+0.10)
- Vue: 0.50 → 0.46 (-0.04)
- Svelte: 0.45 → 0.42 (-0.03)
- Angular: 0.40 → 0.37 (-0.03)
- Verification: √(0.65² + 0.46² + 0.42² + 0.37²) ≈ 1 (L² constraint maintained)

**Pattern**:
- Buying one opinion affects all others
- Changes follow spherical geometry, not linear redistribution
- L² norm constraint always maintained
- Effects depend on relative positions on unit sphere

### Implied Consensus

Reading between the lines:

**Price gaps** reveal:
- Strength of belief differences
- Hierarchy of consensus
- Potential for movement

**Example**:
- PostgreSQL: 0.70 (70%)
- MySQL: 0.45 (45%)
- Gap: 0.25 = Strong consensus PostgreSQL > MySQL

**Stability of gaps**:
- Persistent gap → Settled consensus
- Closing gap → Shifting consensus
- Widening gap → Strengthening consensus

### Arbitrage Opportunities

Prices should reflect true consensus. Deviations create arbitrage:

**Scenario**: New information emerges, but price hasn't adjusted yet

**Strategy**:
1. Identify mispriced opinion
2. Trade to correct it
3. Profit from correction
4. Market returns to efficiency

**Example**:
- React announces major performance breakthrough
- Price still at 0.35 (undervalued)
- Buy at 0.35
- Others catch on, price → 0.65
- Profit from information edge

**Efficient markets**:
- Arbitrage opportunities rare
- Prices adjust quickly
- Information incorporated fast

## Practical Price Analysis

### Before Trading

Check:

**Current price**:
- Is it reasonable given the opinion's merits?
- Does it reflect recent information?

**Price history**:
- Trending up or down?
- Stable or volatile?
- Recent reversals?

**Price impact**:
- How much will your trade move the price?
- Is slippage acceptable?

**Relative prices**:
- How does this compare to others?
- Are gaps justified?
- Any mispricing?

### During Market Evolution

Monitor:

**Price movements**:
- Which opinions gaining/losing?
- Why are they moving?
- Is it justified?

**Volume**:
- High volume = strong conviction
- Low volume = weak signal

**Velocity**:
- Fast changes = new information
- Slow drift = gradual consensus shift

**Convergence**:
- Prices stabilizing?
- Settlement approaching?

### Near Settlement

Consider:

**Final price prediction**:
- Where will it settle?
- Current price vs expected final
- Profit potential remaining

**Time value**:
- How long until settlement?
- Opportunity cost of capital
- Better opportunities elsewhere?

**Risk**:
- Chance of reversal before settlement
- New information possibility
- Exit strategy if wrong

## Common Price Patterns

### The Front-Runner

**Pattern**: One opinion consistently leads
- Price: 0.65-0.85 (65-85%)
- Trend: Stable or slowly rising
- Volume: High on that opinion

**Interpretation**: Consensus forming around leader
**Strategy**: Assess if fairly priced or still undervalued

### The Dark Horse

**Pattern**: Low-priced opinion suddenly surges
- Price: 0.25 → 0.55 rapidly
- Trend: Sharp uptrend
- Volume: Increasing

**Interpretation**: New information or reassessment
**Strategy**: Evaluate if momentum sustainable

### The Standoff

**Pattern**: Two opinions locked in tight race
- Prices: ~0.71 each (perfect split at √0.5)
- Trend: Oscillating
- Volume: High on both

**Interpretation**: Market split, no clear consensus
**Strategy**: Pick a side or wait for resolution

### The Fade

**Pattern**: Previously leading opinion declining
- Price: 0.75 → 0.45 over time
- Trend: Downtrend
- Volume: Decreasing

**Interpretation**: Consensus shifting away
**Strategy**: Avoid or short if conviction strong
