# How It Works

Trigger Market enables prediction markets on subjective debates through a novel combination of mechanisms. This page explains the complete system from end to end.

## The Big Picture

Traditional prediction markets require oracles to determine outcomes. This limits them to objective questions with verifiable answers.

**Trigger Market's innovation**: Replace oracles with **endogenous settlement** based on price stability over time.

### Three Core Mechanisms

1. **L² Norm AMM** - Automated market maker that enables continuous trading
2. **Dynamic Opinion Replacement** - New opinions can replace existing ones
3. **Time-Weighted Settlement** - Markets settle when sustained consensus is detected

These work together to create a self-contained system for subjective consensus formation.

## Market Lifecycle

<Steps>

### Market Creation

Anyone can create a market by:
- Defining the debate question
- Proposing initial opinions (typically 3-5)
- Providing initial liquidity
- Setting market parameters

**Example**:
- Question: "Best database for startups?"
- Initial opinions: PostgreSQL, MySQL, MongoDB, Redis
- Initial liquidity: 1 ETH
- Parameters: Default settlement criteria

The creator earns 0.25% of all future trading volume.

### Opinion Activation

Markets start with a set of active opinions:
- Users can trade between these opinions
- Prices are determined by the AMM
- All active opinion prices satisfy the L² norm constraint √(Σp_i²) = 1

**Initial state example** (4 equal opinions):
- PostgreSQL: 0.50 (50%)
- MySQL: 0.50 (50%)
- MongoDB: 0.50 (50%)
- Redis: 0.50 (50%)
- **Verification**: √(0.5² + 0.5² + 0.5² + 0.5²) = √1 = 1 ✓

### Trading Phase

Users trade based on their beliefs:

**Buying an opinion**:
- Increases its price
- Decreases other opinions' prices (maintaining L² norm)
- Removes liquidity from the pool

**Selling an opinion**:
- Decreases its price
- Increases other opinions' prices (maintaining L² norm)
- Adds liquidity to the pool

**Example evolution**:
- Day 1: PostgreSQL 0.50 → Buy pressure
- Day 3: PostgreSQL 0.62 (growing consensus)
- Day 7: PostgreSQL 0.75 (strong leader)

### Opinion Replacement (Optional)

New opinions can be proposed any time:

**Proposal process**:
1. Submit new opinion with description
2. Provide collateral
3. Opinion becomes a "candidate"
4. If it gains support, it can activate

**Replacement mechanism**:
- Candidate must gather minimum support
- If successful, replaces lowest-priced active opinion
- Price space redistributes
- Trading continues with new opinion set

This keeps markets dynamic and responsive to new perspectives.

### Settlement Detection

Markets settle when all four criteria are met:

**Criterion 1: Minimum Age**
- Market must be at least N days old (typically 7)
- Prevents premature settlement

**Criterion 2: Time-Weighted Variance**
- Historical price variance below threshold
- Weights prices by duration, not occurrence
- Ensures sustained stability

**Criterion 3: Instantaneous Variance**
- Recent price volatility below threshold
- Confirms current stability
- No recent disruptions

**Criterion 4: Age-Weighted Variance**
- Combines time-weighting with market age
- Older markets settle more easily
- Recognizes diminishing returns of extended debate

**All four must be met simultaneously.**

### Settlement and Payouts

When settlement triggers:

1. **Prices lock**: Final prices are recorded
2. **Trading stops**: No more trades possible
3. **Payouts available**: Users can claim

**Payout formula**:
```
Payout = Number of Shares × Final Settlement Price
```

**Example**:
- You hold 500 shares of PostgreSQL
- PostgreSQL settles at 0.68 (68%)
- Your payout: 500 × $0.68 = $340

**Every opinion pays out**. No binary win/lose.

</Steps>

## The AMM: L² Norm Market Maker

The automated market maker is the pricing engine.

### How It Works

The AMM maintains a constant:
```
C² = q₁² + q₂² + q₃² + ... + qₙ²
```

Where:
- `C` = liquidity constant (square root of initial liquidity in cents)
- `qᵢ` = quantity of shares for opinion i

**Key properties**:
- Prices satisfy L² norm constraint √(Σp_i²) = 1
- Larger trades have larger price impact
- Impact scales with liquidity

### Price Calculation

Each opinion's price is derived from its share quantities:

```
Price = qᵢ / C = qᵢ / √(q₁² + q₂² + ... + qₙ²)
```

This creates:
- **Smooth price curves** (not linear)
- **Convex trading costs** (large trades expensive)
- **Automatic arbitrage** (prices self-correct)
- **Unit sphere constraint** (√(Σp_i²) = 1)

### Liquidity Dynamics

**High liquidity markets**:
- Lower price impact per trade
- Smaller bid-ask spreads
- More efficient price discovery

**Low liquidity markets**:
- Higher price impact
- Wider spreads
- More volatile prices

Liquidity comes from:
- Initial market creation
- Net selling (adds liquidity)
- Opinion replacements can add liquidity

### Example: Price Impact

**Market**: 1 ETH liquidity (10,000¢ worth)
- C = 100 (√10,000)

**Initial prices** (4 equal opinions):
- Each: q = 50, price = 50/100 = 0.5 (50%)
- Verification: √(0.5² × 4) = √1 = 1 ✓

**After buying PostgreSQL with 0.1 ETH**:
- PostgreSQL q: 50 → 61 (example)
- PostgreSQL price: 61/100 = 0.61 (61%)
- Other opinions adjust down geometrically
- Verification: √(Σp_i²) = 1 still holds

The exact math ensures the L² norm constraint is maintained.

## Opinion Replacement Mechanism

Dynamic opinion replacement keeps markets relevant.

### Why Replacement Matters

Debates evolve:
- New technologies emerge
- Perspectives shift
- Initial options become outdated

Replacement allows markets to adapt without starting over.

### The Replacement Process

<Steps>

### Opinion Proposal

Anyone can propose a new opinion:
- Submit opinion text/description
- Provide collateral (typically ~0.01-0.1 ETH)
- Opinion becomes a candidate

### Candidate Support Phase

The candidate opinion needs support to activate:
- Users can "support" by staking
- Support represents belief the opinion is valuable
- No trading yet - just signaling

### Activation Threshold

If candidate gathers enough support:
- It becomes "eligible for replacement"
- Can challenge the weakest active opinion
- Replacement may trigger based on criteria

### Replacement Execution

When replacement executes:
1. Lowest-priced active opinion is removed
2. Candidate opinion becomes active
3. Liquidity redistributes
4. New pricing equilibrium established
5. Trading resumes with new opinion set

### Post-Replacement

- Removed opinion becomes inactive
- Holders of removed opinion get their shares converted
- Market continues with updated opinion set

</Steps>

### Replacement Example

**Before**:
- PostgreSQL: 0.65 (65%)
- MySQL: 0.55 (55%)
- MongoDB: 0.45 (45%)
- Redis: 0.30 (30%) ← Weakest

**New candidate**: "SQLite" gains support

**Replacement**:
- Redis removed (was 0.30)
- SQLite added at equilibrium price
- New distribution:
  - PostgreSQL: 0.63 (63%)
  - MySQL: 0.53 (53%)
  - MongoDB: 0.44 (44%)
  - SQLite: 0.32 (32%)
- Verification: √(0.63² + 0.53² + 0.44² + 0.32²) ≈ 1 ✓

**Result**: Market now reflects SQLite as relevant option.

## Time-Weighted Consensus

Settlement is based on **sustained** price stability, not snapshots.

### Why Time-Weighting?

**Problem with snapshots**:
- Price at 50¢ for 1 second = same weight as 50¢ for 1 hour
- Allows gaming via brief price manipulation
- Doesn't capture true sustained consensus

**Solution: Exponentially-weighted moving average (EWMA)**:
- Prices weighted by how long they persist
- Brief spikes contribute negligible weight
- Only sustained prices matter

### How EWMA Works

Imagine price history:
- 30¢ for 1 day
- 35¢ for 3 days
- 32¢ for 1 hour
- 40¢ for 2 days

**Simple average**: Equal weight to all prices → Misleading

**EWMA**: Weights by duration → 35¢ and 40¢ dominate

### Variance Calculation

Settlement criteria check **variance** of time-weighted prices:

```
Variance = Average((Price - Mean)² × Duration)
```

**Low variance** = prices have been stable over time
**High variance** = prices fluctuating significantly

### The Four Criteria Revisited

Each criterion uses time-weighting differently:

**Criterion 2**: Historical time-weighted variance
- Looks at entire market history
- Exponentially weights by duration
- Must be below threshold T₂

**Criterion 3**: Instantaneous variance
- Recent price movements only
- Ensures current stability
- Must be below threshold T₃

**Criterion 4**: Age-weighted variance
- Combines time-weighting with market age
- Threshold decreases as market ages
- Older markets settle easier

### Anti-Gaming Properties

**Attack vector**: Try to prevent settlement by brief price spikes

**Why it fails**:
- Brief spike (1 minute at different price)
- Duration = 1/1440 of a day
- Weight in EWMA ≈ 0.07%
- Negligible impact on variance calculation

**Cost**: Attacker loses on trading fees and price impact

**Benefit**: Minimal variance increase

**Result**: Economically irrational to attempt

## Fees and Incentives

The system uses fees to align incentives.

### Fee Structure

**Every trade pays 1% total fees**:
- **0.5% swap fee** → Goes to the protocol/liquidity
- **0.25% creator fee** → Goes to market creator
- **0.25% opinion fee** → Split among opinion creators in the trade

**Example trade** (buying 0.1 ETH of PostgreSQL):
- 0.0005 ETH → Protocol/liquidity
- 0.00025 ETH → Market creator
- 0.00025 ETH → PostgreSQL opinion creator(s)

### Incentive Alignment

**Market creators**:
- Earn 0.25% of all volume forever
- Incentivized to create popular, engaging debates
- Want markets to attract trading

**Opinion creators**:
- Earn 0.25% of trades involving their opinion
- Incentivized to propose valuable perspectives
- Want their opinion to be actively traded

**Traders**:
- Profit from correct consensus prediction
- Provide liquidity and price discovery
- Want efficient, fair markets

**System**:
- Fees fund protocol development
- Sustainable without inflation
- Value accrues from usage
