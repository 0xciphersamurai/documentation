# Opinions and Markets

Markets and opinions are the core building blocks of Trigger Market. Understanding their structure and relationships is essential for effective trading.

## What is a Market?

A **market** represents a single subjective debate or question.

**Anatomy of a market**:
- **Question**: The debate topic (e.g., "Best state management for React?")
- **Opinions**: Competing perspectives (e.g., Redux, Zustand, Jotai, Context)
- **Liquidity**: Capital available for trading
- **State**: Active, approaching settlement, or settled
- **History**: All trades, price movements, and events

### Market Types

While all markets share the same mechanism, they differ in nature:

**Technical Debates**:
- "Best programming language for web apps?"
- "Most effective testing framework?"
- "Superior database architecture?"

**Strategic Questions**:
- "Best go-to-market strategy for B2B SaaS?"
- "Most promising AI application area?"
- "Optimal pricing model for marketplaces?"

**Governance Decisions**:
- "Should the DAO fund this proposal?"
- "Best allocation of treasury funds?"
- "Priority feature for next quarter?"

**Subjective Preferences**:
- "Most underrated city for remote work?"
- "Best framework for productivity?"
- "Most impactful book on entrepreneurship?"

<Callout title="No Wrong Answers" type="info">
Unlike traditional prediction markets where one outcome is "correct," Trigger Market debates can have multiple valid perspectives. Consensus reflects **collective belief**, not objective truth.
</Callout>

### Market Parameters

Each market has configurable parameters:

**Settlement Criteria**:
- Minimum age (typically 7 days)
- Variance thresholds (T₂, T₃, T₄)
- Time-weighting constants

**Economic Parameters**:
- Initial liquidity
- Fee rates (typically 1% total)
- Opinion replacement rules

**Governance**:
- Who can propose opinions
- Collateral requirements
- Moderation policies (if any)

## What is an Opinion?

An **opinion** is a specific perspective or answer within a market's debate.

**Components of an opinion**:
- **Title**: Short name (e.g., "PostgreSQL")
- **Description**: Explanation of this perspective
- **Creator**: Who proposed this opinion
- **Status**: Active, Candidate, or Inactive
- **Creation date**: When it was proposed
- **Fee share**: 0.25% of trades involving this opinion

### Opinion States

Opinions move through different states:

**Candidate**:
- Newly proposed
- Not yet tradeable
- Gathering support
- Can become active if supported

**Active**:
- Currently tradeable
- Has a price (0-100¢)
- Included in AMM
- Earns fees from trades

**Inactive**:
- Was active but got replaced
- No longer tradeable
- Historical record only
- Holders converted to other opinions

### Opinion Quality

Not all opinions are equal. Quality opinions:

**Clear and specific**:
- Well-defined perspective
- Unambiguous meaning
- Not overlapping with others

**Distinct**:
- Represents unique viewpoint
- Meaningfully different from other opinions
- Adds value to the debate

**Relevant**:
- Addresses the market question
- Timely and applicable
- Resonates with traders

**Defensible**:
- Has rational basis
- Can be argued for
- Supported by evidence or reasoning

<Callout title="Opinion Creation Strategy" type="info">
When proposing opinions (whether creating a market or suggesting new ones), focus on **meaningful diversity**. Each opinion should offer a genuinely different perspective that traders would want to support.
</Callout>

## Market Structure

### Active Opinion Set

Each market has 3-7 active opinions at any time:

**Why minimum 3?**
- Ensures meaningful debate
- Prevents binary yes/no structure
- Requires nuanced consensus

**Why maximum 7?**
- Maintains price discovery efficiency
- Prevents dilution of liquidity
- Keeps UI manageable

**Typical: 4-5 active opinions**
- Balances diversity and tractability
- Allows comprehensive coverage
- Maintains healthy liquidity per opinion

### Price Space

Active opinions satisfy the L² norm constraint on a unit sphere:

**Example market** (4 opinions):
- Opinion A: 0.60 (60%)
- Opinion B: 0.55 (55%)
- Opinion C: 0.45 (45%)
- Opinion D: 0.40 (40%)
- **Verification**: √(0.60² + 0.55² + 0.45² + 0.40²) ≈ 1 ✓

**Properties**:
- One opinion rising → others falling (through spherical geometry)
- Cannot all be high simultaneously (unit sphere constraint)
- Competitive dynamics through L² norm, not linear sum

### Opinion Relationships

Opinions relate to each other:

**Substitutes**:
- Similar perspectives
- Compete for the same consensus
- Example: React vs Preact (very similar)

**Complements**:
- Different aspects of same question
- Can both be "right" in different contexts
- Example: "Best for startups" vs "Best for enterprise"

**Orthogonal**:
- Unrelated dimensions
- Don't directly compete
- Example: "Fast" vs "Easy to learn"

Well-designed markets have opinions that are substitutes (competing for consensus on the same dimension).

## Creating a Good Market

### Choosing a Question

Great market questions are:

**Subjective**:
- No objectively verifiable answer
- Multiple valid perspectives exist
- Consensus is meaningful

**Timely**:
- Relevant to current discussions
- People care about the answer
- Stakes are significant

**Focused**:
- Specific enough to be answerable
- Not too broad or vague
- Clear scope and context

**Interesting**:
- Attracts diverse opinions
- Genuine debate possible
- Community will engage

### Selecting Initial Opinions

When creating a market, choose initial opinions that:

**Cover the space**:
- Represent major perspectives
- Include consensus favorites
- Add contrarian views

**Are distinct**:
- Meaningful differences between them
- Not redundant or overlapping
- Each has unique selling points

**Invite more**:
- Leave room for new opinions later
- Don't exhaustively list everything
- Best initial opinions attract trading

**Example**:
Market: "Best framework for building APIs?"

**Good initial opinions**:
- Express.js (Node.js standard)
- FastAPI (Python, modern)
- Rails (Ruby, convention over config)
- Go Fiber (Performance-focused)

**Why good?**
- Cover different languages/paradigms
- Distinct philosophies
- Room for others (Django, Spring, etc.)

### Bad Market Examples

**Too objective**:
- "Will Bitcoin reach $100K in 2024?"
- Has verifiable answer
- Should use traditional prediction market

**Too vague**:
- "What's best?"
- Best for what? In what context?
- Needs specificity

**Too niche**:
- "Best obscure library no one uses?"
- No one cares
- Won't attract traders

**Binary structure**:
- "Should we do X? Yes/No"
- Not enough diversity
- Better as traditional market

## Opinion Dynamics

### How Opinions Compete

Opinions compete for price share:

**Trading drives competition**:
1. New information emerges about opinion A
2. Traders buy A, driving price up
3. A's price increase comes from other opinions falling
4. Creates relative winners and losers

**Example**:
- PostgreSQL gets major performance update
- Traders buy PostgreSQL
- PostgreSQL: 0.50 → 0.70 (+0.20)
- MySQL: 0.55 → 0.45 (-0.10)
- MongoDB: 0.45 → 0.38 (-0.07)
- Others decrease to maintain L² norm √(Σp_i²) = 1

### Opinion Lifecycle

Typical lifecycle of a successful opinion:

**Phase 1: Introduction** (Days 0-2)
- Opinion proposed as candidate
- Gathers initial support
- Activates if threshold met
- Initial price ~equal to others

**Phase 2: Price Discovery** (Days 3-10)
- Traders evaluate merits
- Price fluctuates with information
- Volume increases
- Position-taking occurs

**Phase 3: Consensus Formation** (Days 10-30)
- Price stabilizes around true consensus
- Less volatility
- Occasional corrections
- Leadership emerges

**Phase 4: Settlement** (Days 30+)
- Prices highly stable
- Little new information
- Consensus clear
- Settlement criteria approach

**Phase 5: Settlement** (When criteria met)
- Prices lock
- Trading stops
- Payouts available

### Opinion Replacement

Opinions can be replaced if they become irrelevant:

**Reasons for replacement**:
- Losing relevance (price declining toward 0)
- Better alternative proposed
- Fundamental change (e.g., library deprecated)
- Overlapping with another opinion

**Replacement mechanism**:
1. New opinion proposed as candidate
2. Candidate gathers support
3. If successful, replaces weakest active opinion
4. Market continues with new opinion set

**Impact on traders**:
- Holders of replaced opinion get converted
- New trading opportunities emerge
- Market remains relevant

## Advanced: Market Mechanics

### Liquidity Provision

Markets have liquidity from:

**Initial creation**:
- Creator provides starting liquidity
- Typically 0.1 - 1 ETH
- Sets initial market size

**Net selling**:
- Sells add liquidity to pool
- Buys remove liquidity
- Natural fluctuation

**Opinion replacement**:
- Can inject liquidity
- Rebalances the pool
- Maintains tradability

### Market Maturity

Markets mature over time:

**Young markets** (0-7 days):
- High volatility
- Rapid price discovery
- More speculative
- Cannot settle (minimum age)

**Mature markets** (7-30 days):
- Lower volatility
- Established consensus
- More information processed
- Can settle if criteria met

**Old markets** (30+ days):
- Very stable
- Consensus formed
- Settlement likely soon
- Lower trading volume

### Settlement Probability

Estimating settlement likelihood:

**Check criteria progress**:
- ✅ Criterion 1: Age > 7 days
- ⏳ Criterion 2: Variance 85% of threshold
- ✅ Criterion 3: Recent stability good
- ⏳ Criterion 4: Age-weighted at 90%

**Interpretation**:
- 2 of 4 met → Settlement distant
- 3 of 4 met → Settlement possible soon
- 4 of 4 met → Settlement imminent

**Trading strategy impact**:
- Near settlement → Lower volatility expected
- Far from settlement → More trading opportunity
- Just met → Lock in positions or exit

## Key Takeaways

✅ **Markets** = subjective debates with multiple opinions

✅ **Opinions** = specific perspectives, can be Active, Candidate, or Inactive

✅ **Prices satisfy √(Σp_i²) = 1** - opinions compete within L² norm constraint

✅ **3-7 active opinions** - balance diversity and efficiency

✅ **Opinion replacement** - keeps markets dynamic and relevant

✅ **Market quality matters** - good questions + good opinions = good markets

✅ **Lifecycle awareness** - young vs mature vs settling markets differ
