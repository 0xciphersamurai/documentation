# The Breakthrough

The challenge seemed impossible: create a market without an oracle. Let people trade on subjective opinions where there's no "right answer" to verify. Make it actually settle and pay out fairly.

**Trigger Market solves this with an elegant insight.**

## The Core Innovation

Instead of markets settling when an oracle determines an outcome, **markets settle when natural consensus emerges through sustained price stability.**

Think about it: on subjective questions, the goal isn't to predict some external event. It's to discover where consensus lies. What does the collective intelligence of participants believe?

The market price *is* the signal. When prices stabilize over time - when the community's beliefs have solidified - that stability itself indicates consensus has formed.

At that point, the market settles. Each opinion pays out proportionally to its final price.

## Why This Works

Traditional markets need oracles because there's a future event to verify: "Did Bitcoin hit $100K?" - check the price feed.

Consensus markets don't predict external events. They aggregate current beliefs about subjective topics. The market's job isn't to predict the future - it's to discover what participants currently think.

**The stability-based settlement mechanism perfectly matches this goal:**

- Trading reveals information through continuous price discovery
- Prices naturally stabilize when consensus forms
- Sustained stability indicates genuine agreement
- Final prices represent the collective's considered judgment

No oracle needed. The market's own dynamics determine both the outcome AND when consensus has been reached.

## What Makes It Stable?

The settlement mechanism is sophisticated enough to distinguish between temporary price movements and genuine consensus formation.

Without going into mathematical details, the key insight is that the system gives more weight to price stability that persists over time. A price that holds steady for days matters much more than a brief spike.

This means:

**Market manipulation doesn't work.** You can't just pump a price briefly before settlement. The system only recognizes sustained, genuine stability.

**Natural consensus emerges.** As participants trade based on their evolving beliefs, prices move until they find levels that reflect genuine collective agreement.

**Settlement is fair.** By the time the market settles, prices have stabilized for long enough that everyone had time to react, trade, and find the right levels.

## The Multi-Opinion Innovation

Traditional prediction markets are binary: yes/no, will/won't, happens/doesn't. This works for objective events but terribly for subjective questions.

Trigger Market supports **multiple competing opinions** in each market:

| Question | Opinions |
|----------|----------|
| Best framework? | React, Vue, Svelte, Angular |
| Most promising AI application? | Healthcare, Education, Creative Tools, Research, Enterprise |
| Strategic priority? | User growth, Revenue, Product quality, Ecosystem, Infrastructure |

This multi-opinion structure captures the reality of subjective debates: multiple valid perspectives exist, and discovering their relative support is what matters.

**The elegant part:** All opinions settle simultaneously at their final prices. Someone who bought a less-popular opinion still gets paid out - just proportionally less. There are no binary "winners" and "losers."

This creates sophisticated incentives. You're not just betting that your pick "wins" - you're evaluating how much consensus each option will ultimately attract.

## Dynamic Opinion Evolution

Markets aren't static. As debates evolve, new perspectives emerge. Old options become irrelevant.

Trigger Market handles this through **opinion proposals**: anyone can suggest new opinions to add to a market. If their proposal gains enough support, it gets activated and becomes tradeable.

This means markets can adapt as understanding deepens. The "best framework" market from 2024 can incorporate whatever new frameworks emerge in 2025. The debate evolves with the community's collective intelligence.

## What This Enables

By removing the oracle requirement, entire categories of questions become market-tradeable:

**Strategic Decisions**
"Should we expand to enterprise first or focus on consumer adoption?" Create a market. Watch the organization's distributed intelligence aggregate through trading.

**Resource Allocation**
"Which research directions deserve funding?" Market prices represent collective assessment. Resource allocation follows consensus.

**Technical Choices**
"What's the best approach for scaling this protocol?" Engineers, researchers, and users all trade based on their knowledge. The market aggregates it all.

**Governance Questions**
"Should the DAO implement this proposal?" Instead of binary votes, create a market comparing different versions or approaches. See what garners genuine support.

**Philosophical Debates**
"What values should define the community?" Even pure preference questions can have markets. The goal is discovering where consensus lies, not determining objective truth.

## Why It Feels Different

If you're used to prediction markets, consensus markets will feel strange at first. You're not predicting future events or trying to be "right" about what happens.

You're doing something subtler: evaluating where collective consensus will form. You're reading social dynamics, community sentiment, information flow. You're trading on your understanding of how groups reach agreement.

It's less about having insider information and more about understanding people. Less about predicting outcomes and more about reading the room - at scale, with skin in the game.

This creates opportunities for a different kind of intelligence. The best traditional prediction market traders might not dominate here. The winners might be community builders, social dynamics experts, people who understand how consensus forms.

## The Technical Achievement

Without going too deep, what makes this possible is a sophisticated combination of:

- An automated market maker designed for opinion pricing
- A settlement mechanism that detects genuine stability
- An opinion activation system that lets markets evolve
- Economic incentives that reward accurate evaluation

The mathematics is elegant. The economics work. The incentives align.

But the real achievement is conceptual: recognizing that consensus markets are fundamentally different from prediction markets, and designing mechanisms that match that difference.

## What This Means

Markets are one of humanity's best tools for aggregating information. But they've been limited to objective questions with verifiable outcomes.

Trigger Market removes that limitation.

Now, any question - no matter how subjective - can have a market. With all the benefits markets provide: price discovery, liquidity, transparency, incentive alignment.

The oracle problem isn't solved with a better oracle. It's solved by recognizing that for subjective questions, you don't need one.
