# Real-World Impact

Consensus markets aren't just a clever technical innovation. They're a new tool for human coordination that can transform how groups make decisions.

Let's explore what becomes possible.

## DAO Governance Revolution

Current DAO governance is broken. Forum discussions go nowhere, snapshot votes get minimal participation, whales dominate outcomes, and decisions lack genuine community buy-in.

**With consensus markets:**

A DAO considering a major protocol upgrade creates a market comparing different approaches. Instead of binary "yes/no" votes, options trade continuously. Stakeholders can express nuanced views: "I support approach A at 0.45, but approach B at 0.55" by trading accordingly.

The market aggregates this distributed intelligence. Engineers who understand technical tradeoffs trade based on feasibility. Users trade based on UX preferences. Token holders trade based on economic impact.

As information flows through the community - debates in Discord, technical analyses, usage data - prices adjust. The market continuously updates its consensus view.

When the market settles, the DAO sees clear signals: approach B has consensus at 0.67, approach A at 0.48, approach C at 0.38. Not a contentious 51/49 vote - a measured evaluation of each option's support.

**Resources can flow toward consensus, not just majority.**

## Corporate Strategy Meets Market Wisdom

Companies spend millions on consultants to help with strategic decisions. They run surveys, hold offsites, debate in executive meetings.

**With consensus markets:**

The company launches internal markets on strategic questions. "Should we focus on enterprise or SMB next year?" Employees trade based on their front-line knowledge.

Sales teams know what customers want. Product teams understand feasibility. Finance evaluates economics. All this distributed knowledge gets aggregated through prices.

The beautiful part: skin in the game. Trade on "enterprise focus" and you're staking your judgment. If it becomes consensus, you profit. This creates real incentives for honest evaluation rather than political posturing.

The market might reveal that while executives lean SMB, front-line employees have conviction in enterprise (as shown by their trading). That's valuable signal that traditional meetings would never surface.

## Research Prioritization at Scale

Research labs and foundations struggle with funding allocation. Which directions are most promising? Who decides what deserves resources?

Current approach: committees evaluate proposals, make subjective calls, allocate millions based on a handful of expert opinions.

**With consensus markets:**

Create markets on research directions. Let the entire scientific community trade on what's promising.

Researchers close to the work trade based on technical feasibility. Practitioners trade based on real-world applicability. Funders trade based on strategic importance. All these perspectives aggregate into prices.

The market doesn't determine absolute truth - it reveals where expert consensus lies. A research direction at 0.75 has strong community support. One at 0.30 faces skepticism.

Funding can follow consensus. Not perfectly, but as one strong signal among many.

**The wisdom of experts gets aggregated transparently.**

## Community Decision-Making

Open source projects, protocols, and movements constantly face subjective choices. "Should we focus on performance or features?" "Which direction aligns with our values?" "What deserves developer attention?"

These debates rage in GitHub issues, Discord servers, Twitter threads. Outcomes emerge through rough consensus and running code - or don't emerge at all.

**With consensus markets:**

The community creates markets on key questions. Contributors trade based on their understanding. Maintainers trade based on technical constraints. Users trade based on their needs.

Market prices provide continuous feedback on community sentiment. A proposed feature trading at 0.65 has support. One at 0.20 faces pushback. This helps maintainers prioritize based on actual consensus rather than whoever yells loudest.

Trading also creates skin in the game. It's easy to post "we should do X!" in Discord. It's different to put 500 USDC on it. This filters signal from noise.

## Prediction Markets Meet Governance

Here's where it gets interesting: some questions blend objective and subjective elements.

"Will the protocol's TVL exceed $1B by end of year?" - That's objective, traditional prediction markets work.

"Should we implement EIP-XXXX?" - That's subjective, consensus markets work.

But many real decisions have both: "If we implement approach A, will TVL grow to $1B?"

**With consensus markets**, you can create combined markets:

- Market 1: "Which technical approach should we take?" (consensus)
- Market 2: "What will TVL be in 12 months if we take approach A?" (prediction)
- Market 3: "What will TVL be in 12 months if we take approach B?" (prediction)

Now the community can both (1) decide which approach has consensus AND (2) predict the objective outcomes of each choice.

This combines the strengths of prediction markets (forecasting measurable outcomes) with consensus markets (aggregating subjective preferences) for more sophisticated decision-making.

## Cultural and Social Questions

Not everything is about business and governance. Some of the most interesting applications are purely cultural.

**Music and art:** Which emerging artist is gaining consensus recognition? Markets can measure cultural consensus formation in real-time.

**Social movements:** What direction has community support? Markets reveal where genuine agreement lies versus vocal minorities.

**Intellectual debates:** Which framework is gaining academic consensus? Markets aggregate distributed expert opinion across universities and research groups.

**Design choices:** Should the product use dark mode by default? Let users trade their preferences with skin in the game.

These aren't trivial. Cultural consensus matters enormously - it shapes communities, movements, and collective identity. But measuring it has always been fuzzy. Markets make it quantifiable.

## The Coordination Layer

Zoom out further. What Trigger Market really provides is a coordination layer for subjective decision-making.

Right now, coordination happens through:
- **Markets** (for objective events)
- **Voting** (for binary choices)
- **Debates** (for exploring options)
- **Committees** (for making calls)

Each has strengths but also severe limitations. Consensus markets slot into a unique niche:

**Better than voting because:** Continuous feedback, intensity of preference, skin in the game

**Better than debates because:** Produces clear signals, scales to large groups, has resolution

**Better than committees because:** Aggregates distributed knowledge, transparent, resistant to capture

**Better than prediction markets because:** Works on subjective questions without oracles

This isn't replacing existing tools. It's adding a new one for problems that other tools handle poorly: subjective questions that need group consensus.

## Long-Term Vision

If consensus markets work - if they become a standard coordination primitive - the impact could be profound.

**Organizations become more intelligent:** Distributed knowledge gets aggregated efficiently. Decisions have genuine buy-in because they reflect real consensus.

**Resources flow better:** Funding, attention, and effort go toward directions that have community support rather than politics or luck.

**Governance becomes scalable:** DAOs, protocols, and projects can coordinate at scale without centralized decision-making.

**Cultural consensus becomes measurable:** We can track how communities evolve, what gains acceptance, where agreement forms.

**Coordination costs drop:** Less time in meetings, fewer contentious votes, faster decisions with better outcomes.

This is ambitious. This is long-term. But the foundation is here, working, on-chain, today.
