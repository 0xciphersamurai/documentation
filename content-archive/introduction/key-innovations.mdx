# Key Innovations

Three technical breakthroughs make oracle-free consensus markets possible. This page explains what makes Trigger Market different from anything that came before.

<Callout title="Non-technical?" type="info">
  You don't need to understand these mechanisms to use Trigger Market. Skip to [Getting Started](/docs/getting-started/quick-start) if you want to jump straight to trading.
</Callout>

## 1. Settlement via Price Stability

**The Problem**: Traditional prediction markets need external oracles to determine outcomes. Who wins the election? Check official results. Will BTC hit $100K? Check price feeds.

**Our Solution**: **Stability = Consensus**. When prices stop moving significantly, participants have reached agreement. No external input needed.

### How It Works

The settlement mechanism uses four criteria that must **all** be met:

#### Criterion 1: Minimum Age
Markets must be at least a specified age (configurable per market, typically 7 days).

**Why**: Prevents flash consensus on immature markets. Gives time for discovery, debate, and price evolution.

#### Criterion 2: Time-Weighted Variance
Historical price variance (volatility) must be below threshold (typically 0.01% = 1% standard deviation).

**Why**: Ensures prices have been historically stable, not just momentarily calm.

**Key Innovation**: Prices are weighted by **duration**, not just occurrence. A 10-minute price spike contributes negligible weight compared to days of stability.

```
Weight of price P = (Time P persisted) × (Exponential decay factor)
```

Brief manipulation gets filtered out automatically. Extended stability dominates.

#### Criterion 3: Current Deviation
Current prices must be within a threshold (typically 2%) of their historical means.

**Why**: Prevents sniping attacks where someone manipulates prices right at settlement time.

Even if historical variance is low, current prices must match the stable average.

#### Criterion 4: Sustained Confirmation
Criteria 2-3 must hold for multiple consecutive checks (typically 5 checks × 30min = 2.5 hours).

**Why**: Prevents false positives from temporary calm periods.

### Settlement Timeline Example

```
Day 1-7:  Market active, high volatility, criterion 1 not met
Day 8-20: Trading continues, prices slowly converge
Day 21:   Low variance achieved (criterion 2 ✓)
          Current deviation low (criterion 3 ✓)
          Confirmation counter: 1/5
Day 21+:  Every 30min, check criteria
          If still stable: increment counter
          If becomes unstable: reset counter to 0
After 5 consecutive confirmations (2.5 hours):
          → SETTLEMENT TRIGGERED
          → Market frozen
          → Payouts calculated from final prices
```

<Callout title="Why this prevents manipulation" type="warn">
  To manipulate settlement, an attacker would need to:

  1. Wait for natural stability (can't be rushed)
  2. Maintain low historical variance (requires weeks of stability)
  3. Keep current prices near historical mean (can't spike prices)
  4. Sustain all criteria for 2.5 hours (expensive to maintain artificial prices)

  Cost far exceeds potential benefit.
</Callout>

### The Math Behind Stability

As markets accumulate liquidity, they naturally become harder to move. This is due to the Hessian magnitude (measure of price sensitivity) decreasing cubically with total liquidity:

Price sensitivity is proportional to 1 / (total_liquidity)³

**What this means**: A $1000 market is 8× harder to move than a $500 market (2³ = 8).

This creates **natural stabilization** as mature markets resist price changes, setting up conditions for settlement.

## 2. Dynamic Opinion Replacement

**The Problem**: Traditional prediction markets lock in outcomes at creation. Can't adapt to new information, emerging perspectives, or improved framings.

**The Solution**: **Competitive opinion marketplace**. Opinions can enter and exit based on demonstrated support.

### The Mechanism

At any time, there are two types of opinions:

**Active Opinions** (max 10)
- Currently in the AMM (automated market maker)
- Tradeable via buy/sell
- Have quantities and prices
- Compete to avoid being weakest

**Candidate Opinions** (unlimited)
- Outside the AMM
- Stakeable (participants pool money)
- Waiting to enter
- Compete to exceed threshold

### Replacement Condition

The **weakest active opinion** (lowest quantity/value) can be replaced when:

```
Candidate stake ≥ (Weakest value) × (1 + threshold)
```

Default threshold: 5%

**Example**:
- Weakest active opinion: $456 value
- Required candidate stake: $456 × 1.05 = $478.80
- Candidate "MobX is underrated" has $520 staked
- → Eligible for replacement!

### Replacement Process

When replacement triggers (can be called by anyone):

**Step 1: Exit**
- Weakest opinion liquidated
- Holders receive payout (calculated from liquidation formula)
- Opinion moves to candidate pool with liquidation value as initial stake

**Step 2: Enter**
- Candidate's stake converts to AMM quantity
- New opinion becomes active at calculated entry price
- Stakers receive shares proportional to their contribution

**Step 3: Circular Flow**
- Exited opinion doesn't disappear - it becomes a candidate again
- Can re-enter if it accumulates more support
- Creates evolutionary pressure toward best opinions

<Callout title="Why 5% threshold?" type="info">
  Without a buffer, opinions could flip-flop endlessly. The 5% threshold ensures replacements demonstrate meaningfully stronger support. This parameter is governance-adjustable (typically 0%-20%).
</Callout>

### Opinion Lifecycle

```
New Opinion Proposed
        ↓
    Candidate
    (stake accumulates)
        ↓
    [Exceeds threshold]
        ↓
    Active
    (tradeable in AMM)
        ↓
    [Gets replaced by stronger candidate]
        ↓
    Candidate
    (can re-enter if support returns)
```

Opinions can cycle through active ↔ candidate states multiple times. Markets track:
- Times active
- Times replaced
- Peak price achieved
- Total volume
- Comeback count

## 3. L² Norm Automated Market Maker

**The Problem**: Traditional AMMs (like Uniswap's x×y=k) don't have the mathematical properties needed for oracle-free settlement.

**The Solution**: Custom AMM using **Euclidean (L²) norm** as the cost function.

### What is the L² Norm?

For quantities `q₁, q₂, ..., qₙ`:

```
Cost = √(q₁² + q₂² + ... + qₙ²)
```

This is just the length of the vector in n-dimensional space. Simple, elegant, powerful.

### Why L² Specifically?

#### Property 1: Automatic Price Normalization

Prices are defined as normalized quantities:

```
Price of opinion i = pᵢ = qᵢ / Cost = qᵢ / √(q₁² + q₂² + ... + qₙ²)
```

These prices automatically satisfy the **L² norm constraint**:

```
√(p₁² + p₂² + ... + pₙ²) = 1  (unit sphere)
```

This is the fundamental constraint - prices lie on a unit sphere in n-dimensional space.

**Benefit**: No artificial constraints needed. Prices stay in bounds naturally through spherical geometry.

#### Property 2: Closed-Form Solutions

All operations have instant, exact solutions:

**Buy $M worth of opinion i**:
```
Δq = -qᵢ + √(qᵢ² + 2M·Cost + M²)
```

One formula, no iteration, computed in nanoseconds.

**Sell to receive $R**:
```
Δq = -qᵢ + √(qᵢ² - 2R·Cost + R²)
```

**Complete withdrawal payout**: Calculated using the vault liquidation formula

**Entry quantity for candidate with stake M**: Calculated to ensure proper vault value increase

Every operation: one formula, instant result.

**Benefit**: Efficient onchain execution. No gas wasted on iterations.

#### Property 3: Convergence Property

The Hessian (matrix of price sensitivities) has magnitude:

```
||H|| ∝ 1 / Cost³
```

As the market grows (Cost increases), price sensitivity decreases **cubically**.

**Benefit**: Mature markets naturally stabilize, facilitating settlement.

#### Property 4: Substitution Effects

Buying opinion A decreases prices of all other opinions proportionally. This creates proper competitive dynamics between perspectives.

**Benefit**: Opinions compete for finite "probability mass".

### Visual Intuition

Imagine opinions as vectors in space. The L² norm cost function creates a sphere. All valid price vectors lie on this sphere's surface.

Trading moves you along the sphere. Prices can't leave the sphere (natural bounds). The sphere's radius grows with liquidity (stability).

## How These Innovations Work Together

The three mechanisms reinforce each other:

**L² AMM** provides:
- Natural price bounds
- Closed-form efficiency
- Convergence properties

**Dynamic opinions** allow:
- Market evolution
- Improved framings
- Competitive pressure

**Stability-based settlement** uses:
- AMM convergence properties
- Time-weighted filtering
- Mathematical certainty

Together, they create markets that:
1. **Start simple** (anyone can create, initial opinions)
2. **Evolve intelligently** (better opinions replace weak ones)
3. **Converge naturally** (maturity → stability)
4. **Settle deterministically** (criteria → settlement → payouts)

No oracles. No subjective judgment. Just math, markets, and consensus.
