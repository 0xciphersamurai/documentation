# How Markets Work

Forget everything you think you know about markets. Consensus markets look similar to traditional markets - prices, trades, buy and sell - but underneath, they work differently.

**Understanding these differences is where your edge comes from.**

## The Core Mechanic

Traditional markets have buyers and sellers. Someone wants to buy Apple stock. Someone wants to sell. They meet at a price. Transaction happens.

Consensus markets work through continuous liquidity. There's no counterparty waiting on the other side. Instead, you trade against an automated market that adjusts prices based on supply and demand.

**Think of it like a smart vending machine.** Want to buy opinion shares? The market sells them to you and adjusts the price up. Want to sell? The market buys them from you and adjusts the price down.

This means you can always trade. No waiting for counterparties. No orderbook depth worries. Just instant execution at current prices.

## How Prices Move

Prices reflect collective consensus. When someone buys an opinion, its price rises. When someone sells, price falls.

But it's more elegant than simple supply and demand. The market balances all opinions simultaneously. When one opinion rises, others adjust. The system maintains internal consistency across all perspectives.

**This creates interesting dynamics:**

Buy opinion A aggressively? Its price shoots up. But the system also redistributes consensus weight away from other opinions. Their prices adjust down slightly. The market maintains equilibrium.

This interconnection means you can't just pump one opinion. The system automatically rebalances. True consensus formation requires sustained, genuine belief.

## Liquidity and Depth

Every market has liquidity - capital available for trading. More liquidity means easier trading, less price impact, better execution.

**When you trade:**

Small trades in liquid markets barely move prices. You buy $100 worth at essentially the current price.

Larger trades or thin markets show noticeable price impact. You buy $10,000 worth and the price climbs as you buy. You get worse average execution.

This price impact isn't a bug - it's a feature. It prevents manipulation. Big trades cost more because they're moving consensus significantly. That cost reflects the information weight of large trades.

## The Multi-Opinion Structure

Traditional prediction markets are binary: yes or no. Will Bitcoin hit $100K? Yes or no.

Consensus markets support multiple competing opinions simultaneously. "Best framework?" might have React, Vue, Svelte, Angular, and more.

**This changes everything:**

In binary markets, you're either right or wrong. In multi-opinion markets, everything pays out proportionally.

An opinion doesn't need to "win" to be profitable. You profit if you bought below where it settles. An opinion settling at 40 cents still pays out - just less than one settling at 60 cents.

This creates sophisticated strategy space. You're not picking "the winner" - you're evaluating how much consensus each perspective will attract.

## Settlement Through Stability

Here's where it gets really different: markets settle when consensus naturally emerges.

There's no oracle declaring winners. No external judgment. The market itself determines when agreement has been reached - through sustained price stability.

**The logic is elegant:**

When people genuinely agree on something, trading slows. Prices stabilize. No one's trying to move the market because consensus has formed.

The settlement mechanism detects this stability. When prices hold steady long enough, the market concludes consensus has been reached. Settlement triggers. Everyone gets paid proportionally to final prices.

**What this means for traders:**

You're not predicting some future event. You're evaluating where prices will stabilize once the community reaches agreement.

That's a different skill. It's less about forecasting and more about understanding group dynamics. Where will this conversation land once everyone's had their say?

## Opinion Evolution

Markets aren't static. As debates evolve, new perspectives emerge. Old options become irrelevant.

Consensus markets handle this through opinion proposals. Anyone can suggest adding a new opinion to an existing market.

**The mechanism is simple:**

Propose a new opinion. Put some capital behind it. If others agree it's valuable, they can support the activation. If it gains enough support, it becomes a tradeable opinion in the market.

This means markets adapt. The "best framework" debate from 2024 can incorporate frameworks that emerge in 2025. The conversation evolves with the community.

**For traders, this creates opportunities:**

Spot emerging perspectives early. Support their activation. Trade them before others recognize their importance. Being early to valuable new opinions is highly profitable.

## Price Discovery Process

Here's what actually happens when markets are active:

**Phase 1: Initial Chaos** - Market launches. Nobody knows what anything's worth. Prices are exploratory. High volatility. Maximum uncertainty.

**Phase 2: Information Emergence** - Traders start sharing analysis. Some perspectives gain support. Others lose it. Prices begin reflecting actual community sentiment.

**Phase 3: Consensus Formation** - Patterns become clear. One or two opinions pull ahead. Others consolidate support at various levels. Volatility decreases.

**Phase 4: Stability** - The conversation has been had. People know where they stand. Prices stabilize. Trading slows. Settlement approaches.

**Phase 5: Settlement** - Sustained stability triggers settlement. Final prices reflect the community's collective judgment. Payouts occur.

Understanding which phase a market is in tells you how to trade it. Early phases: take bigger risks for bigger rewards. Late phases: conservative positions or profit-taking.

## What Moves Prices?

In traditional markets, prices move on earnings reports, economic data, news events. Clear external triggers.

Consensus markets are more subtle. Prices move on:

**Information flow:** Key community members express opinions. Technical analyses get published. Debates happen in public forums. This distributed information gradually gets incorporated into prices.

**Social momentum:** An opinion gains buzz. People talk about it. Others notice and investigate. Momentum builds. Prices reflect this growing attention.

**New participants:** Fresh traders enter with different perspectives. They trade based on their views. Prices adjust to incorporate this new information.

**External events:** Sometimes real-world developments favor certain perspectives. A framework releases a major update. A governance approach succeeds elsewhere. Prices respond.

**Positioning changes:** Large holders adjust positions. A whale selling creates opportunity for others. Prices temporarily dip before recovering.

The key insight: prices move on distributed social signals, not centralized data releases. Reading these signals is the skill to develop.

## Timing Considerations

Unlike day trading, consensus markets reward patience.

Consensus doesn't form in hours. It forms over days and weeks. Communities need time to debate, consider, reconvider, and ultimately agree.

**This creates a different tempo:**

Checking prices every five minutes is pointless. Nothing meaningful happens that fast.

Checking every few days captures real movement. You see genuine shifts in consensus, not random noise.

Holding positions for weeks is normal. The timeframe matches the natural speed of community decision-making.

**Successful traders adapt their expectations.** This isn't high-frequency trading. It's high-conviction, patient position-taking.

## The Role of Fees

Small fees apply to every trade. These fund market creators and opinion proposers - the people who made the market possible.

For traders, fees create a natural barrier to overtrading. Unlike zero-fee environments where hyperactivity is costless, here every trade has friction.

**This is good news:** It means the hyperactive traders lose money on fees. Patient traders with genuine insight don't overtrade. They wait for clear opportunities. Fees punish noise, reward signal.

Factor fees into your strategy. If you're trading frequently, they compound. If you're taking patient positions, they're negligible compared to potential returns.

## Market Depth and Maturity

New markets are thin. Little capital, few participants, high price impact. Trading is riskier but potential returns are enormous.

Mature markets are deep. More capital, many participants, low price impact. Trading is safer but potential returns are smaller.

**Your strategy should match market maturity:**

Early markets: If you have strong conviction and can handle volatility, early positions offer maximum upside. You're literally helping establish consensus.

Mature markets: If you want safety and clarity, wait until markets develop. You give up some upside but get clearer signals.

Different traders thrive in different environments. Some love the chaos of early markets. Others prefer the clarity of mature ones. Know yourself.

## Reading Market Signals

Successful trading requires reading between the lines:

**Large trades** mean someone has strong conviction. Follow or fade them depending on your view.

**Volume spikes** indicate new information entered the market. Figure out what changed.

**Price stability** suggests consensus is forming. Late movers should be conservative.

**Volatility clusters** mean active debate is happening. Opportunity for those who can read it correctly.

**Trending prices** show momentum. Sometimes worth following, sometimes worth fading. Context matters.

Learn to read these signals. They're the market speaking. Listen.

## The Network Effect

As more people trade consensus markets, something interesting happens: price quality improves.

Early markets have sparse information. Few traders mean prices reflect limited perspectives.

As markets mature and attract participants, prices become more informative. They aggregate more viewpoints, more information, more distributed knowledge.

**This creates a paradox:** Early markets offer better opportunities but worse price signals. Mature markets offer clearer signals but fewer opportunities.

The traders who win are those who can operate in both environments. Use sparse early signals when others can't. Trust mature signals when they're clear.
