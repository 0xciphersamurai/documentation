# Making Trades

Theory is worthless without execution. You can understand markets perfectly and still lose money if you execute poorly.

**This is your guide to practical trading mastery.**

## The Pre-Trade Checklist

Before every trade, run through this mental checklist. It takes 30 seconds and prevents most beginner mistakes.

**Do I understand this market?** If you're trading something you don't genuinely understand, stop. Trade only where you have insight.

**Do I have a thesis?** Can you articulate in one sentence why this trade will work? If not, don't trade.

**What's my entry plan?** Know the price you want. Don't just "buy at market." Have a target entry.

**What's my exit plan?** Profit target? Loss limit? Hold until settlement? Decide now, not when emotions are high.

**Is my position size appropriate?** Too large and you'll panic. Too small and it doesn't matter. Get this right.

**Can I afford to lose this?** If losing this position would hurt significantly, it's too big. Size down.

If any answer is unclear, don't trade. Clarity before capital.

## Finding Trade Opportunities

**Browse active markets.** Look for debates you understand. Topics where you have genuine insight.

**Filter by your knowledge domains.** Technical trader? Look for framework or protocol debates. Community expert? Look for governance or strategic questions. Cultural observer? Look for preference and value debates.

**Check market age and activity.** Early markets are higher risk, higher reward. Mature markets are safer but less upside. Match the market stage to your risk tolerance.

**Read opinions carefully.** Don't just scan - read. Understand what each opinion actually represents. Nuance matters.

**Look for mispricing.** The whole game is finding opinions that are trading above or below where they'll settle. Your knowledge tells you what's mispriced.

## Analyzing Before Entry

You've found a potential trade. Now analyze it properly.

**What's the current consensus?** Look at prices. Which opinions are winning? Which are losing? Does this match your understanding of the community?

**What's the price history?** Check charts. Has this opinion been gaining or losing support? Trends matter.

**What's the volume?** High volume means confident pricing. Low volume means uncertainty - risk and opportunity.

**What information exists?** Read discussions. Check forums. See what community leaders are saying. Prices should reflect this information - if they don't, you've found edge.

**What's the counterargument?** Why might you be wrong? If you can't articulate this, you don't understand the trade well enough.

## Entry Execution

You've decided to trade. Now execute well.

**Choose your entry type:**

**Market Buy/Sell** - Trade immediately at current prices. Use this when you want instant execution and price impact doesn't matter.

**Planned Entry** - Wait for your target price. Use this when you have price discipline and time isn't critical.

**Scaled Entry** - Split your position across multiple trades over time. Use this for large positions or uncertain timing.

**Enter your amount:** Start with your planned position size. Don't inflate it at the last second because you're excited.

**Review price impact:** Check what your trade will do to prices. Large impact? Consider scaling. Small impact? You're good.

**Double-check everything:** Opinion correct? Amount right? Action is buy (not accidentally sell)? One last look prevents expensive mistakes.

**Execute:** Click through. Confirm transaction. Wait for blockchain confirmation.

**Document:** Write down your thesis, entry price, and planned exit. Future you will thank you.

## Managing Price Impact

Large trades move prices. This is inescapable physics of liquidity.

**For small trades (under $1000 in most markets):** Price impact is minimal. Trade normally.

**For medium trades ($1000-$5000):** Check impact display. If it's under 3-5%, acceptable. If higher, consider splitting the trade.

**For large trades (over $5000):** Definitely split. Multiple smaller trades over hours or days get you better average execution than one big trade.

**The math is simple:** If your trade moves price 10%, you're getting terrible execution on the later shares. Split into five trades with 2% impact each, you save significant money.

## Position Sizing Strategy

How much to trade? Use these guidelines:

| Conviction Level | Position Size | Example |
|------------------|---------------|---------|
| **High Conviction** | 5-10% of portfolio | Deep domain expertise, unique information, strong thesis |
| **Standard Trade** | 2-5% of portfolio | Solid analysis, reasonable confidence, typical opportunity |
| **Speculative** | 1-2% of portfolio | Interesting idea, lower conviction, learning trade |
| **Experimental** | 0.5-1% of portfolio | Testing hypothesis, exploring market, building experience |

**Never go above 15% in a single position.** No matter how convinced you are, consensus markets can surprise. Diversification protects you.

**Scale with confidence.** Strong thesis, clear edge, extensive analysis? Size up. Uncertain or learning? Size down.

## Buying vs Selling

You can take positions in two directions.

**Buying (Going Long)**

You acquire shares. You profit if price rises or settles higher than your entry.

**When to buy:**
- You believe this opinion will gain consensus
- Community momentum is building
- Price is below where you think settlement will occur
- You have positive conviction

**Selling (Going Short)**

You sell shares you don't own. You profit if price falls or settles lower than your entry.

**When to sell:**
- You believe this opinion will lose consensus
- Hype is fading
- Price is above where you think settlement will occur
- You have negative conviction

**For beginners:** Start with buying. It's simpler conceptually and risk is capped at 100% loss. Selling can be counterintuitive at first.

## Reading The Interface

**Current Price:** What the opinion trades at right now. This is your baseline.

**Your Input Amount:** How much you're trading. Can be specified in dollars or shares.

**Estimated Execution:** What price you'll actually get after impact. Often slightly worse than current price for buys, slightly better for sells.

**Price Impact:** How much your trade will move the market. Keep this low.

**Fees:** Total cost of trading. Factor this into your profitability calculations.

**Estimated Shares/Value:** What you'll receive. Verify this matches your expectations.

**Confirm button:** Final step. Read the summary carefully before clicking.

## Common Execution Mistakes

**Mistake 1: Trading on Emotion**

You see price moving. FOMO hits. You trade impulsively.

**Fix:** Never trade in response to sudden price movement. Pause. Analyze. Is the movement justified? If you didn't want this trade 10 minutes ago, you probably don't want it now.

**Mistake 2: Chasing Prices**

Price is rising. You desperately try to buy. You keep entering higher amounts. You get terrible execution.

**Fix:** Set your entry price. If market moves past it, either wait for a pullback or move on. Chasing is how you buy tops.

**Mistake 3: Overtrading**

You make 20 trades a day. Fees eat your profits. You're reacting to noise, not signal.

**Fix:** Consensus markets reward patience, not activity. Wait for clear opportunities. Quality over quantity.

**Mistake 4: Ignoring Fees**

You trade frequently, not realizing 1% fees each way add up fast.

**Fix:** Calculate break-even. If you buy at 40 cents with 1% fees, you need to sell above ~40.8 cents just to break even. Factor this in.

**Mistake 5: Poor Position Sizing**

You put 50% of your portfolio in one trade. It goes against you. You panic.

**Fix:** Use the position sizing guidelines. Even if you're supremely confident, cap single positions at 15%.

**Mistake 6: No Exit Plan**

You enter with no idea when or why you'll exit. You hold losers too long and sell winners too early.

**Fix:** Decide exit criteria before entering. Write them down. Follow them.

## Trade Timing Strategies

**Early Entry Strategy**

Get in when markets launch. Maximum uncertainty, maximum opportunity.

**Pros:** Cheap prices, huge upside potential, opportunity to help establish consensus
**Cons:** High volatility, uncertain outcome, price signals are weak
**Best for:** Risk-tolerant traders with strong conviction

**Momentum Strategy**

Wait for clear trends. Buy what's working, sell what's not.

**Pros:** Trend confirmation, clearer signals, momentum often continues
**Cons:** Worse entry prices, risk of buying tops/selling bottoms
**Best for:** Traders who need confirmation before committing

**Value Strategy**

Buy opinions that appear undervalued relative to likely settlement.

**Pros:** Better risk/reward, contrarian edge, patient capital
**Cons:** Can be early (which feels like being wrong), requires strong conviction
**Best for:** Patient traders with deep domain knowledge

**Settlement Strategy**

Enter positions near settlement when outcome is nearly certain.

**Pros:** Low risk, clear thesis, short holding period
**Cons:** Small gains, crowded trades, limited opportunity
**Best for:** Conservative traders or capital preservation

Different strategies work for different traders. Experiment. Find what matches your skills and temperament.

## Managing Open Positions

**Check regularly but not obsessively.** Daily glance is fine. Hourly checking is neurotic.

**Update your thesis.** When new information emerges, revisit your reasoning. Still valid? Hold. Invalidated? Exit.

**Use alerts.** Set price notifications for key levels. Let the market tell you when attention is needed.

**Take partial profits.** Position up 50%? Consider selling half. Lock in gains while keeping upside exposure.

**Add on conviction.** Thesis being validated? Price dipped but fundamentals improved? Adding to winners can be smart.

**Cut losers decisively.** Wrong is okay. Staying wrong is expensive. Exit when thesis is invalidated.

## Exit Execution

**Profit-taking exits:**

Your trade worked. Time to close.

**Sell at market** if you want instant exit. **Wait for better prices** if you can be patient. **Scale out** if position is large.

Don't get greedy. "Target price or better" beats "trying to time the perfect exit."

**Loss-cutting exits:**

Your trade didn't work. Time to accept it.

**Exit quickly.** Losses that sit tend to grow. Cut decisively.

**Learn from it.** What went wrong? What can you do differently next time?

**Move on.** Don't revenge trade. Don't try to "win it back." Accept the loss and find the next clear opportunity.

## Advanced: Order Types

Most platforms offer simple market trades. Some offer more sophisticated options:

**Limit Orders:** Trade only at your specified price or better. Good for patient traders with price discipline.

**Stop Losses:** Automatically sell if price falls to certain level. Good for risk management, but can trigger on temporary dips.

**Trailing Stops:** Lock in profits as price rises while protecting against reversal. Advanced but powerful.

Start with simple market trades. Add complexity only when you understand the basics perfectly.

## The Trading Journal

Keep detailed records of every trade:

**Entry date, opinion, price, size, thesis** - Document what you expected and why.

**Exit date, price, profit/loss, outcome** - Record what actually happened.

**Lessons learned** - What did this trade teach you?

Review this journal monthly. Patterns emerge. You'll see what works for your style and what doesn't.

The best traders I know all keep journals. It's not optional for serious improvement.
