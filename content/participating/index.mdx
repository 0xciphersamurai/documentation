# Trading Opinions

This section explains the specific mechanics of trading on Trigger Market. We explain how the system works, not how you should trade.

## AMM, Not Orderbook

**Critical difference:** Trigger Market uses an Automated Market Maker (AMM), not an orderbook.

**What this means:**
- **No limit orders** (at least not currently implemented)
- **Only market orders** that execute immediately at current prices
- **You trade against the AMM**, not against other users
- **No need for counterparty** to take the other side of your trade
- **Prices adjust automatically** based on vault values

**Traditional orderbooks** require:
- Someone else placing an opposite order
- Waiting for your limit order to fill
- Market depth and liquidity from other traders

**AMM provides:**
- Always-available liquidity from the vault
- Instant execution at current price (plus slippage)
- No waiting for matches

## How Buying Works

When you buy shares of an opinion:

**What happens:**
1. You spend USDC (your purchase amount)
2. That USDC enters the opinion's vault
3. The opinion's vault value increases
4. The opinion's price rises
5. All other opinions' prices fall proportionally (they're now smaller slices of total liquidity)
6. You receive shares representing your proportional claim on that vault

**Example:**
- Python vault: $5,000 (price: 50%)
- JavaScript vault: $3,000 (price: 30%)
- Rust vault: $2,000 (price: 20%)
- Total liquidity: $10,000

You buy Python for $1,000:
- Python vault becomes $6,000
- Total liquidity becomes $11,000
- Python price: 54.5% (up from 50%)
- JavaScript price: 27.3% (down from 30% - vault unchanged but now smaller slice)
- Rust price: 18.2% (down from 20% - vault unchanged but now smaller slice)

**Your shares:** You receive shares worth $1,000 at the average price you paid during the trade. Your shares give you proportional claim on Python's vault.

## How Selling Works

When you sell (exit) shares of an opinion:

**What happens:**
1. You burn your shares
2. USDC exits the opinion's vault to you
3. The opinion's vault value decreases
4. The opinion's price falls
5. All other opinions' prices rise proportionally
6. You receive USDC based on vault value (minus exit fee)

**Exit fee:** 1% total on exit amount (currently 0.5% with platform fees disabled)
- 0.25% to debate creator
- 0.25% to opinion creators
- Platform fee: 0.5% (currently disabled)

**Example:**
Continuing from above, you exit your Python position:
- Python vault decreases by your proportional share
- Python price falls
- JavaScript and Rust prices rise (they're now bigger slices)
- You receive USDC minus the 0.5% exit fee

**Key point:** You can always exit. You're not dependent on another buyer. The AMM provides liquidity directly from the vault.

## Understanding Slippage

**Slippage:** The price change that happens *during* your trade.

**Why it occurs:**
- Your trade changes vault values
- Vault changes immediately affect prices
- Larger trades = larger vault impact = larger price changes

**Example - Small trade:**
- Buying $100 worth in $100,000 liquidity market
- Minimal vault impact (0.1%)
- Negligible slippage - price barely moves

**Example - Large trade:**
- Buying $5,000 worth in $10,000 liquidity market
- Significant vault impact (50% increase)
- Substantial slippage - price moves considerably during trade

**Slippage is nonlinear:**
The AMM mathematics mean slippage doesn't scale linearly. A trade that's 2x larger doesn't cause 2x slippage—it causes more. This is due to the L² norm cost function's properties.

**The UI shows expected slippage** when you preview trades. Pay attention to this before confirming.

**Slippage affects your average price:**
- You don't buy at a single price
- Price rises continuously as you buy
- Your average price paid is higher than the starting price
- The larger your trade relative to liquidity, the worse your average price

## Settlement vs Exit: When to Hold

**The tradeoff:** Exit now and pay fees/slippage, or hold until settlement?

**Reasons to hold for settlement:**
- **No slippage:** Settlement price is fixed, no execution impact
- **No exit fees:** Settlement payouts have no fees (claim payout, not exit position)
- **If you believe prices won't move much:** Holding captures final value without costs

**Reasons to exit early:**
- **If you believe prices will move against you:** Exit now to lock in current value
- **If you need capital elsewhere:** Liquidity now vs potential value later
- **If debate is far from settlement:** Opportunity cost of locked capital

**Example calculation:**

Current position value: $1,000
Expected exit value after 0.5% fee: $995

If you believe:
- Opinion will maintain or improve: hold for settlement (save $5 fee + avoid slippage)
- Opinion will decline significantly: exit now (pay $5 fee, avoid larger loss)

**Settlement has no deadline:** After settlement, you can claim your payout whenever you want. No rush.

## Price Ranges and Bounds

**Prices are always 0-100%:**
- Prices automatically normalized by the AMM mathematics
- Cannot go negative
- Cannot exceed 100%

**What this means:**
- An opinion can't "go to zero" unless its vault is literally empty
- An opinion can't go above 100% - that's the mathematical ceiling
- If one opinion rises, others *must* fall (zero-sum on squared prices)

**Practical implication:**
You're not just betting on your opinion going up. You're betting it will capture a larger share of total consensus relative to others.

## Position Value

**How your position value is calculated:**

Your position value = current price × your quantity

Where:
- Current price = your opinion's vault / total liquidity (L² norm)
- Your quantity = the shares you hold in that opinion

**This means:**
- If your opinion's vault grows: your position value increases
- If your opinion's vault shrinks: your position value decreases
- If total liquidity changes: price adjusts, affecting your position value

**Your P&L:**
Profit/Loss = current position value - total amount you spent acquiring shares

**The UI shows this** in your portfolio view.

## Replacement Risk

**If you hold the weakest active opinion:**

Your opinion can be replaced if a candidate accumulates 105% of your opinion's liquidation value.

**What happens if replaced:**
- Your opinion exits the active market
- Its vault is liquidated
- You receive proportional payout to candidate pool
- Your opinion becomes a candidate again
- You can either:
  - Exit from candidate pool (claim your liquidation value)
  - Keep stake in candidate pool (hope it re-enters later)

**Monitoring this:**
The UI shows which opinion is weakest and how close candidates are to replacement threshold.

**Strategy consideration:**
Holding the weakest opinion is riskier. If replaced, you're forced to realize current value (as candidate stake).

## No Counterparty Risk

**Key difference from peer-to-peer markets:**

The AMM is your counterparty, not another user.

**This means:**
- Someone else doesn't need to want to buy when you sell
- You don't need to find a matching order
- Liquidity comes from accumulated vault value, not from buyer demand
- You can always exit (subject to slippage and fees)

**The tradeoff:**
You pay via slippage (price impact) rather than via orderbook spreads. Different mechanism, similar economic cost for large trades.
