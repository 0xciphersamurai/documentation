# Trading Opinions

Trading in consensus markets is different from traditional prediction markets or stock trading. You're not predicting objective outcomes—you're positioning for where collective belief will settle.

## The Trading Mindset

### What You're Really Doing

When you trade opinions, you're:
- Expressing your view on what the community will conclude
- Staking capital on your understanding of the debate
- Contributing to the price discovery process
- Potentially profiting if your view aligns with final consensus

**You profit when:** The opinion you support ends up with a higher final price than you paid.

**You lose when:** The opinion's final price is lower than your cost basis.

## Trading Approaches

### Value Trading

**Philosophy:** Find opinions that are undervalued relative to their merit.

**Strategy:**
1. Research the debate thoroughly
2. Evaluate each opinion's arguments
3. Compare market prices to your assessment
4. Buy opinions you believe are underpriced

**Example:**
- Opinion A has strong technical merit: you assess it as "best"
- Market price: 35%
- You believe it should be 50%+
- You buy at 35%, betting consensus will shift

**Best for:** People who enjoy deep research and analysis

### Momentum Trading

**Philosophy:** Ride trends as they develop.

**Strategy:**
1. Identify opinions gaining momentum
2. Enter when trend confirms
3. Exit when momentum slows
4. Don't fight the trend

**Example:**
- Opinion B rising: 30% → 35% → 38%
- Volume increasing
- You buy at 40%, betting trend continues
- Sell at 48% when volume drops

**Best for:** Active participants who monitor frequently

### Contrarian Trading

**Philosophy:** Bet against consensus when you believe the market is wrong.

**Strategy:**
1. Identify where market has strong bias
2. Evaluate if bias is justified
3. Take opposite position if you spot error
4. Be patient—takes time to prove right

**Example:**
- Opinion C heavily favored at 60%
- You see flaws in reasoning that market missed
- Buy Opinion D at 20%, betting realization will come
- Hold through skepticism until market corrects

**Best for:** Independent thinkers with strong convictions

### Settlement Hold

**Philosophy:** Buy quality opinions and hold until debate settles.

**Strategy:**
1. Identify debates you understand well
2. Buy opinions you believe will win consensus
3. Hold through volatility
4. Claim settlement payout

**Example:**
- Debate: "Best database for use case X"
- You have deep expertise in databases
- Buy PostgreSQL at 42%
- Ignore day-to-day fluctuations
- Hold until settlement at 51%, profit $9 per share

**Best for:** Patient participants with domain expertise

## Execution Strategies

### Entry Strategies

**Market Buy (Immediate):**
- Buy at current price
- Instant execution
- Accept whatever price impact
- Good for: urgent positions, high conviction

**Limit Mindset (Patient):**
- Wait for price to dip to your target
- May miss opportunity if doesn't dip
- Better average price
- Good for: patient traders, less urgent positions

**Dollar Cost Averaging:**
- Split purchase across multiple buys
- Average out price volatility
- Reduces timing risk
- Good for: larger positions, uncertain timing

### Exit Strategies

**Profit Target:**
- Decide profit goal before entering
- Sell when target hit
- Removes emotion from decision
- Example: "Sell when +25%"

**Stop Loss:**
- Decide maximum loss tolerance
- Exit if hit to prevent larger loss
- Protects capital
- Example: "Sell if down 15%"

**Trailing Exit:**
- Let winners run
- Exit if price falls X% from peak
- Captures upside while protecting gains
- Example: "Sell if drops 10% from highest price"

**Settlement Hold:**
- No intermediate exit
- Hold regardless of fluctuations
- Payout determined by final price
- Requires conviction and patience

## Position Sizing

### Risk-Based Sizing

Allocate based on confidence and risk:

**High confidence, low risk:**
- 20-30% of trading capital
- Strong thesis, good research
- Setup looks favorable

**Medium confidence:**
- 10-15% of capital
- Reasonable thesis, some uncertainty
- Standard position

**Low confidence, high risk:**
- 5% of capital or less
- Speculative, learning trade
- Can afford total loss

### Diversification

**Within a debate:**
- Consider holding 2-3 different opinions
- Reduces single-opinion risk
- Express nuanced views

**Across debates:**
- Don't put all capital in one debate
- Spread across different topics
- Reduces correlation

**Balance:**
- Too diversified: can't track all positions
- Too concentrated: excessive risk
- Find your sweet spot

## Common Pitfalls

### 1. FOMO (Fear of Missing Out)

**Trap:** See opinion pumping, buy at peak
**Result:** Buy high, price corrects, you lose
**Fix:** Stick to your analysis, wait for pullbacks

### 2. Averaging Down Blindly

**Trap:** Opinion falling, keep buying "it's cheap now"
**Result:** Throwing good money after bad
**Fix:** Re-evaluate thesis when price falls significantly

### 3. Overtrading

**Trap:** Trading too frequently, paying excessive fees
**Result:** Fees eat into profits
**Fix:** Be selective, make each trade count

### 4. Ignoring Replacement Risk

**Trap:** Buy low-support opinion, it gets replaced
**Result:** Your position becomes candidate stake
**Fix:** Monitor vault values, understand vulnerability

### 5. Panic Selling

**Trap:** Price drops, sell immediately in fear
**Result:** Lock in losses unnecessarily
**Fix:** Have plan before entering, stick to it

## Advanced Concepts

### Relative Value

Sometimes the trade isn't about absolute prices, but relative:

**Example:**
- Opinion A: 48%
- Opinion B: 46%
- You believe A and B are equally strong
- A slightly overvalued, B slightly undervalued
- Sell some A, buy B
- Profit if they converge

### Opinion Lifecycle Trading

**Opportunity:** Trade around replacement events

**Strategy:**
- Buy candidates approaching threshold
- Profit when they enter AMM
- Or buy replaced opinions cheap
- Profit if they regain support and re-enter

**Risk:** Timing difficult, replacement not guaranteed

### Settlement Timing

**As settlement approaches:**
- Volatility typically decreases
- Prices converge to equilibrium
- Less opportunity for large moves
- Consider if worth holding vs. exiting

**Strategy:**
- Early in debate: more volatility, more opportunity
- Near settlement: lock in positions or take profits

## Tools for Better Trading

### Research

**Before trading:**
- Read full opinion descriptions
- Check debate comments
- Review price history
- See who's holding what
- Understand the context

**During holding:**
- Monitor developments
- Track new information
- Reassess thesis regularly

### Journaling

**For each trade, record:**
- Why you entered
- Your thesis
- Entry price and size
- Exit plan
- What happened
- What you learned

**Over time:** Patterns emerge, you improve

### Community Engagement

**Participate in discussions:**
- Ask questions
- Share your reasoning (without shilling)
- Learn from others' perspectives
- Build understanding

Better information → Better decisions

## Risk Management

### Never Risk More Than You Can Afford to Lose

Consensus markets are experimental. Capital is at risk. Only use funds you can lose completely.

### Size Appropriately

No single position should be able to wipe you out. Keep risk per position reasonable relative to total capital.

### Have an Exit Plan

Before entering any position:
- What price would make you sell?
- What developments would change your mind?
- How long are you willing to hold?

### Monitor Regularly

Don't set and forget. Check positions regularly:
- Has anything changed?
- Is your thesis still valid?
- Should you adjust?

### Accept Losses

Some trades won't work out. Accept it, learn from it, move on. Don't let ego prevent cutting losses.

## The Meta Game

### Information Edge

Your advantage comes from:
- Better understanding of the topic
- More thorough research
- Faster incorporation of new information
- Better market reading skills

Develop these edges.

### Patience

Good setups take time to develop. Don't force trades. Wait for opportunities where you have conviction.

### Adaptability

Markets evolve. What worked before might not work again. Stay flexible, keep learning.

### Community Contribution

The more people participate intelligently, the better markets become. Your trading helps with price discovery. You're contributing to the system.

<Callout title="Remember" type="info">
Trading is a skill developed over time. Start small, learn from each position, and build your understanding gradually. There are no shortcuts.
</Callout>
