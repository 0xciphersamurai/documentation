# Creating Debates

Anyone can create a consensus market for any debate. When you do, you earn a share of all trading fees forever. Here's how to turn arguments into markets.

## Why Create Debates?

### Economic Incentive

**You earn 0.25% of all trading volume** in your debate. Forever.

**Example 1: Anime Ranking**
- You create "Best anime of 2025"
- Over 3 months, generates $50,000 in trading volume
- You earn: $50,000 × 0.25% = $125

**Example 2: Technical Debate**
- You create "Best database for real-time apps"
- Over 6 months, generates $200,000 in trading volume
- You earn: $200,000 × 0.25% = $500

**Example 3: Cultural Argument**
- You create "Pineapple on pizza: acceptable or crime?"
- Generates $30,000 in volume
- You earn: $75

Popular or controversial debates generate significant fees over time.

### Capturing Existing Arguments

People are arguing online right now about:
- Every Reddit thread
- Every Twitter fight
- Every Discord debate
- Every YouTube comment war
- Every forum discussion

**You can turn any of these into a market.**

Find an argument that's already raging and make it real. When people finally have stakes, things get interesting.

### Building Reputation

Creating quality debates builds your reputation as:
- Someone who knows what people care about
- A curator of good arguments
- Part of the infrastructure
- Early to emerging debates

## What Makes a Good Debate?

### 1. People Actually Argue About It

The most important test: **Are people already fighting about this somewhere?**

**Great debates capture:**
- Existing Reddit threads with hundreds of comments
- Twitter fights that keep recurring
- Discord arguments that never end
- Forum debates that rage for years
- YouTube comment wars

**If people are arguing about it online with zero stakes, imagine what happens when you add capital.**

**Examples:**

**Anime and media:**
- "Best anime of 2025"
- "Sub vs dub for anime"
- "Most overrated anime"
- "Marvel vs DC movies: which is superior"
- "Best Star Wars movie (excluding Empire)"

**Food and taste:**
- "Pineapple on pizza: yes or no"
- "Which city has the best pizza"
- "Is a hot dog a sandwich"
- "Best burger chain"
- "Steak temp: anything above medium rare is wrong"

**Tech tribalism:**
- "Best programming language for beginners"
- "Tabs vs spaces"
- "Vim vs Emacs vs VS Code"
- "iOS vs Android"
- "PC vs Console for gaming"

**Lifestyle and culture:**
- "City life vs suburban"
- "Remote work vs office"
- "Best way to make coffee"
- "Dogs vs cats"
- "Morning person vs night owl: which is superior"

**Sports:**
- "GOAT debate for [sport]"
- "Best team dynasty in [sport] history"
- "Most overrated player"

**Philosophy and morals:**
- "Is eating meat ethical"
- "Universal basic income: good or bad"
- "Capitalism vs socialism for 2025"

**Technical opinions:**
- "Best React state management"
- "Which framework will dominate web dev"
- "Best database for [use case]"
- "Monorepo vs polyrepo"

**Gaming:**
- "Best game of 2025"
- "Most overrated AAA title"
- "PC vs Console"
- "Best FromSoft game besides Elden Ring"

**The bad ones: Things literally nobody argues about**
- "What's 2+2?" (objective, no debate)
- "Will it rain tomorrow?" (has oracle, not subjective)
- Questions so obscure nobody cares

**Key insight: If people are arguing about it passionately somewhere, it's a valid debate. "Trivial" doesn't mean invalid—it means you haven't found the community that cares.**

### 2. Multiple Legitimate Perspectives

The debate needs real alternatives:

**Good:**
- 3-6 distinct opinions
- Each has genuine support somewhere
- Reasonable people disagree
- No obviously "correct" answer (if there was, it wouldn't be a debate)

**Examples:**

**For "Pineapple on pizza":**
- Acceptable
- Crime against humanity
- Depends on other toppings
- Only acceptable with specific combinations

**For "Best programming language for beginners":**
- Python
- JavaScript
- Rust
- Go
- Depends on goals

**For "Remote vs office work":**
- Remote is superior
- Office is superior
- Hybrid is optimal
- Depends on industry/role

**Bad:**
- Only 2 binary options (makes it feel like a yes/no vote, not a nuanced debate)
- One option clearly superior by objective measures
- Options not mutually exclusive
- Poorly defined alternatives

### 3. Reachable Audience

**Ask yourself:**
- Where are people arguing about this NOW?
- Can I share this debate there?
- Will they care enough to put money on it?
- Is the topic evergreen or timely?

**Example circulation paths:**
- Reddit communities (subreddits related to the topic)
- Twitter hashtags and accounts
- Discord servers
- Telegram groups
- Forum communities
- LinkedIn groups (for professional debates)

**The bigger the existing argument, the more potential participants.**

## Creating Your Debate

### Step 1: Identify the Argument

Find debates happening right now:

**Method 1: Browse Reddit**
- Find threads with 100+ comments
- Look for recurring arguments
- Notice what people feel strongly about

**Method 2: Monitor Twitter**
- Track controversial threads
- Watch what technical communities fight about
- Notice recurring culture war topics

**Method 3: Listen to Your Community**
- What does your Discord argue about?
- What debates keep coming up?
- What do people feel strongly about?

**Method 4: Hot Topics**
- What's trending that has multiple valid takes?
- What recent announcement sparked debate?
- What cultural moment has people divided?

### Step 2: Frame the Question

**Good framing:**
- Clear and specific
- Neutral (doesn't favor one side)
- Captures the real debate
- Easy to understand

**Examples:**

**Good:** "Best anime of 2025"
**Bad:** "What's the greatest anime ever made in human history"

**Good:** "Pineapple on pizza: yes or no?"
**Bad:** "Should pineapple be allowed on pizza by law?"

**Good:** "Best programming language for web development in 2025"
**Bad:** "What's the ultimate programming language"

**Keep it focused. One debate = one question.**

### Step 3: Craft Initial Opinions

Start with 3-6 opinions that cover the main positions:

**Requirements:**
- Each opinion should be a legitimate position people actually hold
- Clear, concise descriptions (1-2 sentences)
- Neutral tone
- Distinct from each other

**Example: "Best anime of 2025"**

Opinion 1: Attack on Titan Final Season
"The conclusion to one of the most influential anime of the decade"

Opinion 2: Demon Slayer
"Consistently highest quality animation and storytelling"

Opinion 3: Jujutsu Kaisen
"Best character development and fight choreography"

Opinion 4: Frieren
"Most emotionally impactful and mature narrative"

Opinion 5: New dark horse will emerge
"2025 will have a breakout hit we haven't seen yet"

**Example: "Remote vs office work"**

Opinion 1: Remote is superior
"Better work-life balance, productivity, and cost savings"

Opinion 2: Office is superior
"Better collaboration, culture, and career growth"

Opinion 3: Hybrid is optimal
"Best of both: flexibility with in-person benefits"

Opinion 4: Depends on role/industry
"No universal answer, context determines best approach"

### Step 4: Launch

**What you need:**
- Debate title and description
- 3-6 initial opinions with descriptions
- Small amount of initial liquidity (optional but recommended)
- USDC on Base for gas fees

**Process:**
1. Navigate to "Create Debate"
2. Fill in details
3. Add initial opinions
4. Set initial liquidity if desired
5. Confirm transaction
6. Share with relevant communities

### Step 5: Promote

A debate needs participants. Share it:

**Where to share:**
- Reddit threads where this argument is happening
- Twitter with relevant hashtags
- Discord servers that care
- Telegram groups
- Your own social media

**How to frame it:**
- "Tired of arguing about X with no stakes? Put money on it"
- "This Reddit thread has 500 comments. Let's make it real"
- "Who actually believes their take on X? Market is live"
- "Finally, a way to settle the X debate with skin in the game"

**The pitch:** Take any online argument where people claim strong opinions, and ask "Will you bet on that?"

### Step 6: Seed and Engage

**Seed with initial positions:**
- Take a position yourself (shows commitment)
- Encourage others to stake their beliefs
- Don't manipulate—let the market find truth

**Engage in discussion:**
- Answer questions about the debate
- Clarify opinion descriptions if needed
- Foster healthy conversation
- Don't try to control outcomes

**Monitor and respond:**
- Watch for issues
- Address concerns
- Build community around the debate

## Quality Standards

### What Makes People Want to Participate

**Well-framed:**
- Question captures the real argument
- Neutral presentation
- Fair to all perspectives

**Good opinions:**
- Each represents a real position people hold
- Clear descriptions
- Distinct from each other
- Comprehensive coverage of the debate space

**Active promotion:**
- Creator shares in relevant communities
- Engages with participants
- Builds discussion

**Clean execution:**
- No errors in text
- Proper formatting
- Professional presentation

### What to Actually Avoid

**Genuinely objective questions:**
- Things with verifiable answers
- Questions with oracles available
- Math problems

**Actual spam:**
- Copy-paste debates with no thought
- Malicious or harmful content
- Purely extractive (no real debate)

**Biased framing:**
- One opinion obviously favored in descriptions
- Manipulative language
- Unfair representation

**Duplicate debates:**
- Same question already has an active market
- Fragments liquidity unnecessarily
- Confuses participants

**Abandoned debates:**
- Creator disappears
- No community engagement
- No response to issues

**Important: "Trivial" is NOT on this list. If people care enough to argue about it online, it's valid.**

## Legal and Ethical Considerations

### Neutrality

Present all opinions fairly, even ones you disagree with. You're creating infrastructure, not pushing an agenda.

### Intellectual Property

Don't use copyrighted material without permission. Write original descriptions or properly attribute.

### Sensitive Topics

Some debates may involve controversial subjects. Consider:
- Potential for harm
- Community standards
- Your own comfort level
- Local regulations

You have freedom to create debates on controversial topics, but also responsibility for how you frame them.

### Disclosure

If you have conflicts of interest (financial stake in an outcome, relationships with opinion proponents), consider disclosing in the debate description.

## The Creator's Role

As a debate creator, you're:

**A facilitator** - Creating space for arguments to become real
**A curator** - Identifying which arguments deserve markets
**A community builder** - Bringing participants together
**An entrepreneur** - Building valuable infrastructure

But you're not:

**A judge** - The market decides, not you
**A manipulator** - Let consensus emerge naturally
**A dictator** - Participants shape outcomes

Your role is to enable, not control.
