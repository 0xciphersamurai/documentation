# Dynamic Evolution

Traditional prediction markets lock in outcomes at creation. Consensus markets are different: they **evolve as understanding deepens**.

This adaptability is what makes them suitable for complex, subjective debates where the right framing emerges over time.

## The Problem with Static Markets

Imagine creating a prediction market in 2010: "Which mobile OS will dominate?"

Initial options:
- Symbian
- BlackBerry OS
- Windows Mobile
- Palm OS

**What's missing?** iOS and Android—the actual winners—barely existed yet.

A static market would be stuck with outdated options. Participants forced to choose from a set that doesn't reflect reality.

## The Dynamic Solution

In Trigger Market, opinions **compete for inclusion**:

- Markets start with initial opinions (3-10)
- Anyone can propose new opinions
- New opinions accumulate support in the candidate pool
- When a candidate demonstrates sufficient support, it can **replace** a weak active opinion
- Replaced opinions don't disappear—they become candidates and can return

The market **adapts** based on demonstrated economic support.

## How Opinion Competition Works

### The 10-Opinion Limit

Active markets are capped at 10 opinions maximum.

**Why?**
1. **Cognitive clarity**: Users can meaningfully evaluate 10 competing perspectives
2. **Mathematical stability**: Prices remain well-behaved with this limit
3. **UX simplicity**: Interface stays usable and clear

With 10 slots, opinions must compete to stay included.

### What Makes an Opinion "Weak"

Opinions are ranked by **vault value**—the total value they could pay out if completely liquidated.

Mathematically: V = q² / (||q|| + √(||q||² - q²))

In practice: **Lower support = lower vault value = more vulnerable to replacement.**

### The Replacement Threshold

A candidate can replace the weakest opinion when:

**Candidate stake ≥ 1.05 × (Weakest opinion's vault value)**

The 5% buffer (threshold) prevents rapid flip-flopping.

**Example:**
- Weakest opinion value: $100
- Candidate needs: $105+
- Buffer prevents immediate back-and-forth replacement

This threshold is governance-adjustable (typically 0-20%), balancing market stability with evolutionary pressure.

## The Replacement Cycle

### Stage 1: Proposal

Riley believes "Approach X" is missing from the debate. Riley proposes it with initial stake ($25).

**Approach X enters candidate pool:**
- Not yet in the AMM
- No price, just accumulated stake
- Progress bar shows: "$25 / $105 needed"
- Others can add stake in agreement

### Stage 2: Accumulation

More people review Approach X and stake:
- Alice adds $30
- Bob adds $25
- Carol adds $30
- Total: $110

**Threshold reached!** Approach X is now eligible for replacement.

### Stage 3: Replacement Trigger

Anyone (doesn't have to be a staker) clicks "Replace Weakest Opinion"

**What happens:**
1. **Contract identifies weakest opinion** (say, "Approach Z" with $100 vault value)
2. **Approach Z exits:**
   - Liquidated at $100
   - Becomes candidate with $100 stake
   - All Z shareholders' shares now represent claims on $100 candidate stake
3. **Approach X enters:**
   - $110 stake converts to AMM quantity
   - Now has price and trades
   - All X stakers' shares now represent claims on AMM quantity

**Gas cost:** ~$0.10-0.30 paid by whoever triggers
**Net result:** Market evolved. X in, Z out. Z can return if support builds.

### Stage 4: The Cycle Continues

**Weeks later:**
- Approach Z (now candidate) accumulates $130 stake
- Becomes eligible to replace whoever is now weakest
- Can re-enter the AMM
- Cycle continues...

## Why This Creates Better Markets

### Initial Framings Can Be Wrong

Debate creators don't always know the best way to frame questions. Initial opinions might:
- Miss important perspectives
- Include irrelevant options
- Use unclear language
- Reflect limited understanding

**Dynamic evolution lets the market correct this organically.**

### New Information Emerges

As debates progress:
- New approaches develop
- Technology evolves
- Understanding deepens
- Better framings become clear

**Markets adapt to incorporate new information.**

### Community Discovery

The best perspectives often emerge from collective exploration, not individual insight.

**Example:**
Initial debate: "Best database for our use case?"
- PostgreSQL
- MongoDB
- MySQL

After discussion:
- "Actually, we need a hybrid approach"
- This perspective gets proposed
- Gains support, enters market
- Market now reflects deeper understanding

### Natural Selection for Quality

Opinions that:
- Don't resonate with the community
- Lose relevance over time
- Were based on outdated information
- Simply don't have support

...get replaced by stronger alternatives.

The market **self-curates** based on demonstrated support.

## Practical Examples

### Example 1: Technology Debate

**Debate:** "Best frontend framework for 2025?"

**Week 1 (initial):**
- React: 55%
- Vue: 45%
- Angular: 35%
- Svelte: 30%

**Week 3 (evolution):**
- Someone proposes "Solid.js"
- Gains traction in candidate pool
- Replaces Angular (lowest support)
- New market: React, Vue, Svelte, Solid.js

**Week 6 (continued evolution):**
- "Qwik" proposed and enters
- Replaces Vue
- Market keeps adapting

**Result:** Market reflects current thinking, not initial assumptions.

### Example 2: Best Game of 2024

**Debate:** "What was the best game of 2024?"

**Initial:**
- Elden Ring DLC: 48%
- Baldur's Gate 3: 42%
- Tears of the Kingdom: 40%

**After launch season:**
- "Black Myth: Wukong" proposed
- Community plays it, discusses
- Gains support, enters market
- Replaces Tears of the Kingdom (least supported)

**After awards season:**
- "Metaphor: ReFantazio" proposed
- Enters discussion, gains support
- Eventually enters market

**Result:** The debate evolved as games launched and consensus shifted.

### Example 3: Opinion Comeback

**Week 1:** "Approach A" active at 25%
**Week 3:** Replaced by "Approach B" (became candidate)
**Week 5:** New information favors A, people stake
**Week 7:** "Approach A" re-enters, replaces "Approach C"
**Week 10:** Settles at 38% (strongest opinion)

**Result:** The market wasn't locked into initial mistake. A came back when understanding shifted.

## Managing Evolution as a Participant

### When Your Opinion Gets Replaced

**Don't panic.** This is normal market evolution.

**Your options:**
1. **Unstake immediately** - Cut losses, move on (1% fee)
2. **Hold and wait** - Believe in comeback, keep stake
3. **Add more stake** - Support re-entry campaign

**What you lost:** Exposure to AMM trading, potential gains from price appreciation

**What you kept:** Proportional ownership, optionality on comeback

### Supporting a Comeback

If you believe a replaced opinion deserves to return:

1. **Review the candidate**
   - Why was it replaced?
   - Has anything changed?
   - Does it have merit?

2. **Add stake**
   - Contribute to re-entry pool
   - Show economic commitment
   - Help reach threshold

3. **Advocate**
   - Discuss in debate comments
   - Explain why it matters
   - Build community support

**If enough people agree:** Opinion re-enters. You profit from being right.

### Defending Your Opinion

To prevent your active opinion from being replaced:

**Maintain support:**
- Keep position or add more
- Attract other believers
- Demonstrate ongoing value

**Stay relevant:**
- Respond to new information
- Adapt arguments
- Remain competitive

**Accept evolution:**
- If legitimately weaker, replacement is fair
- Can re-enter later if circumstances change

## The Meta-Game

Opinion replacement creates interesting dynamics:

### Strategic Timing

**When to propose:**
- Too early: Market not ready, wastes stake
- Too late: Consensus already forming
- Right time: Discussion active, minds open

### Coalitional Dynamics

Groups can coordinate to:
- Support promising candidates
- Defend valuable opinions
- Shape market composition

This isn't manipulation—it's collective action to express shared beliefs.

### Information Cascades

**Pattern:**
1. Someone proposes compelling opinion
2. Early stakers take risk
3. If good, others pile in
4. Rapid accumulation, enters market
5. Market adapts quickly

### Persistence vs. Adaptation

**Tension:**
- Hold weak position hoping for comeback?
- Cut losses and find better opportunity?

No right answer—depends on conviction and information.

## Why Evolution Matters

### Better Consensus Quality

Dynamic markets produce higher-quality consensus because:
- Initial framings can be corrected
- New information incorporated
- Bad options filtered out
- Good options emerge organically

### Longer Useful Life

Static markets become stale. Dynamic markets stay relevant longer:
- Adapt to changing context
- Incorporate new developments
- Remain useful as situation evolves

### More Engaging

Participants stay engaged because:
- Market composition changes
- New opportunities arise
- Strategic depth increases
- Learning continues

### True Discovery

The process itself generates knowledge:
- What perspectives matter most?
- How do framings shape outcomes?
- Where does consensus naturally form?

These insights emerge through evolutionary process, not initial planning.

## Governance Parameters

The replacement threshold (currently 5%) can be adjusted by governance:

**Lower threshold (0-5%):**
- Faster evolution
- More replacements
- Higher churn
- More dynamic

**Higher threshold (5-20%):**
- Slower evolution
- Fewer replacements
- More stability
- Less churn

Different debates might warrant different settings. Critical decisions might use higher thresholds for stability. Exploratory debates might use lower for rapid adaptation.
