# Opinions and Prices

At the heart of every debate are **opinions** with **prices**. Understanding what these represent is key to participating effectively.

## What is an Opinion?

An opinion is a perspective or position in a debate. It's not right or wrong—it's a viewpoint that has supporters.

**Examples:**

In a debate about "Best approach to solve problem X":
- Opinion A: "Use existing framework Y"
- Opinion B: "Build custom solution"
- Opinion C: "Hybrid approach combining both"

Each opinion represents a distinct perspective that people can support economically.

## What Do Prices Represent?

Prices are **consensus signals**—they show collective belief weighted by capital commitment.

**A price of 45% doesn't mean:**
- 45% probability of being "correct"
- 45% of people agree

**A price of 45% means:**
- Given the current distribution of capital
- And the mathematical pricing formula (L² norm)
- This opinion's relative support leads to this price
- **Most usefully: This is what the market believes right now**

### Price Normalization

Prices don't sum to 100%. Instead, they satisfy: √(Σp²) = 1

**Example prices:**
- Opinion A: 52%
- Opinion B: 48%
- Opinion C: 42%
- Opinion D: 38%

Sum = 180% (not 100%)
But: √(52² + 48² + 42² + 38²) = 100% ✓

This L² normalization is a mathematical property of the system. What matters for you: **higher price = more support**.

## How Prices Change

Prices move based on trading:

### Buying Increases Price

When you buy shares in Opinion A:
1. Your money goes into the market vault
2. Opinion A's quantity increases (using L² formula)
3. Opinion A's price rises
4. All other prices decrease slightly

**Example:**
- Before: A: 45%, B: 43%, C: 40%
- You buy $50 of A
- After: A: 48%, B: 42%, C: 39%

Your buy pushed A up, compressed others down.

### Selling Decreases Price

When you sell shares of Opinion A:
1. You receive money from the vault (minus 1% fee if enabled)
2. Opinion A's quantity decreases
3. Opinion A's price falls
4. All other prices increase slightly

### Prices Compete

Opinions compete for "price space". The prices are coupled—when one rises, others must fall. This creates substitution effects.

Think of it like filling a fixed container. More liquid in one section means less in others.

## Price Sensitivity and Liquidity

Here's a crucial property: **As liquidity grows, prices become more stable.**

**Early market (low liquidity):**
- $100 trade might move price 5-10%
- High volatility
- Individual actions matter a lot

**Mature market (high liquidity):**
- $100 trade might move price 0.5-1%
- Low volatility
- Requires large capital to shift consensus

**Why this matters:** It's what makes oracle-free settlement possible. As markets mature, prices naturally stabilize around equilibrium values. When they stop moving, consensus has formed.

Mathematically: The Hessian (price sensitivity measure) decreases as 1/liquidity³. This cubic relationship means stability increases rapidly as capital accumulates.

## Reading the Market

### Absolute Price

**High price (50%+):** Strong consensus support
**Medium price (30-50%):** Significant but not dominant support
**Low price (10-30%):** Minority view, but present
**Very low (below 10%):** Minimal support, vulnerable to replacement

### Price Changes

**Rapid increase:** New information or growing conviction
**Rapid decrease:** Waning support or competing opinions strengthening
**Stable prices:** Market equilibrium, consensus forming

### Volume

High trading volume means:
- Active debate
- Information flowing
- Opinions shifting
- Not yet settled

Low trading volume means:
- Positions established
- Less information flow
- Approaching equilibrium

## What Prices Tell You

Prices aggregate:
- **Capital allocation**: How much money backs each view
- **Conviction**: People stake real funds, not just votes
- **Current belief**: This is what the market thinks *now*
- **Relative support**: How opinions compare to each other

Prices don't tell you:
- Which opinion is "correct" (debates don't have correct answers)
- What will happen in the external world
- What everyone thinks (just those with capital at stake)

<Callout title="Key Insight" type="info">
Prices are consensus signals, not truth predictions. They show what participants with skin in the game currently believe, weighted by their conviction.
</Callout>

## Your Position Value

When you hold shares in an opinion, your position value = shares × current price.

**Example:**
- You hold 100 shares in Opinion A
- Current price: 0.45
- Your position value: 100 × 0.45 = $45

This value changes as prices change:
- Price rises → your position value increases
- Price falls → your position value decreases

**At settlement:**
- Prices freeze
- Your payout = shares × final price

## Price Impact

When you trade, you impact the price. Larger trades = larger impact.

**The system shows you:**
- Current price: 45%
- Your trade size: $100
- Estimated new price: 48%
- Shares you'll receive: ~208

You can see the impact before confirming.

**Slippage**: The difference between current price and execution price is your price impact. Larger trades relative to liquidity = more slippage.

## Multiple Opinions

Debates can have up to 10 active opinions (hard cap).

**Why 10?**
- Cognitive clarity—users can evaluate 10 options
- Mathematical stability—L² norm prices stay well-behaved
- UX simplicity—interface remains usable

With 10 opinions, prices spread across the range. Some high (30-50%), some medium (15-30%), some low (5-15%).

**The market automatically enforces:** Weakest opinions are vulnerable to replacement by candidates with more support.

## Practical Examples

### Example 1: Clear Leader

**Debate: "Best deployment platform for startups"**
- Vercel: 58%
- Netlify: 38%
- Cloudflare Pages: 32%
- AWS Amplify: 18%

**Reading**: Strong consensus forming around Vercel. Netlify viable alternative. Others have minority support.

### Example 2: Contested Debate

**Debate: "DAO governance model"**
- Token voting: 42%
- Quadratic voting: 40%
- Conviction voting: 39%
- Delegate system: 37%

**Reading**: No clear winner. Multiple viable approaches. Market hasn't reached consensus yet.

### Example 3: Evolution

**Week 1:**
- Old framework: 55%
- New framework: 45%

**Week 3 (after new framework improves):**
- Old framework: 42%
- New framework: 58%

**Reading**: Consensus shifted as information emerged.
