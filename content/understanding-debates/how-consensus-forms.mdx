# How Consensus Forms

Every debate starts chaotic. Multiple opinions competing. No clear winner. Prices moving as people discover it and stake their positions.

Then something beautiful happens: **consensus emerges naturally.**

Understanding this process helps you participate effectively. Not to game it - you can't. But to recognize where debates are in their lifecycle and what that means.

## The Natural Arc of Debates

Debates follow a predictable pattern. Not because they're forced to, but because this is how communities naturally reach agreement.

### **Phase 1: Discovery (Days 1-7)**

**What's happening:**
- Debate just launched or newly discovered
- People finding it, reading opinions, forming initial views
- Prices volatile as early stakes move things dramatically
- No clear consensus yet - everything feels uncertain

**What it means for you:**
- If you have strong conviction, this is when backing opinions is cheapest
- Expect chaos - prices will swing wildly
- Domain knowledge matters most here (you understand the topic better than prices reflect)

**The feeling:** Like a party just starting. People arriving, conversations beginning, no clear vibe yet.

### **Phase 2: Debate (Days 7-21)**

**What's happening:**
- Arguments being made in comments
- New information arriving
- Opinions gaining or losing support based on merit
- Some perspectives pulling ahead, others falling behind
- Candidate opinions entering, weak ones exiting

**What it means for you:**
- The debate is actually happening - participate in it
- Your job is reading where sentiment is heading, not where it is now
- Good time to propose new opinions if you see gaps
- Still decent prices for backing opinions before consensus locks in

**The feeling:** Like a dinner party in full swing. Passionate discussions. Some arguments winning, some losing. Energy is high.

### **Phase 3: Crystallization (Days 21-40)**

**What's happening:**
- Patterns becoming clear
- One or two opinions pulling significantly ahead
- Volatility decreasing as consensus forms
- Trading slowing as positions settle
- People mostly holding, not changing minds much

**What it means for you:**
- Consensus is forming - you can see where it's landing
- Less opportunity for huge gains, but more certainty
- Time to evaluate: does your conviction match where consensus formed?
- If not, exit. If yes, hold for settlement

**The feeling:** Like the dinner party winding down. Main arguments have been made. People found their positions. Calm settling in.

### **Phase 4: Settlement (Day 40+, varies)**

**What's happening:**
- Prices stabilized
- Very little movement
- System detecting sustained stability
- Market approaching settlement

**What it means for you:**
- The debate has concluded naturally
- Consensus reached
- Soon payouts will happen based on final prices
- Too late to enter profitably, but you can see the outcome

**The feeling:** Like everyone leaving the party. Conversations concluded. Consensus reached. Natural ending.

## What Drives Opinion Prices?

Not just "supply and demand" - that's too abstract. Here's what actually moves prices:

**Community Support** - When more people believe in an opinion, they back it. More backing = higher price. This is the core driver.

**New Information** - Someone posts compelling arguments. A related project ships something impressive. New data arrives. Opinions whose case got stronger rise. Those whose case weakened fall.

**Momentum & Attention** - Opinions getting discussed draw more attention. More attention = more evaluation = more backing (if the opinion is good) or more selling (if it's weak).

**Opinion Competition** - When a new candidate enters the big league, it takes support from others. When an opinion exits, its support redistributes. The ecosystem is dynamic.

**Network Effects** - Early backers promote what they believe in. This brings more attention. If the opinion is solid, it compounds. If it's weak, scrutiny kills it.

## The Opinion Lifecycle (What It Means)

You've heard "baby league" and "big league" - here's why that matters:

### **Baby League (Candidates)**

**What it is:** Opinions gathering support outside the main debate.

**What it means:** These are perspectives trying to prove they deserve to be in the main conversation. They need believers to stake on them.

**Why it matters to you:**
- Backing baby league opinions = betting they'll make it to the big league
- If they do, you were early → better effective price
- But risk they never make it → stake sits there or you unstake at loss

**The dynamic:** Like minor league sports. Proving themselves. Some graduate to majors. Some don't.

### **Big League (Active)**

**What it is:** The top opinions actively debated and priced by the community.

**What it means:** These are the main contenders. The conversation centers on them. They have real prices reflecting real community support.

**Why it matters to you:**
- This is where most action happens
- Prices are "real" - reflect actual consensus
- Backing these is straightforward: you think the price is too low, you back it

**The dynamic:** Like major league sports. The main stage. Highest stakes. Most attention.

### **The Circulation**

**Here's the key insight:** Opinions move between these states based on support.

**Baby → Big:** Gets enough backing → enters main debate → starts trading with real price

**Big → Baby:** Loses support, gets replaced → drops to candidates → can come back if it regains support

**Why this matters:** Nothing is permanent. Weak arguments get cycled out. Strong arguments can return. The debate self-curates based on what the community values.

**Your role:** You shape which opinions circulate. Your backing determines what rises and what falls.

## Reading Where Debates Are Headed

The most valuable skill isn't predicting outcomes - it's reading social dynamics.

**Signs consensus is forming around an opinion:**
- Steady price increases over time (not spikes, steady)
- Growing discussion and arguments in its favor
- Key community members backing it
- Counterarguments getting weaker or less frequent

**Signs an opinion is losing:**
- Slow price decline or stagnation
- Arguments against it gaining traction
- Backers exiting positions
- Newer, better alternatives emerging

**Signs a debate is maturing:**
- Decreasing volatility
- Fewer big price moves
- Slower trading activity
- Comments slowing down as people reach conclusions

**Your advantage:** If you're deep in the community, you see sentiment shifts before prices reflect them. That's your edge - not finance skills, but social awareness.

## Settlement: When Debates End

Debates don't end on arbitrary timelines. They end when consensus naturally forms.

**How you know it's coming:**
- Prices barely moving anymore
- Very little trading activity
- Comment discussions concluded
- Clear leader(s) emerged

**What triggers it:**
The system watches for sustained price stability. When prices stop changing for long enough, that signals consensus has been reached.

**What happens:**
- Market freezes at current prices
- Those prices become final
- Everyone's shares pay out at those final prices
- Debate becomes historical record

**Why this works:**
When genuine consensus exists, prices naturally stabilize. People stop changing positions because the community has decided. The system just detects this natural endpoint.

## Participating Effectively

**Early phases:** Back what you believe in. Your conviction versus the chaos. High risk, high potential.

**Middle phases:** Engage in the actual debate. Comment, argue, share information. Shape consensus.

**Late phases:** Evaluate if your views align with where consensus landed. Hold if yes, exit if no.

**Throughout:** Remember you're part of forming consensus, not just betting on it. Your participation literally shapes outcomes.

<Callout title="Consensus is Participation" type="info">
You're not observing consensus formation from outside. You're IN it. Your backing moves prices. Your arguments shape opinions. Your proposals add options.

**This isn't prediction - it's participation in collective decision-making with economic stakes.**
