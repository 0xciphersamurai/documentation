# The Opinion Lifecycle

This is the **most important concept** in Trigger Market. Understanding it is essential to knowing what you own and how the system works.

## The Core Concept

Opinions don't live in just one state. They **cycle continuously** between two states:

**ACTIVE** ↔ **CANDIDATE**

Opinions never disappear. They never get deleted. They transition between these states indefinitely based on demonstrated support.

## The Two States

### ACTIVE (In the AMM)

When an opinion is active:

- ✅ Has a **quantity** in the AMM
- ✅ Has a **price** (calculated from quantity)
- ✅ People **buy/sell shares** using the trading interface
- ✅ Contributes to settlement tracking
- ✅ Limited to 10 active opinions maximum

**What you own:** Shares that represent a claim on quantity in the AMM. Your position value = shares × current price.

### CANDIDATE (In the Pool)

When an opinion is candidate:

- ✅ Has accumulated **stake** (USDC in a pool)
- ✅ No price—just total stake amount
- ✅ People **add/withdraw stake** directly
- ✅ Waiting to enter the AMM
- ✅ Unlimited number of candidates allowed

**What you own:** Shares that represent a proportional claim on the total stake pool.

## The Complete Cycle

```
Someone proposes new opinion
           ↓
    Starts as CANDIDATE
           ↓
    Accumulates stake
           ↓
Stake ≥ 105% of weakest active opinion's value
           ↓
   Anyone triggers replacement
           ↓
    Becomes ACTIVE (enters AMM)
    Weakest becomes CANDIDATE (exits AMM)
           ↓
     Trades happen, prices evolve
           ↓
    Another candidate accumulates enough stake
           ↓
   This opinion gets replaced
           ↓
    Becomes CANDIDATE again (exits AMM)
           ↓
Can accumulate stake and re-enter...
(cycle repeats indefinitely)
```

## The Replacement Mechanism

### The Core Logic: Financial Strength Comparison

Here's the simple truth about replacements:

**The weakest opinion** is the one with the **fewest shares** in the AMM.

If you were to **sell all those shares** back to the AMM, you'd receive some amount of money—call this the **liquidation value**.

If a **candidate opinion has more money** than that liquidation value (plus a 5% buffer), then the candidate is **financially stronger** than the weakest active opinion.

So we **swap them**:
1. Sell all the weak opinion's shares (reducing its AMM quantity to zero)
2. Use the candidate's money to buy shares starting from zero
3. After this swap, the **AMM vault grows**—more money came in than went out
4. The market is now financially stronger overall

The **5% buffer** prevents flip-flopping. Without it, two opinions at nearly equal strength could replace each other endlessly.

### When Can Replacement Happen?

A candidate can replace the weakest active opinion when:

**Candidate stake ≥ 1.05 × (Money you'd get from selling all the weakest opinion's shares)**

**Example:**
- Weakest active opinion: if you sold all its shares, you'd get back $100
- Candidate needs stake ≥ $105 to be eligible (5% stronger)
- Once candidate has $105+, anyone can trigger replacement
- After swap: AMM vault increases by $5 (the difference)

### Who Decides Which Opinion Gets Replaced?

**Not you—the contract automatically replaces the weakest.**

"Weakest" means the opinion with the **fewest shares** in the AMM. (Technically, the one with the lowest liquidation value—the dollar amount you'd get back from selling all its shares.)

This ensures fair competition. Opinions defend their position by maintaining support. If support wanes, they become vulnerable.

### What Happens During Replacement?

**Step 1: Weakest opinion exits**
1. System identifies weakest active opinion (fewest shares)
2. Sells all its shares, receiving liquidation value (call it $100)
3. Opinion leaves the AMM
4. Becomes a candidate with stake = $100

**Step 2: Candidate enters**
1. Candidate's stake (say $105) buys shares starting from zero
2. Opinion enters the AMM with newly purchased shares
3. Now has a price and can trade
4. AMM vault increased by $5 (candidate brought more than weak opinion took)

**Step 3: User positions automatically adjust**
- Users holding the exited opinion: shares now represent claims on $100 candidate stake
- Users holding the entering opinion: shares now represent claims on AMM quantity
- **No on-chain migration needed**—share counts stay the same, interpretation changes

## Proportional Claims: The Key Innovation

Here's the breakthrough that makes this work: **You always own a proportional share of whatever state your opinion is in.**

### When Opinion is ACTIVE

Your shares = proportional claim on AMM quantity

**Example:**
- Total shares in Opinion A: 1000
- You own: 100 shares (10%)
- Opinion A has AMM quantity: 50 units
- Your effective position: 10% of 50 = 5 units
- At price 0.40: Your value = 5 × 0.40 = $2.00

### When Opinion Moves ACTIVE → CANDIDATE

Opinion gets replaced, receives $200 liquidation value.

**What changes:**
- Your share count: Still 100 (unchanged)
- What you own: Now 10% of $200 candidate stake
- Your position value: $20.00

**Your ownership percentage stayed exactly the same (10%). What you own changed state.**

### When Opinion Moves CANDIDATE → ACTIVE

Opinion accumulates more stake ($500 total), then enters AMM.

**What changes:**
- Your share count: Still 100 (unchanged)
- What you own: Now 10% of new AMM quantity (say, 30 units)
- At price 0.50: Your value = 3 × 0.50 = $1.50

**Again, your ownership percentage stayed 10%. The opinion changed state, your proportional claim adapted.**

<Callout title="Critical Understanding" type="warn">
Your **share count never changes** when opinions swap states. What changes is what those shares represent:
- When ACTIVE: proportional claim on AMM quantity
- When CANDIDATE: proportional claim on stake pool

This is **automatic**—no migration, no claiming, no transactions needed.
</Callout>

## Real-World Example

Let's follow Alice through a complete cycle:

### Initial Position
- Alice stakes $50 on Opinion "Approach X" (candidate)
- Total stake in Approach X: $200
- Alice owns: 50/200 = 25% of shares

### Approach X Enters AMM
- Stake converts to AMM quantity: 35 units
- Alice's shares: 25% of Opinion X (unchanged)
- Alice's quantity: 25% of 35 = 8.75 units
- Current price: 0.42
- Alice's value: 8.75 × 0.42 = $3.675

**Alice can now buy/sell shares like any AMM opinion.**

### Weeks Pass, Trading Happens
- Approach X gains support
- Price rises to 0.55
- Alice's value: 8.75 × 0.55 = $4.8125
- Alice is up from $50 stake

### Approach X Gets Replaced
- New candidate "Approach Y" gets enough stake
- Approach X receives $180 liquidation value
- Becomes candidate again

**Alice's position:**
- Share count: Unchanged (she still owns 25%)
- What shares represent: Now 25% of $180 candidate stake
- Alice's value: $45.00
- Alice lost value (was worth $4.81, now $45... wait, this should be 25% of $180 = $45)

**Correction—let me recalculate:**
- Alice's value: 25% of $180 = $45.00

**Alice can now stake/unstake like any candidate.**

### Approach X Re-Enters
- More people stake on Approach X
- Total stake reaches $300
- Alice's ownership: Still 25%
- Approach X enters AMM again
- Alice's shares convert back to AMM claims

**The cycle continues...**

## Managing Replaced Opinions

### What Should You Do When Your Opinion Gets Replaced?

You have three options:

**1. Unstake Immediately**
- Convert your stake back to USDC
- 1% exit fee applies (if enabled)
- Realize your P&L
- Exit the position

**2. Hold and Wait**
- Keep your shares in the candidate
- Hope the opinion regains support and re-enters
- No deadline—hold indefinitely
- Can unstake anytime

**3. Add More Stake**
- Support the comeback
- Help push toward re-entry threshold
- Increase your position

### How to Track Replaced Opinions

Your profile shows all positions:

**Active positions:**
- Debate name
- Opinion name
- "Active" badge
- Quantity held
- Current value
- Buy/Sell buttons

**Candidate positions:**
- Debate name
- Opinion name
- "Candidate" badge (+ lifecycle history)
- Stake amount
- Progress toward re-entry ($X / $Y needed)
- Stake/Unstake buttons

## Why This Design Matters

### Opinions Never Die

Traditional prediction markets: outcomes are fixed at creation. If you picked wrong initially, you're stuck.

Trigger Market: opinions compete continuously. Today's loser might be tomorrow's winner. The market evolves.

### Efficient Replacements

Because share counts don't change, replacements are **O(1) gas cost** regardless of how many shareholders exist.

This makes the system scalable. A popular opinion with 10,000 holders can be replaced for the same gas cost as one with 10 holders.

### Continuous Ownership

You maintain economic interest through state transitions. If your opinion gets replaced but later returns, you're still participating in that journey.

No need to "rebuy" or "migrate." Your proportional claim persists.

### Fair Competition

Opinions defend their position through sustained support. Lose support → become vulnerable → get replaced.

But replacement isn't permanent. Regain support → re-enter. The system never closes the door.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Do I lose my shares when my opinion gets replaced?**

A: No. Your share count stays the same. What those shares represent changes (AMM quantity → candidate stake).

**Q: Can an opinion re-enter multiple times?**

A: Yes! Opinions can cycle ACTIVE ↔ CANDIDATE indefinitely.

**Q: What's the "Previously Active" badge?**

A: It shows an opinion was active before, got replaced, and is now a candidate again. It indicates lifecycle history.

**Q: Can I sell candidate shares?**

A: Yes, by "unstaking." You receive proportional USDC from the candidate pool (minus 1% fee if enabled).

**Q: How do I know when my opinion might get replaced?**

A: Watch its liquidation value compared to candidates. If it's the weakest and a candidate has 105%+ of its liquidation value, replacement is possible.

**Q: What happens to my shares when debate settles?**

A: Only ACTIVE opinions receive payouts. If your opinion is candidate at settlement, you can claim your proportional stake (no deadline).
