# Backing Opinions

When you back an opinion, you're doing two things:
1. **Amplifying your voice** - Making your conviction visible and weighted
2. **Earning when you're right** - Profiting when consensus forms where you predicted

This is the most direct form of participation. See what you believe in, back it, and profit if the community agrees.

## How You Earn

**Simple formula:** If your opinion settles higher than where you backed it, you profit.

**Example:**
- You back "Zustand" at price 0.35 → buy shares
- Community debates for 3 weeks
- Consensus forms, debate settles
- "Zustand" final price: 0.62
- Your payout: Shares × 0.62
- Your profit: (0.62 - 0.35) × shares = 77% return

**You were right about where consensus would form. You profit.**

## The Conviction Model

**This isn't day trading** - It's conviction-based participation over weeks or months.

**Why this matters:**
- You need to actually believe in your position
- Hold through short-term volatility
- Wait for consensus to form naturally
- Can't profit from noise trading

**The winners:** People who deeply understand topics and correctly read where communities are headed.

**Not the winners:** People trying to scalp quick moves or trade technically without domain knowledge.

## When Backing Makes Sense

### **You Have Domain Expertise**

**The setup:**
- Technical debate on a topic you know deeply
- You understand which approach is genuinely better
- Market price doesn't reflect this yet (other participants don't have your knowledge)

**Your move:**
Back heavily. Your expertise is edge. Hold through noise. Consensus will form your direction if you're right.

**Example:** You're a developer, debate is "Best React state library." You've used all of them in production. You know which one actually scales. Others don't. Your knowledge = profit when consensus catches up.

### **You're Deep in the Community**

**The setup:**
- Governance or cultural debate
- You participate actively in community discussions
- You see sentiment shifting toward a particular direction
- Prices haven't fully reflected this shift yet

**Your move:**
Back the direction you see forming. You're reading social dynamics others miss. Not technical merit, but where the community will land.

**Example:** DAO debate on funding priorities. You're active in forums, Discord, calls. You sense "infrastructure over features" gaining traction. Back it before prices fully adjust.

### **You're Passionate About a Cause**

**The setup:**
- Values or philosophical debate
- You believe deeply in a perspective
- Whether you profit or not, you want this view amplified

**Your move:**
Back it because you believe in it. Profit is secondary to making your voice count. But if consensus aligns with you, you do profit.

**Example:** Debate on AI safety approaches. You believe deeply that "governance matters most." Back it heavily. Even if you lose money, you made your position visible.

## The Early Advantage

**When you back matters:**

**Early (Days 1-10):** Cheapest prices, highest potential returns, highest uncertainty

**Middle (Days 10-30):** Decent prices, clearer picture, solid potential

**Late (Days 30+):** Expensive, low remaining upside, high certainty

**The opportunity:** Early backers get the best prices but take the most risk. Late backers get certainty but minimal returns.

**Your decision:** Match timing to conviction. High conviction? Go early. Uncertain? Wait for clarity (accept lower returns).

## Where To Back

### **Big League Opinions**

**What:** Opinions actively in the debate, with market prices

**Pros:**
- Clear price
- Liquid (can sell anytime)
- Straightforward

**When to back:**
- You think price is too low relative to eventual consensus
- Want clarity and liquidity
- Prefer main conversation

### **Baby League Candidates**

**What:** Opinions gathering stakes, not yet in main debate

**Pros:**
- Cheaper effective entry price if they graduate
- Help perspectives reach big league
- Higher upside potential

**Cons:**
- Might not graduate
- Less liquid (can unstake but not trade)
- Higher risk

**When to stake:**
- You believe opinion deserves to be in main debate
- Want better entry price vs waiting for graduation
- Willing to bet on graduation happening

## How Much To Back

**Think in conviction levels, not portfolio percentages:**

**Low conviction ($10-50):**
"Interesting but uncertain. Want exposure to see how it plays out."

**Medium conviction ($50-500):**
"I think this is right and want meaningful stake."

**High conviction ($500+):**
"This is my area of expertise. I'm confident. Backing heavily."

**The principle:** Your stake size should reflect your conviction level, not some portfolio theory.

## Building Positions Over Time

**You don't have to go all-in day one.**

**Accumulation strategy:**
- Initial position based on early conviction
- Add more as thesis confirms (price movement, comment support, expert backing)
- Add even more if prices dip but thesis remains intact (buying weakness)

**Example:**
- Day 1: Back $100
- Day 10: Thesis confirming, add $150
- Day 18: Price dipped on noise, add $200
- Day 25: Total $450 backed, average price better than going all-in day 1

## When To Add More

**Your thesis is playing out:**
- Arguments in comments supporting your view
- Price rising steadily
- New backers joining
- External events strengthening your case

**Decision:** Add more while still undervalued, but before obvious to everyone.

**Your opinion is threatened:**
- Baby league candidate challenging it for replacement
- Consider adding to defend its position (if you still believe)

## When To Exit

**Your thesis was wrong:**
- New information contradicts what you believed
- Better arguments emerged elsewhere
- You changed your mind

**Honorable exit.** No shame in being wrong. Exit and back what you now believe.

**Consensus formed elsewhere:**
- Clear a different opinion will win
- Cutting losses makes sense
- Move capital to better opportunities

**Pragmatic exit.** Reading consensus correctly sometimes means accepting you were wrong.

**External reasons:**
- Need the money
- Debate taking longer than expected
- Want to reallocate to different debates

**Practical exit.** Life happens.

## The Long View

**Short-term:**
Individual debates settle over weeks/months. Some profits, some losses.

**Medium-term:**
You're building pattern recognition. Learning which types of debates you read well. Developing your edge.

**Long-term:**
You become known as someone who correctly reads consensus. Your backing signals to others. Reputation compounds.

**The goal isn't maximizing every trade** - It's building genuine skill at reading where communities are headed.

## Earning Potential

**Small, consistent approach:**
- Back 5-10 debates at $50-100 each
- Win rate 60%
- Average winner +80%, average loser -100%
- Net positive over time

**Focused expertise approach:**
- Back few debates in your domain
- Very high conviction
- $500-1000 each
- Higher win rate because expertise
- Fewer trades but better quality

**Portfolio approach:**
- Back many debates across topics
- Smaller amounts each
- Diversified risk
- Some big winners cover losers

**Your style depends on:**
- How much capital you have
- How many topics you know deeply
- Your risk tolerance
- Your conviction formation speed

## Combining With Other Participation

**Don't just back opinions - create them too:**

**Back + Create:**
- Earn from backing (when consensus aligns with you)
- Earn from creating debates (fees on all trading)
- Double income stream

**Back + Propose:**
- Earn from backing (consensus alignment)
- Earn from proposing opinions (fees on your opinion's trading)
- Multiplied opportunities

**The strategy:**
Backing is one mode. But combining with creation and proposal multiplies your earning and impact.
