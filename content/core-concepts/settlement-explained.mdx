# Settlement Explained

Traditional prediction markets need an oracle—a trusted external source—to determine who wins. This limits them to objective events with verifiable outcomes.

Trigger Market works differently. **Markets settle when observable price stability indicates consensus has formed.** No oracle needed. The market's own dynamics determine settlement.

## The Core Idea

When participants with capital at stake reach equilibrium and stop changing their minds, consensus has formed. Sustained price stability is the signal.

Think of it like a pendulum:
- Early: Wild swings as opinions clash
- Middle: Dampening oscillations as patterns emerge
- Late: Small fluctuations around equilibrium
- Settlement: Stillness. The market has decided.

## The Four Settlement Criteria

For a debate to settle, **all four conditions must be met simultaneously:**

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  1. Minimum Age        ✓               │
│  2. Historical Stability ✓             │
│  3. Current Agreement    ✓             │
│  4. Sustained Confirmation ✓           │
│                                        │
│  All Four Met? → Settlement Triggers   │
└────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### Criterion 1: Minimum Age

**Requirement:** Debate must be at least `T_min` days old (default: 7 days, configurable per market)

**Why:** Prevents premature settlement before the community has had time to:
- Discover the debate
- Read all opinions
- Trade on their beliefs
- Allow consensus to form naturally

**Example:**
- Market created January 1
- Minimum age: 7 days
- Cannot settle before January 8
- Even if all other criteria met earlier

**Rationale:** Important debates deserve time for thoughtful participation, not rushed decisions.

### Criterion 2: Historical Stability (Time-Weighted Variance)

**Requirement:** Historical price variance < threshold (default: 0.0001, equivalent to ~1% standard deviation)

**What it measures:** Price stability over the market's entire history, weighted by **how long** prices persisted.

**Key insight:** A 10-minute price spike contributes negligible weight compared to days of stable prices.

**Formula (simplified):**
```
For each price period:
  Weight = Duration × Recency Factor

Variance = Average((Price - Mean)² × Weight)
```

**Example:**
- Price at 0.50 for 5 days → Large weight
- Price spikes to 0.65 for 30 minutes → Tiny weight
- Price returns to 0.52 for 3 days → Large weight
- Spike barely affects variance calculation

**Why this works:** Brief manipulation is automatically filtered. Only sustained price levels matter.

### Criterion 3: Current Agreement

**Requirement:** Current prices must be near their historical averages (default: within 2%)

**What it prevents:** Last-minute price manipulation can't trigger settlement.

**How it works:**
```
For each opinion price p_i:
  |current_price - historical_mean| < threshold
```

**Example:**
- Historical mean price for Opinion A: 0.55
- Current price: 0.56
- Deviation: 1.8% ✓ Within 2% threshold

- Historical mean price for Opinion B: 0.45
- Current price: 0.50
- Deviation: 11.1% ✗ Exceeds threshold → No settlement

**Sniping protection:** Even if variance is low, someone moving prices just before settlement breaks this criterion.

### Criterion 4: Sustained Confirmation

**Requirement:** Criteria 2 and 3 must hold for multiple consecutive checks (default: 5 confirmations)

**Update frequency:** Checks occur:
- Automatically during trades (if 30min have elapsed since last check)
- Manually via "Update Settlement Status" button (if 30min elapsed)
- Rate-limited to save gas

**Example timeline:**
```
Check 1 (Day 10, 2:00 PM): All criteria met → Count = 1
Check 2 (Day 10, 4:30 PM): All criteria met → Count = 2
Check 3 (Day 10, 8:15 PM): All criteria met → Count = 3
Check 4 (Day 11, 9:00 AM): All criteria met → Count = 4
Check 5 (Day 11, 12:45 PM): All criteria met → Count = 5 ✓ SETTLEMENT!
```

**If criteria broken:**
```
Check 1: Met → Count = 1
Check 2: Met → Count = 2
Check 3: NOT met (price spike) → Count = 0 (reset)
Check 4: Met → Count = 1 (restart)
...must reach 5 consecutive again
```

**Why:** Ensures stability isn't momentary. The market must remain stable across multiple independent observations.

## Time-Weighted Variance Explained

This is the key innovation enabling oracle-free settlement.

### The Problem with Simple Variance

**Simple approach:** Average all historical prices equally.

**Problem:**
- 1 second at 0.50 = same weight as 1 day at 0.50
- Easy to game: Brief manipulation affects variance significantly
- Doesn't capture actual consensus formation

### The Solution: Duration Weighting

**Better approach:** Weight prices by how long they persisted.

**Implementation:** Exponentially-Weighted Moving Average (EWMA)

**How it works:**

Each time prices are observed at time `t`:
1. Calculate time since last update: `Δt = t - t_prev`
2. Apply exponential decay to historical accumulators
3. Add new observation weighted by `Δt`

**Accumulators:**
```
W_i  = Weight accumulator (total effective observation time)
S_i  = Sum accumulator (weighted price sum)
S2_i = Sum of squares accumulator (for variance)

Update formulas:
W_i  ← α·W_i + Δt
S_i  ← α·S_i + Δt·price
S2_i ← α·S2_i + Δt·price²

where α = e^(-Δt/τ)
      τ = time constant (default: 86400s = 24 hours)
```

**Statistics:**
```
Mean: μ = S_i / W_i
Variance: σ² = S2_i / W_i - μ²
```

### Why This Resists Manipulation

**Attack scenario:** Try to prevent settlement by creating price volatility.

**Attacker's move:**
- Current stable price: 0.55 (held for 5 days)
- Attacker trades to move price to 0.70
- Holds for 1 hour
- Price returns to 0.55

**Weight contribution:**
- 5 days at 0.55: 432,000 seconds of weight
- 1 hour at 0.70: 3,600 seconds of weight
- Ratio: 0.8% contribution from attack

**Variance impact:** Negligible. The 1-hour spike is washed out by days of stability.

**Cost to attacker:**
- Gas fees for trades
- Price impact costs (buying high, selling low)
- Minimal disruption to settlement

**Result:** Economically irrational. Attack costs more than it accomplishes.

### Visual Example

```
Price History:
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Day 1-3:  0.50 (stable) ███████████████
Day 4:    0.65 (spike)  █
Day 5-7:  0.52 (stable) ███████████████

Time-weighted variance calculation:
  Days 1-3: 3 days × (0.50 - 0.51)² = tiny
  Day 4:    0.04 days × (0.65 - 0.51)² = small
  Days 5-7: 3 days × (0.52 - 0.51)² = tiny

  Overall variance: ~0.00008 ✓ Below threshold

Simple variance (equal weighting):
  Would be much higher, falsely indicating instability
```

## Settlement Process Flow

```
Market Active
    │
    ├─→ Trades occur
    │   └─→ Every 30min+ check criteria
    │       ├─→ Not all met? Continue
    │       └─→ All met? Increment confirmation
    │
    ├─→ Confirmation count < 5? Keep trading
    │
    └─→ Confirmation count = 5?
        │
        ↓
    SETTLEMENT TRIGGERED (Block N)
        │
        ↓
    Finality Period (100 blocks)
        │
        ├─→ Block N+1
        ├─→ Block N+2
        │    ...
        ├─→ Block N+99
        │
        ↓
    FINALITY REACHED (Block N+100)
        │
        ├─→ Users can claim payouts (no deadline)
        └─→ Creators can claim fees (365-day deadline)
```

## Settlement Finality

<Callout title="100-Block Finality Period" type="info">
After settlement triggers, there's a **100-block confirmation period** (~20 minutes on Base) before claims become available.

This ensures settlement is final and won't be reorganized by blockchain reorgs.
</Callout>

**Why 100 blocks?**
- Protects against blockchain reorganizations
- Ensures settlement is truly final
- Standard safety measure for high-value operations
- Gives participants confidence in finality

**Timeline:**
```
Block N:     Settlement triggered
             ↓ Market frozen
             ↓ Final prices recorded
             ↓ Trading disabled
Blocks N+1 to N+99:
             ↓ Waiting for finality
             ↓ No claims allowed yet
Block N+100: Finality reached
             ↓ Claims now enabled
             ✓ Users can claim payouts
             ✓ Creators can claim fees
```

**During finality period:**
- ✅ Settlement is recorded
- ✅ Final prices are locked
- ✅ No more trades possible
- ❌ Claims not yet available
- ❌ Cannot reverse settlement

**After finality:**
- ✅ Payouts claimable (no deadline)
- ✅ Fees claimable (365-day deadline)
- ✅ Market archived
- ✅ Historical data preserved

## Manual Settlement Checks

While checks happen automatically during trades, users can manually trigger updates:

**"Update Settlement Status" Button:**
- Available on debate pages
- Calls `updateConvergence()` function
- Rate-limited: Must wait 30 minutes since last check
- User pays gas (~$0.05-0.15 on Base)
- Returns current status

**When to use:**
- Market has low trading volume
- Want to check if settlement is near
- Help trigger settlement if criteria met
- Monitor progress toward settlement

**Reverts if:**
- Less than 30 minutes since last update
- Prevents spam and saves gas

## After Settlement

**What happens:**

1. **Market freezes** - No more trading, staking, or replacements
2. **Prices lock** - Final prices permanently recorded
3. **Positions finalize** - All shares valued at final prices
4. **Claims available** - After 100-block finality period
5. **Archive status** - Market becomes historical reference

**Payout calculation:**
```
For each opinion you hold shares in:
  Payout = Your Shares × Final Price

Example:
  Opinion A: 500 shares × 0.62 = $310
  Opinion B: 200 shares × 0.35 = $70
  Total Payout: $380 (minus 1% fee)
```

**Final prices as consensus:**

The final price vector represents the market's collective conclusion. It's not binary (one winner, others losers)—every opinion receives a proportional payout based on its final consensus level.

**Example final state:**
```
Debate: "Best database for startups?"
  PostgreSQL: 0.58 (58% consensus)
  MongoDB:    0.42 (42% consensus)
  MySQL:      0.35 (35% consensus)
  Redis:      0.28 (28% consensus)

Note: Prices satisfy √(Σp_i²) = 1, not Σp_i = 1
Verification: √(0.58² + 0.42² + 0.35² + 0.28²) ≈ 1 ✓
```

## Default Parameters

| Parameter | Default | Description |
|-----------|---------|-------------|
| `T_min` | 604800s (7 days) | Minimum market age (user-configurable) |
| `τ` (tau) | 86400s (24 hours) | EWMA time constant |
| `ε_σ` (epsilon_sigma) | 0.0001 | Variance threshold (~1% std dev) |
| `ε_μ` (epsilon_mu) | 0.02 | Deviation threshold (2%) |
| `k` | 5 | Consecutive confirmations required |
| `Δt_min` | 1800s (30 min) | Minimum update interval |
| Finality blocks | 100 | Blocks before claims enabled |

**User-configurable:**
- `T_min` can be set per debate at creation
- Other parameters are governance-controlled

## Key Properties

**✅ No oracle needed**
- Settlement based purely on observable price dynamics
- Deterministic and verifiable on-chain
- No external data sources
- No subjective judgment required

**✅ Manipulation resistant**
- Duration weighting filters brief price spikes
- Multiple criteria must be met simultaneously
- Confirmation requirement prevents false triggers
- Economically expensive to prevent settlement

**✅ Flexible timing**
- Markets settle when ready, not on fixed schedules
- Can settle quickly if strong consensus forms fast
- Can take longer for contentious debates
- Natural adaptation to market dynamics

**✅ Transparent and verifiable**
- All criteria checkable on-chain
- Anyone can verify settlement was legitimate
- Full audit trail of price history
- No hidden logic or backdoors

## Comparison with Traditional Markets

| Aspect | Traditional Markets | Trigger Market |
|--------|---------------------|----------------|
| **Resolution** | Oracle determines outcome | Price stability indicates consensus |
| **Timing** | Fixed event date | When consensus forms |
| **Subjectivity** | Requires objective event | Handles subjective debates |
| **Trust** | Trust the oracle | Trust the math |
| **Scope** | Objective questions only | Any debate |
| **Manipulation** | Oracle corruption possible | Economically protected |

## Practical Implications

**For traders:**
- Monitor settlement criteria to anticipate timing
- Understand that late trades won't affect final outcome if criteria met
- Final prices reflect consensus, not "truth"
- All opinions pay out proportionally

**For debate creators:**
- Choose appropriate `T_min` for your topic
- Longer minimums for important, complex debates
- Shorter minimums for time-sensitive questions
- Can't be changed after creation

**For the platform:**
- Settlement is deterministic and automated
- No intervention needed or possible
- Transparent and verifiable process
- Enables permissionless operation

<Callout title="Settlement is Consensus" type="warn">
Settlement doesn't determine "the right answer." It captures where the market—participants with economic stake—reached equilibrium.

The final prices are the community's collective conclusion, revealed through price discovery and sustained stability.
</Callout>
