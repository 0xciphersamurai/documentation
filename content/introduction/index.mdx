# What is Trigger Market?

Trigger Market is where arguments become real. Every debate—from "best anime of 2025" to "which DAO proposal to fund"—can have a market where people back their opinions with capital. No oracles. No gatekeepers. Just skin in the game.

Settlement emerges naturally when prices stabilize, revealing what participants with real money at stake actually believe.

## The Problem: Arguments Without Stakes

People argue online constantly:

- Reddit threads about which city has the worst drivers
- Twitter fights about programming languages
- Discord debates about game balance
- Forum wars about food preferences
- YouTube comments about literally everything

**The problem: None of it matters because nobody has skin in the game.**

You can be wrong a thousand times. You can take extreme positions for engagement. You can virtue signal without consequences. Arguments cost nothing, so they mean nothing.

Traditional prediction markets tried to fix this, but they only work for objective questions:

- "Will Bitcoin reach $100K?" (verifiable)
- "Who will win the election?" (objective outcome)
- "Will the Fed raise rates?" (observable event)

These need oracles—trusted sources that verify what happened.

**But what about debates with no objective answer?**

## The Gap: Everything People Actually Argue About

Most online arguments don't have objective answers:

**Everyday debates:**
- "Best programming language for beginners"
- "Pineapple on pizza: yes or no"
- "Which anime is overrated"
- "iOS vs Android for real"
- "Best Star Wars movie (excluding Empire)"

**Technical opinions:**
- "Which React state management is superior"
- "Tabs vs spaces (seriously)"
- "Framework X vs Framework Y"
- "Best architecture pattern for Z"

**Cultural and moral:**
- "Is eating meat ethical"
- "Remote work vs office"
- "City life vs suburban"
- "Universal basic income: good or bad"

**Governance and coordination:**
- "Which DAO proposal should we fund"
- "What direction for the community"
- "Research priorities for grants"
- "Technical standards for protocols"

These are **debates**, not predictions. There's no oracle that can tell you which anime is best or whether pineapple belongs on pizza. Traditional prediction markets can't handle them.

**Yet these are the arguments people actually care about.**

## The Breakthrough: Make The Argument A Market

**What if the argument itself becomes a market, and consensus formation *is* the resolution?**

When people with real money at stake reach equilibrium—when prices stop changing because everyone has expressed their conviction—that equilibrium *is* the answer.

Not because an authority declared it. Because the market, through economic commitment and skin in the game, revealed it.

## How Trigger Market Works

### 1. Create a Debate

Anyone can create a market for any debate with initial opinions:

**Example 1: "Best programming language for beginners in 2025?"**

Initial Opinions:
- Python
- JavaScript
- Rust
- Go

**Example 2: "Pineapple on pizza?"**

Initial Opinions:
- Acceptable
- Crime against humanity
- Depends on other toppings

**Example 3: "Which DAO proposal should we fund?"**

Initial Opinions:
- Proposal A: Marketing campaign
- Proposal B: Technical infrastructure
- Proposal C: Community events
- Proposal D: Research grants

### 2. Trade on Opinions

Participants buy and sell shares in opinions they believe in. Prices adjust based on trading:

- Buy an opinion → Its price rises, others fall
- Sell an opinion → Its price falls, others rise

**Prices reflect collective belief weighted by capital commitment.**

The person who put $1000 on Python has more influence than the person who put $10. That's the whole point—skin in the game reveals true conviction.

### 3. Market Evolves Dynamically

Unlike traditional markets where outcomes are fixed, **opinions can enter and exit**:

- Anyone can propose new opinions (like "TypeScript for beginners")
- New opinions accumulate stake in the "candidate pool"
- When a candidate has enough support, it can **replace** the weakest active opinion
- Replaced opinions don't disappear—they become candidates and can re-enter later

This creates **evolutionary markets** where better framings can emerge as the debate progresses.

### 4. Consensus Emerges Through Stability

As the debate matures:

- Arguments have been made
- Discussions happen
- Capital flows to strong positions
- Prices naturally stabilize around what people actually believe

When prices remain stable over time (meeting specific mathematical criteria), **the market has reached consensus** and settles.

### 5. Settlement Happens Automatically

No oracle needed. The system detects price stability through four criteria:

1. **Minimum age**: Market must be at least 7 days old
2. **Low variance**: Prices must be historically stable
3. **Current agreement**: Recent prices must match historical averages
4. **Multiple confirmations**: Stability must persist across multiple checks

When all four are met, the debate settles. Final prices determine payouts.

Unlike winner-take-all prediction markets, **every opinion is worth something based on final prices**. The distribution shows degrees of support, not binary outcomes.

<Callout title="The Key Insight" type="info">
Price stability = consensus reached. When participants with skin in the game stop changing their positions, that equilibrium state *is* the answer. Not imposed from above—emerged from economic commitment.
</Callout>

## What Makes It Possible

Two innovations working together:

### Mathematics: Natural Convergence

The AMM (Automated Market Maker) uses an L² norm cost function with a special property: **as liquidity grows, prices become progressively more stable**.

Mathematically, price sensitivity decreases cubically with total liquidity. In plain English: mature markets naturally resist manipulation, creating conditions where consensus can emerge.

The math also guarantees that accumulated liquidity exactly equals total settlement payouts. Every dollar in gets allocated out.

### Dynamic Opinion Competition

Opinions enter and exit based on demonstrated support. This lets debates evolve:

- Initial framings can be inadequate
- New perspectives emerge as discussion progresses
- Irrelevant options get replaced
- The market adapts naturally to better capture the real debate

## What This Enables

**Settling Internet Arguments**: Every Reddit thread, Twitter fight, and Discord debate can have a market. Put money on your take or shut up.

**Exposing False Conviction**: When you ask "Will you bet on that?", performative takes collapse. Keyboard warriors revealed.

**Cultural Consensus**: Anime rankings, food debates, music taste, sports arguments—all the things people actually argue about, now with stakes.

**Technical Debates**: Framework wars, language battles, architecture opinions—let the people with experience back their claims with capital.

**Governance That Works**: DAOs, communities, organizations—decisions backed by economic commitment instead of free voting.

**Research Prioritization**: What directions deserve funding? Let experts stake their beliefs.

**Values and Direction**: What principles should guide us? Markets reveal true preferences versus virtue signals.

## The Vision

A world where:

- **Every argument has stakes** - Make your claim, back it with capital
- **Being right is profitable** - Truth-seeking has economic incentives
- **Trolls pay real money** - Bad faith becomes expensive
- **Consensus emerges** - Not from authority, but from participants
- **Accountability is default** - You can always ask "Will you bet on that?"
- **Permissionless** - Anyone can create debates, anyone can participate
- **Uncensorable** - Fully onchain on Base, unstoppable
- **Transparent** - All logic visible and verifiable

This is infrastructure for making online discourse real. For adding the one thing that's been missing from every internet argument: **consequences**.

## Built Fully Onchain

Everything happens on Base (Ethereum L2):

- **Smart contracts** hold all funds and execute all logic
- **Settlement** determined by observable on-chain price history
- **No multisigs** controlling outcomes
- **No admin keys** that can rug users
- **Permissionless** - anyone can participate, create, propose
- **Transparent** - anyone can verify the math

The frontend (built with Next.js) is just a convenient interface. You could build your own. The blockchain is the source of truth.
