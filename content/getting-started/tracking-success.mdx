# Tracking Success

You've made trades. Taken positions. Put capital to work. Now comes the equally important part: managing those positions effectively.

**Great traders don't just make smart entries. They manage positions brilliantly.**

## Why Tracking Matters

In traditional markets, tracking is simple. Stock goes up, you profit. Stock goes down, you lose. Check price. Done.

Consensus markets are more nuanced. Prices fluctuate. Markets evolve over weeks. New opinions emerge. Settlement timing is uncertain. Your edge comes partly from managing this complexity better than others.

**Tracking isn't just accounting. It's strategy.**

Good tracking tells you:
- Which positions are working and why
- Where you have outsized exposure or risk
- When to take profits or cut losses
- What patterns predict success
- How your overall strategy performs

## Your Portfolio Dashboard

The portfolio view shows your active positions across all markets. This is your command center.

**At a glance, you see:**

Each position you hold. The market. The opinion. Your shares. Current value. Profit or loss. How long you've held it.

This overview reveals patterns. Maybe you're crushing technical debates but losing on governance questions. Maybe your early entries win but late entries lose. Maybe you hold winners too long or exit losers too early.

**Successful traders study their portfolio obsessively.** Not to stress about daily movements, but to understand their own patterns. Self-knowledge is edge.

## Reading Individual Positions

Click into any position for detailed information.

**The basics:**

**Opinion Name & Description** - What you're holding. Sometimes markets evolve and context changes. Reread descriptions. Make sure your thesis still makes sense.

**Current Price** - Where the market thinks this opinion stands right now. Compare this to when you entered. Has consensus shifted your direction or away?

**Your Shares** - How many shares you hold. Combined with price, this determines your position value.

**Entry Price** - What you paid on average. Your profit/loss is measured against this. If current price is higher, you're winning. Lower, you're losing.

**Current Value** - If you sold right now at current prices, this is what you'd receive. This fluctuates constantly. Don't obsess over every tick.

**Unrealized P&L** - Your paper profit or loss. It's "unrealized" because you haven't closed the position yet. This number can change dramatically.

## Understanding Profit & Loss

Here's what matters: you profit when you sell above where you bought, or when markets settle above your entry.

**Simple example:**

You bought 1000 shares at 40 cents each. You paid $400.

**Scenario A: Price rises to 55 cents**
- Current value: $550
- Unrealized profit: $150 (+37.5%)
- If you sell now, you lock in approximately $150 profit (minus small fees)

**Scenario B: Price falls to 28 cents**
- Current value: $280
- Unrealized loss: -$120 (-30%)
- If you sell now, you realize the loss

**Scenario C: Market settles at 62 cents**
- Payout: $620
- Realized profit: $220 (+55%)
- You held through volatility and won

The key insight: unrealized gains aren't real until you close the position or market settles. Prices can reverse. Manage accordingly.

## Time-Based Perspectives

Track positions across different time horizons.

**Daily View** - Today's movements. Mostly noise. Don't overreact to daily swings unless something fundamental changed.

**Weekly View** - Meaningful patterns emerge. Is consensus trending toward or away from your position? Are you winning or need to adjust?

**Since Entry** - Your overall performance on this position. What matters most. Are you up or down from entry?

**Market Lifetime** - How has consensus evolved since market creation? Sometimes understanding the full arc matters.

Different time frames tell different stories. A position down today might be up overall. A position up this week might be down since entry. Know which view matters for your decision.

## The Settlement Horizon

Unlike traditional markets, you need to track settlement progress.

Markets settle when sustained consensus forms. The platform tracks various stability metrics. You don't need to understand the math, but you should understand the concept:

**Early markets** (first few days) - Settlement is far away. Prices are exploratory. High volatility is normal. Risk is highest but so is opportunity.

**Maturing markets** (weeks old) - Consensus is forming. Prices are stabilizing. Settlement is approaching. Less dramatic moves expected.

**Near settlement** - Prices are stable and have been for a while. Settlement could trigger soon. Markets are pricing in expected final consensus.

**Your strategy should adapt to settlement proximity:**

Early: Wide position sizes if you have strong conviction. Maximum uncertainty means maximum opportunity.

Middle: Moderate positions. Some consensus clarity emerging but room for movement remains.

Late: Conservative positions or profit-taking. Limited upside left, settlement will come soon anyway.

## Position Sizing Rules

Here's how to think about position sizes:

| Conviction Level | Position Size | When To Use |
|------------------|---------------|-------------|
| **Strong Edge** | 5-10% of capital | You have unique information or deep domain expertise |
| **Solid Thesis** | 2-5% of capital | Good analysis, reasonable confidence, normal trade |
| **Speculative** | 0.5-2% of capital | Interesting opportunity but lower conviction |
| **Learning Trade** | &lt;0.5% of capital | Testing ideas, exploring markets, building experience |

Notice: even "strong edge" is only 10% maximum. Consensus markets can surprise. Diversification protects you.

**Red flag:** If any single position is more than 15% of your portfolio, you're overexposed. Reduce size or be prepared for significant volatility.

## When To Take Action

Tracking reveals when to do something. Here are the key decision points:

**Take Profits When:**
- You hit your predetermined profit target
- Your thesis played out (consensus formed where you expected)
- Price has run up dramatically and you want to de-risk
- Market nearing settlement and current prices meet your goals

**Cut Losses When:**
- Your thesis was clearly wrong (new information invalidated your view)
- Opinion is losing support and trend is accelerating
- You need capital for better opportunities elsewhere
- Position is causing stress disproportionate to size

**Hold When:**
- Your thesis is intact despite price fluctuations
- Short-term noise doesn't affect your long-term conviction
- Settlement value should exceed current prices
- No better opportunities exist for that capital

**Add To Position When:**
- Your thesis is being validated by events
- Prices dipped but fundamentals strengthened
- You have new information that increases conviction
- Settlement approaching and current prices are below expected final consensus

## The Tracking Routine

Develop a sustainable monitoring habit.

**Daily Check** (2-3 minutes) - Open portfolio. Scan for any dramatic moves. Read headlines about your markets. Note anything unusual. That's it. Don't trade on daily noise.

**Weekly Review** (15-30 minutes) - Deep dive each position. Review your thesis. Check if market developments support or contradict your view. Update notes. Decide if any action needed.

**Monthly Analysis** (1-2 hours) - Comprehensive portfolio review. Calculate actual returns. Analyze what's working and what isn't. Identify patterns. Adjust strategy based on learnings.

This rhythm keeps you informed without becoming obsessive. You want awareness, not anxiety.

## Tracking Metrics That Matter

Beyond simple profit/loss, track these strategic metrics:

**Win Rate** - What percentage of your closed positions were profitable? Aim for above 50%, but remember: size matters more than frequency. Three big wins can outweigh five small losses.

**Average Gain vs Average Loss** - Winners should be bigger than losers. If your average win is +40% but average loss is -35%, you need high win rate to profit overall. Better: average win +60%, average loss -20%. Now even 50% win rate is very profitable.

**Hold Time** - How long do you typically hold positions? Are you profitable in quick trades or long holds? Match your strategy to what works for your style.

**Market Type Performance** - Do you win more in technical debates? Governance questions? Cultural markets? Double down on your strengths.

**Entry Timing Success** - Do early entries work better than late entries for you? This reveals your edge.

## Learning From Closed Positions

Every settled market or exited position teaches something. Extract the lessons.

**For winners:** Why did this work? Did I spot something others missed? Was my analysis correct or did I get lucky? Can I replicate this pattern?

**For losers:** What did I miss? What signals were there that I ignored? How could I have recognized this sooner? What does this teach me about consensus formation?

Keep notes. Seriously. Write down your thesis when you enter. Review it when you exit. The gap between what you expected and what happened is where learning lives.

## The Emotional Tracking

Track your emotional state too. Sounds soft, but it's crucial.

**Are you:**
- Checking prices constantly? (Overexposed or over-leveraged)
- Feeling FOMO about markets you're not in? (Discipline problem)
- Holding losers too long? (Loss aversion bias)
- Taking profits too early? (Risk aversion bias)
- Revenge trading after losses? (Emotional, not strategic)

Great traders are ruthlessly honest with themselves. If emotions are driving decisions, reduce position sizes until you regain control.

## Tools & Systems

Build systems that make tracking effortless.

**Price Alerts** - Set notifications for significant moves. Don't watch constantly; let the market tell you when attention is needed.

**Position Notes** - Document your thesis, entry reasoning, and exit plan. Future you will thank current you.

**Performance Spreadsheet** - Track every trade. Date, market, opinion, entry price, exit price, reason, profit/loss. Patterns emerge from data.

**Decision Journal** - Write down major decisions and why you made them. Review monthly. This builds self-awareness.

## The Compound Effect

Here's why disciplined tracking compounds:

Month one: You notice you lose money on late market entries.
Month two: You stop entering markets near settlement.
Month three: Your win rate improves 10%.
Month six: You've avoided dozens of bad trades.
Year one: That one pattern recognition is worth thousands.

**Tracking turns experience into wisdom.** Without tracking, you repeat mistakes. With tracking, you systematically eliminate them.
