# Understanding Your Position

You've made your first trade. Now let's understand exactly what you own and how to manage it effectively.

## What Shows in Your Profile

Navigate to your profile (click your avatar → "Positions"). You'll see each position listed:

```
DEBATE: "Best React State Management 2025"
OPINION: Zustand
STATE: Active
POSITION: 51.2 shares
VALUE: $20.45
P&L: +$0.45 (+2.25%)
```

Let's break down each element.

## The Components

### Debate

The market this position belongs to. Click to return to debate page and see:
- Current prices for all opinions
- Recent trading activity
- Settlement progress
- Community discussion

One debate can have multiple positions if you hold shares in different opinions.

### Opinion

The specific perspective you're backing. In the debate page:
- Click the opinion name to see full description
- Review the case being made
- Check who else is holding
- See its price history

### State: Active vs. Candidate

This is critical to understand:

**ACTIVE (in the AMM):**
- Opinion is trading in the main market
- Has a price that changes with trades
- Your shares represent AMM quantity claims
- Can buy/sell using trading interface

**CANDIDATE (in the pool):**
- Opinion is not in main market currently
- Accumulating stake, waiting to enter (or was replaced)
- Your shares represent proportional stake claims
- Can stake/unstake, cannot "trade" per se

If you see "Previously Active" badge: Opinion was active, got replaced, now candidate. Can return if support builds.

### Position (Shares)

The quantity you own. This number is fixed unless you:
- Buy more (increases shares)
- Sell some (decreases shares)

**Important:** Share count doesn't change when opinion switches between active/candidate states. Only the *interpretation* changes (AMM quantity vs. candidate stake).

### Value

What your position is worth right now:

**If ACTIVE:**
Value = Shares × Current price

**If CANDIDATE:**
Value = (Your shares / Total shares) × Total candidate stake

This changes as:
- Prices move (for active opinions)
- More people stake (for candidates)
- Trading happens

Value is real-time. Refresh to see latest.

### P&L (Profit & Loss)

How much you're up or down:

P&L = Current value - Amount you paid

**Green (+):** You're profitable
**Red (-):** You're at a loss
**Percentage:** Relative to initial investment

**Example:**
- Bought for: $20.00
- Current value: $23.50
- P&L: +$3.50 (+17.5%)

## Position Types

### Long Positions (Buy and Hold)

**What it means:**
- You bought shares
- Betting price will rise (or at least not fall much)
- Profit if final settlement price > your average cost

**When this works:**
- Opinion gaining momentum
- Community consensus shifting your way
- New information supporting your view

### Active Management

**What it means:**
- Buying and selling to capture price movements
- Taking profits when prices spike
- Accumulating when prices dip

**When this works:**
- You actively monitor the debate
- Can identify temporary mispricings
- Willing to pay transaction fees

### Settlement Hold

**What it means:**
- Buy and hold until settlement
- No intermediate trading
- Payout determined by final price

**When this works:**
- Strong conviction in long-term consensus
- Don't want to time the market
- Willing to wait for resolution

## Managing Active Positions

### Monitoring

**Daily check:**
- Current price vs. your cost basis
- Has anything changed in debate?
- New opinions proposed?
- Settlement getting close?

**Weekly review:**
- Overall P&L across all positions
- Rebalance if needed
- Consider selling laggards
- Consider adding to winners

### Adding to Positions

**When to buy more:**
- Price dipped but thesis unchanged
- New positive information emerged
- Want to increase exposure
- "Averaging down" if you believe in recovery

**How:**
1. Navigate to debate page
2. Select your opinion
3. Enter additional amount
4. Confirm trade

New shares added to existing position. Your average cost basis adjusts.

### Reducing Positions

**When to sell some:**
- Price spiked, want to lock gains
- Conviction weakened
- Need capital elsewhere
- Risk management (cut losses)

**How:**
1. Go to debate page or profile
2. Click "Sell"
3. Enter share amount or USDC value you want
4. Preview shows: USDC you'll receive, new position size
5. Confirm

Remaining shares stay in your position.

### Full Exit

**When to sell all:**
- Thesis completely wrong
- Settlement imminent and price acceptable
- Need capital urgently
- Want to eliminate exposure

**How:** Same as reducing, but enter "Max" or all shares.

After exit, position disappears from active positions (moves to history).

## Managing Candidate Positions

If your opinion got replaced and is now a candidate:

### Your Options

**1. Unstake immediately:**
- Convert proportional stake to USDC
- Cut losses and move on
- 1% fee applies (if enabled)

**2. Hold and wait:**
- Opinion might regain support and re-enter
- No deadline pressure
- No costs to hold

**3. Add more stake:**
- Support comeback attempt
- Help reach re-entry threshold
- Increase potential gain if successful

### Tracking Progress

For candidate positions, watch:

**Progress bar:**
```
$85 / $105 (105% of weakest active)
Needs $20 more to be eligible for replacement
```

**Other stakers:**
- Who else is supporting?
- Is stake growing or stagnant?
- Community sentiment?

**Weakest active opinion:**
- What's its current vault value?
- Is it gaining or losing support?
- Threshold changing?

### If Opinion Re-Enters

When your candidate opinion replaces the weakest active:

1. **You receive notification**
2. **Your shares now represent AMM quantity** (automatically)
3. **Opinion has price again**
4. **Can trade normally**

No action needed—conversion happens automatically.

## Multiple Positions Strategy

You can hold positions in multiple opinions within same debate:

### Spread Strategy

**Approach:** Hold 2-3 different opinions
**Why:** Diversify within the debate, reduce single-opinion risk
**Risk:** Lower potential upside if one opinion dominates

**Example:**
- 40% of capital in Opinion A (most likely)
- 30% in Opinion B (strong alternative)
- 30% in Opinion C (dark horse)

### Conviction Strategy

**Approach:** All capital in one opinion
**Why:** Maximum upside if you're right
**Risk:** Maximum loss if you're wrong

**Example:**
- 100% in Opinion A
- High conviction, willing to accept risk

### Pairs Strategy

**Approach:** Long one opinion implicitly "short" another (by not holding it)
**Why:** Express relative belief
**Risk:** Both could move against you

**Example:**
- Buy Opinion A, don't hold B
- Betting A outperforms B
- Useful when comparing close alternatives

## Position Sizing

How much to allocate to each position?

### General Guidelines

**High conviction, high knowledge:**
- 20-40% of trading capital
- You've researched deeply
- Strong thesis

**Medium conviction:**
- 10-20% of capital
- Reasonable thesis, some uncertainty
- Want exposure but not max

**Experimental/Learning:**
- 5-10% of capital
- Testing hypothesis
- Learning experience

**Never allocate:**
- Money you can't afford to lose
- More than you're comfortable with
- Borrowed funds

### Risk Management

**Diversify across debates:**
- Don't put all capital in one debate
- Spread across different topics
- Reduces correlation risk

**Consider time horizons:**
- Near-settlement debates: lower risk, lower reward
- Early debates: higher volatility, more opportunity

**Track total exposure:**
- Sum of all position values
- Should be ≤ your comfortable risk level

## Understanding P&L

### Unrealized vs. Realized

**Unrealized P&L** (paper gains/losses):
- Shown in profile while position open
- Changes as prices move
- Not final until you sell or settle

**Realized P&L** (actual gains/losses):
- Locked in when you sell
- Or when debate settles and you claim
- Actual USDC in/out

### Accounting

The system tracks for you:
- Total amount spent acquiring position (accumulated vault)
- Current position value
- P&L = Value - Accumulated vault

**Example:**
- First buy: $20 → 50 shares
- Second buy: $15 → 35 shares
- Total shares: 85
- Accumulated vault: $35
- Current value (at price 0.45): 85 × 0.45 = $38.25
- P&L: $38.25 - $35 = +$3.25 (+9.3%)

### After Replacement

If opinion exits to candidate:
- Accumulated vault stays the same (what you spent)
- Current value is now stake claim
- P&L adjusts based on stake value vs. what you paid

**Example:**
- You spent $35 (accumulated vault)
- Opinion exits with $30 liquidation value
- Your 25% of shares → $7.50 stake value
- P&L: $7.50 - $35 = -$27.50 (loss)

This is why replacement can hurt if you bought high.

## Best Practices

### Track Your Reasoning

For each position, remember:
- Why did you take it?
- What would change your mind?
- What's your price target?
- When will you reassess?

### Set Rules

Before entering positions:
- "I'll sell if price reaches ___"
- "I'll sell if opinion loses support and becomes candidate"
- "I'll hold until settlement no matter what"

### Review Regularly

Weekly position review:
- What's working? Why?
- What's not? Why?
- What did you learn?
- Adjust strategy

### Don't Panic

Price movements are normal. Short-term volatility doesn't mean your thesis is wrong. Give positions time to develop unless fundamental thesis changes.
