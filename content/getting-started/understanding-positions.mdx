# Understanding Positions

Positions in Trigger Market work differently from traditional prediction markets. This guide explains everything you need to know about how shares, prices, and payouts work.

## What is a Position?

A **position** is your stake in a particular opinion within a market.

**When you buy shares**:
- You acquire a number of shares of a specific opinion
- Each share represents a claim on future payout
- Your position value fluctuates with the opinion's price

**Example**:
- Market: "Best JavaScript framework?"
- Opinion: "React"
- Your position: 500 shares
- Current price: 0.62 (62%)
- Position value: 500 × $0.62 = $310

## How Shares Work

### Share Basics

Each share represents:
- One unit of belief in an opinion
- A claim to payout proportional to final settlement price
- Tradeable asset (can be bought or sold any time)

**Key difference from traditional prediction markets**:
- Traditional: Shares pay $1 if "correct", $0 if "wrong"
- Trigger Market: **All shares pay out** based on final price at settlement

### Positive vs Negative Positions

**Positive Position (Long)**:
- You own shares
- You **want** the price to rise
- Profit when price increases or settles higher than your entry
- Example: +500 shares of React

**Negative Position (Short)**:
- You owe shares (sold without owning)
- You **want** the price to fall
- Profit when price decreases or settles lower than your entry
- Example: -300 shares of Vue

### Position Value

Your position value changes constantly with prices:

**Formula**:
```
Position Value = Number of Shares × Current Price
```

**Example**:
- Position: 500 shares of React
- Current price: 0.62 (62%)
- Value: 500 × $0.62 = $310

If React price rises to 0.75 (75%):
- New value: 500 × $0.75 = $375
- Unrealized gain: $65 (+21%)

## Price Dynamics

### L² Norm Constraint

This is a fundamental invariant of the AMM:

**Example Market**:
- React: 0.60 (60%)
- Vue: 0.55 (55%)
- Svelte: 0.45 (45%)
- Angular: 0.40 (40%)
- **Verification**: √(0.60² + 0.55² + 0.45² + 0.40²) ≈ 1 ✓

**Why this matters**:
- When one opinion's price rises, others must fall to maintain L² norm
- Buying one opinion implicitly bets against all others
- Creates natural competition between perspectives through spherical geometry

### Price Bounds

Opinion prices are bounded by the L² norm:
- **Minimum**: 0 (complete rejection)
- **Maximum**: 1.0 (100%, total consensus)
- **Typical range**: 0.15 to 0.85 (15% to 85%) for active debates

**Extreme prices**:
- **0.90+**: Near-total consensus, little debate remains
- **0.10-**: Near-total rejection, opinion losing relevance

### What Drives Price Changes

Prices change due to:

1. **Trading Activity**
   - Buys push price up
   - Sells push price down
   - Larger trades have larger impact

2. **Opinion Replacement**
   - When new opinion activates, redistributes price space
   - Existing opinions may see prices adjust

3. **Market Sentiment**
   - News and information
   - Community discussions
   - External events (conferences, releases, etc.)

4. **Settlement Approach**
   - As market nears settlement, prices stabilize
   - Volatility decreases over time

## Profit and Loss Calculation

### Unrealized P&L (While Holding)

**Long Position Example**:
- Bought: 500 shares at 0.50 (cost: $250)
- Current price: 0.68 (68%)
- Current value: 500 × $0.68 = $340
- **Unrealized P&L**: $340 - $250 = **+$90** (+36%)

**Short Position Example**:
- Sold: 300 shares at 0.75 (received: $225)
- Current price: 0.58 (58%)
- Position value: 300 × $0.58 = $174 (owed)
- **Unrealized P&L**: $225 - $174 = **+$51** (+23%)

### Realized P&L (After Closing)

**Closing via Trading**:

You can close your position any time by trading in the opposite direction:
- Long position → Sell your shares
- Short position → Buy back the shares you owe

**Example (Long)**:
- Opened: Bought 500 shares at 0.50 for $250
- Closed: Sold 500 shares at 0.72 for $360
- Gross profit: $110
- Fees (2% total): -$6.10
- **Net profit**: **+$103.90** (+42%)

**Example (Short)**:
- Opened: Sold 300 shares at 0.80 for $240
- Closed: Bought 300 shares at 0.58 for $174
- Gross profit: $66
- Fees (2% total): -$4.14
- **Net profit**: **+$61.86** (+26%)

### Settlement Payout

**How settlement works**:
1. Market meets all four settlement criteria
2. Final prices are locked
3. Each opinion's final price = payout per share

**Long Position Payout**:
- You hold: 500 shares of React
- Settlement price: 0.68 (68%)
- **Payout**: 500 × $0.68 = **$340**

**Short Position Payout**:
- You owe: 300 shares of Vue
- Settlement price: 0.52 (52%)
- **You pay**: 300 × $0.52 = **$156**
- Original sale: $240 (when you sold at 0.80)
- **Net profit**: $240 - $156 = **$84**

<Callout title="Settlement is Final" type="warn">
Once a market settles, prices are locked forever. You cannot trade anymore - only claim your payout.

Plan your exit strategy before settlement if you want to actively manage your position.
</Callout>

## Multiple Positions

You can hold positions in multiple opinions within the same market.

### Example Portfolio

**Market**: "Best state management for React?"

**Your positions**:
- Zustand: +400 shares at avg 0.50 (currently 0.68)
- Redux: -200 shares at avg 0.60 (currently 0.45)
- Jotai: +300 shares at avg 0.35 (currently 0.42)

**Analysis**:
- Zustand: +$72 unrealized gain
- Redux: +$30 unrealized gain (short profit)
- Jotai: +$21 unrealized gain
- **Total**: +$123 unrealized gain

**Settlement scenario** (hypothetical):
- Zustand settles at 0.75 → +400 shares × $0.75 = $300
- Redux settles at 0.42 → -200 shares × $0.42 = -$84
- Jotai settles at 0.48 → +300 shares × $0.48 = $144
- **Total payout**: $360

Minus initial costs and fees = Net profit

### Why Hold Multiple Positions?

**Portfolio strategies**:
1. **Hedging**: Long on favorite, short on overvalued alternatives
2. **Pairs trading**: Long undervalued, short overvalued
3. **Diversification**: Spread risk across multiple opinions
4. **Arbitrage**: Exploit temporary mispricing

## Position Risks

### Risk 1: Price Volatility

Prices can be volatile, especially in:
- New markets (first few days)
- Low-liquidity markets
- Controversial debates
- After major news

**Mitigation**:
- Start with smaller positions
- Use limit orders (when available)
- Don't over-leverage
- Have conviction in your thesis

### Risk 2: Settlement Uncertainty

You can't predict exactly:
- When settlement will occur
- What final prices will be
- How consensus will evolve

**Mitigation**:
- Analyze settlement criteria progress
- Understand time-weighted consensus mechanics
- Consider multiple settlement scenarios
- Exit before settlement if uncertain

### Risk 3: Liquidity Risk

Larger positions may be hard to exit without significant price impact.

**Signs of low liquidity**:
- Small total market volume
- Wide bid-ask spread
- High price impact on trades

**Mitigation**:
- Check liquidity before entering large positions
- Split large orders into smaller trades
- Consider limit orders
- Be patient with exits

### Risk 4: Short Position Risk

Short positions have unique risks:
- Losses can exceed initial margin
- Price can rise to 1.0 (100%, max loss is capped but large)
- May need to close at unfavorable price

**Mitigation**:
- Only short with strong conviction
- Monitor positions closely
- Set stop-loss mentally
- Start small

## Position Management Best Practices

### Track Your Positions

Regularly monitor:
- Current prices and P&L
- Market trading activity
- Settlement criteria progress
- Community sentiment
- Related news/events

### Have Entry and Exit Plans

Before opening a position, decide:
- **Entry**: Why are you entering? What's your thesis?
- **Target**: What price or % gain are you targeting?
- **Stop**: At what price/loss will you exit?
- **Timeline**: How long will you hold?

### Size Positions Appropriately

**Position sizing guidelines**:
- Single position: 5-15% of portfolio (max 20%)
- Total exposure: 70-90% (keep 10-30% cash)
- High-conviction plays: Can be larger
- Experimental trades: Keep small (1-5%)

### Rebalance When Needed

Consider adjusting when:
- Thesis changes (new information)
- Position has grown too large (take profits)
- Better opportunities emerge
- Approaching settlement (if you want to exit)

### Document Your Reasoning

Keep notes on:
- Why you entered
- Your price targets
- Key factors to watch
- Lessons learned

This improves your decision-making over time.

## Advanced: Position Visualization

Understanding your exposure:

### Net Exposure
Your total capital at risk across all positions.

**Example**:
- Long 500 shares at 0.60 = $300 exposed
- Short 200 shares at 0.70 = $140 exposed
- **Net exposure**: $440

### Directional Bias
Your overall market bias.

**Bullish positions** (benefit from price rises):
- More long positions than short
- Larger size in longs

**Bearish positions** (benefit from price falls):
- More short positions than long
- Larger size in shorts

**Market neutral** (balanced):
- Equal long and short exposure
- Profit from relative performance, not direction

## Common Position Questions

### Can I hold positions across multiple markets?

Yes! Your portfolio can include positions in any number of markets. Diversification across markets reduces concentration risk.

### What happens to my position when a new opinion is proposed?

Nothing immediately. Your position is in an active opinion. New opinions start as candidates and don't affect active opinion pricing until/unless they get activated.

### Can I transfer positions to another wallet?

Share positions are represented as tokens in your wallet. You can transfer them to another address, and the position transfers with them.

### Do positions expire?

No. Positions last until you close them or the market settles. There's no expiration date.

### Can I use positions as collateral?

Currently no, but this is a potential future feature. For now, positions exist only within Trigger Market.
