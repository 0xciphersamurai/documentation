# Reading the Market

This guide explains Trigger Market-specific mechanics: how prices work, what the UI tells you, and what's unique about this system. We won't tell you when to trade—that's your call.

## How Prices Work

**Core rule: Squared prices always sum to 100%**

Due to the L² norm AMM, prices are normalized so that $\sum p_i^2 = 1$ (not $\sum p_i = 1$).

Opinion vault value = $p_i \times q_i$ where $q_i$ is the quantity and $p_i = q_i / C$ is the price.

**Example:**
- Python vault: $5,000 (50% of total)
- JavaScript vault: $3,000 (30% of total)
- Rust vault: $2,000 (20% of total)
- Total liquidity: $10,000

**Prices (not vault proportions):**
- Python: 70.71% = √0.5
- JavaScript: 54.77% = √0.3
- Rust: 44.72% = √0.2

When you buy Python for $1,000:
- Python vault becomes $6,454.55
- Total liquidity becomes $11,000
- Python price: 76.6% (up from 70.71%)
- JavaScript price: 49.79% (down from 54.77% - vault unchanged, but now smaller slice)
- Rust price: 40.66% (down from 44.72% - vault unchanged, but now smaller slice)

**Prices are relative.** One opinion rising means all others fall proportionally.

## Opinion States

### Active Opinions

**What they are:**
- Currently tradeable
- Have vaults you can enter/exit
- Shown in main market interface
- Maximum 10 active opinions per debate

**What you see:**
- Current price (0-100%)
- Vault value (total USDC committed)
- Your position (if you have shares)

### Candidate Opinions

**What they are:**
- Proposed but not yet active
- Accumulating support to enter market
- Need 105% of weakest active opinion's vault to trigger replacement

**What you see:**
- Candidate vault value
- Percentage toward replacement threshold
- Which active opinion they'd replace (the weakest)

**Why it matters:**
If you hold shares in the weakest active opinion, it could get replaced. Your shares become candidate shares (still valid, but the opinion is no longer active).

## Settlement Progress

The UI shows four criteria that must all be met:

```
✅ Age: 15 days (minimum: 7)
✅ Low Variance: Price stability over time
✅ Current Agreement: Recent prices match historical
⏳ Confirmations: 3/5 needed
```

**What each means:**

**Age:** Debate must be at least 7 days old. Simple countdown.

**Low Variance (EWMA):** Historical price volatility below threshold. Measures if prices have been stable over the debate's lifetime.

**Current Agreement:** Recent prices closely match historical averages. Ensures current state reflects established consensus, not temporary spike.

**Multiple Confirmations:** All criteria must stay true across multiple checks. Prevents false settlement from brief stability.

**When all four are met:** Debate settles automatically at those prices.

<Callout title="Settlement is automatic" type="info">
No voting, no oracle, no admin decision. When on-chain price history meets the mathematical criteria, settlement executes.
</Callout>

## Replacement Mechanics

**The threshold: 105%**

A candidate can replace the weakest active opinion when:

```
Candidate vault value ≥ (Weakest active vault × 1.05)
```

**Example:**
- Weakest active opinion: $1,000 vault
- Candidate needs: $1,050 to trigger replacement

**The 5% buffer** prevents constant back-and-forth. Meaningful gap required.

**What happens on replacement:**
1. Candidate becomes active (its vault enters active market)
2. Weakest active becomes candidate (its vault enters candidate pool)
3. All share counts unchanged
4. Position values adjust to new vault configuration

**If you hold the replaced opinion:** Your shares are still valid. The opinion can re-enter if it gains support again. You can exit anytime to claim current candidate vault value.

## Liquidity and Slippage

**Liquidity = Total vault value across all active opinions**

Higher liquidity = less price impact from same-sized trade

**Example trade: $1,000 purchase**
- In $10k liquidity debate: ~10% of liquidity, significant slippage
- In $1M liquidity debate: 0.1% of liquidity, minimal slippage

**Slippage scales nonlinearly** due to how the AMM works—higher liquidity means less price impact.

You can see expected slippage when previewing trades in the UI.

## Mechanics-Specific Insights

### Price Distribution Tells You Nothing About "Correctness"

Prices reflect collective capital-weighted belief. That's it.

High price = lots of capital backing it. Doesn't mean it's "right" in any external sense. The consensus *is* what matters in these markets.

### Settlement Prices Lock Final Value

Your shares' value at settlement = their proportion of the vault at those frozen prices.

If Python settles at 50%, holding 10% of Python shares means you claim 10% of Python's final vault value.

### Replaced Opinions Don't Disappear

Their vaults move to candidate pool. Holders can exit or wait for re-entry. Nothing forces you to sell.

### You Can Exit Anytime Before Settlement

No lock-ups. Want out? Exit at current prices (minus fees). You're never forced to hold until settlement.
